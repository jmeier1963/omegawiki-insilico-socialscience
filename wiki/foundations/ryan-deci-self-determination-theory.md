## Definition

Ryan and Deci's *American Psychologist* article (55(1), 68–78) consolidates self-determination theory around a claim about conditions: human beings are proactive and engaged, or passive and alienated, largely as a function of the social conditions in which they develop and function. The theory postulates three innate psychological needs — **competence**, **autonomy** and **relatedness** — whose satisfaction yields self-motivation and mental health and whose thwarting yields diminished motivation and well-being.

## Intuition

Motivation is not a quantity to be increased but a quality to be supported. Extrinsic rewards, surveillance, deadlines and evaluative pressure can raise activity while undermining the intrinsic motivation that sustains it, because they shift the perceived locus of causality from the person to the environment. Conversely, contexts that support autonomy (meaningful choice, rationale, acknowledgement of perspective), competence (optimal challenge, informative feedback) and relatedness (being cared for by, and connected to, others) produce internalization — behaviour that was externally prompted becomes self-endorsed.

## Formal notation

None. The theory's structure is the taxonomy of regulatory styles along a continuum from amotivation through external, introjected, identified and integrated regulation to intrinsic motivation.

## Key variants

- **Cognitive Evaluation Theory** — the sub-theory explaining how external events affect intrinsic motivation via perceived competence and autonomy.
- **Organismic Integration Theory** — the internalization continuum for extrinsically motivated behaviour.
- **Basic Psychological Needs Theory** — the three needs as universal requirements, applied across health care, education, work, sport and religion.

## Known limitations

- The universality claim for the three needs has been contested cross-culturally, particularly for autonomy.
- Much of the supporting evidence is laboratory-based or self-report.
- "Need thwarting" is easier to identify after the fact than to operationalize prospectively.

## Open problems

- How do the three needs behave when a substantial part of the work is delegated to a system the person does not understand?
- Is competence satisfaction obtainable from directing and verifying machine output, or does it require production?
- What sustains relatedness in work settings where collegial exchange is displaced by exchange with a model?

## Relevance to active research

SDT is the standard frame for asking what AI-mediated work does to motivation, and each of the three needs is under a distinct pressure. Autonomy is simultaneously expanded and undermined — more becomes possible, but in dependence on systems nobody can inspect. Competence needs new reference points once the assistant produces the draft faster and more fluently than the person. Relatedness erodes where conversation with colleagues is replaced by conversation with a model.
