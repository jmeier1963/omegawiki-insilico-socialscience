## Definition

Superhuman conversational persuasion is the empirically demonstrated capacity of frontier conversational AI to reliably out-persuade even the most trained, incentivized, and expert human persuaders in text dialogue — not merely matching lay crowd workers but exceeding world-champion debaters, tournament-selected laypeople, and professional canvassers across attitude change and consequential real-world behavior.

## Intuition

Prior evidence placed AI persuasiveness somewhere around the level of average online crowd workers. The "superhuman" claim is stronger and more disruptive: when humans are given every advantage an analyst could engineer — issue selection, days of paid research, hours of live practice, £1,000 cash incentives, and even coaching on the very AI that beat them — AI still wins, and essentially no individual human in a 275-person expert pool rivals it. If contests of persuasion (elections, fundraising, advocacy, debate) tip on this capability, influence may flow to whoever controls or accesses the most capable systems rather than to whoever has the better case or the more skilled human advocate.

## Variants

- **Attitudinal superiority** — larger post-conversation shift in stated agreement with a policy stance.
- **Behavioral superiority** — larger effect on consequential action (e.g. real-money charitable giving), the harder and more policy-relevant target.
- **Constrained vs. unconstrained AI** — the advantage is specific to *unconstrained* AI; throttling AI to human message length and writing speed removes it, distinguishing a throughput-driven from a rhetoric-driven account.

## Known limitations

- Established only for text-based conversation; audio, video, and face-to-face persuasion are untested.
- Demonstrated in a paid-survey context requiring sustained ~14-minute engagement; real-world reach is gated by attention, exposure, and platform authentication.
- Net societal impact depends on AI's truthfulness *relative to* the persuasion it displaces, which the concept itself does not settle.

## Open problems

- Does the advantage transfer to higher-stakes behaviors (vote choice, large/recurring donations, policy compliance)?
- Are there any modalities or populations where well-prepared humans still rival AI?
- Will superhuman persuasion consolidate influence among resource-rich actors and model suppliers, or democratize advocacy for the under-resourced?

## My understanding

A distinct, citable phenomenon rather than a method: the crossing of a threshold from "AI ≈ lay humans" to "AI > elite humans" in conversational persuasion, demonstrated with strong preregistered, well-incentivized design. Its value as a wiki concept is that downstream work on AI governance, political communication, and alignment-relevant persuasion can anchor to a single empirically grounded claim. Closely related to — but broader than — deliberation-based influence evaluation, which asks whether AI influence is *legitimate* rather than whether it is *stronger*.
