## Definition

The **mapping problem** is the challenge of discovering *where* and *how* AI creates value
within a firm's production process. It is a friction in **discovery, not access**: two firms with
identical AI tools, training, and budgets can realize very different returns if one searches more
broadly across its production process for where the technology creates value. Solving it is a
prerequisite to the costly complementary investments (process redesign, restructuring, new skills)
needed to actually realize AI's firm-level returns.

## Intuition

Because AI's capabilities are uneven and hard to predict, the application search space is vast, and
firm activities are complementary, managers tend to search **locally** — defaulting to obvious uses
(a customer-support chatbot, an LLM to draft emails) while higher-value uses that require rethinking
how work is organized go undiscovered. Partial automation can even move a bottleneck rather than
relieve it (e.g. building products faster without rethinking what customers want just "builds the
wrong product faster"). The gains compound only when a firm rethinks its full production process and
finds where AI unlocks value in adjacent activities, not just one task.

## Three sources of difficulty

1. **Capabilities are uneven and hard to predict** — even experts systematically misjudge where AI
   will help; closely related tasks differ sharply in how well AI performs them.
2. **The search space is vast** — within any firm AI could apply to dozens of activities, and
   boundedly rational managers search in the neighborhood of what they already know.
3. **Complementarities compound the difficulty** — when activities are tightly coupled, the value
   of applying AI to one activity depends on whether adjacent activities also adjust.

## Distinction from access / skill frictions

The mapping problem is distinct from the access and skill frictions that dominate the current policy
conversation and most of the empirical technology-adoption literature. Providing firms with AI tools
and teaching them to use them may be insufficient; what matters is whether they can **discover where
in production to deploy it and how to organize the rest of the firm to benefit**.

## When to use

Use this concept when analyzing why firm-level AI returns lag AI's task-level capabilities; when
distinguishing discovery frictions from access/skill frictions in technology adoption; or when
designing interventions (frameworks, case studies, search aids) intended to broaden the set of AI
applications a firm considers.

## Key papers

- [[mapping-ai-into-production-field-experiment]] — names the concept and provides causal field-experiment
  evidence that relieving the mapping friction raises AI use cases (+44%) and firm performance (1.9x revenue).

## My understanding

A clean reframing of the AI productivity-paradox debate: the earlier bottleneck is not investing in
reorganization but *discovering* where the complementarities lie. The framing is general (it arises
whenever a technology's applications are vast, uneven, and hard to predict ex ante) but especially
acute for AI. NOTE: closely related framing appears in the sibling "AI-Native Firms" work by the
same authors — candidate for consolidation.
