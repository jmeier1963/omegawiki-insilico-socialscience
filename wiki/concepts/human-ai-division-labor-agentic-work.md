## Definition

A characterization of how humans and AI agents split work in agentic (tool-using, long-horizon) tasks: humans make most **planning** decisions (*what* to do) while the agent makes most **execution** decisions (*how* to do it). Under this split, the marginal value a human adds comes from domain understanding rather than implementation skill, so returns to expertise persist even as agents automate the execution layer.

## Intuition

Give the same coding agent to a software engineer and to a domain professional with no coding background, and both succeed at similar rates — because success is gated by understanding the problem, not by writing the code. The person supplies judgment about *what* is worth building and whether the result is right; the agent supplies the *how*. Deeper domain mastery helps mostly at the margins (faster error recovery), so a moderately proficient domain expert uses the tool almost as well as a deep expert.

## Formal notation

Informal. Operationalized via decision-share (fraction of planning vs. execution decisions made by human vs. agent), success rate conditioned on user domain expertise, and task value relative to a labor-market benchmark.

## Variants

- **Persistent returns to expertise** — domain expertise raises success and value-add and does not erode as models improve (over the observed window).
- **Modest expert–intermediate gap** — domain proficiency, not deep mastery, captures most of the benefit.
- **Mode shift over time** — as agents improve, work shifts from debugging toward end-to-end agentic use (deploy/run/analyze/write).

## Comparison

Contrasts with pure-substitution narratives ("agents replace coders") and with diminishing-returns framings ([[marginal-returns-to-intelligence]]) by locating the durable human contribution in problem understanding rather than task execution.

## When to use

Reasoning about which workers AI agents complement vs. substitute, how task value shifts, and what reskilling (domain understanding vs. coding) the labor market will reward.

## Known limitations

Evidence is from one tool's usage data over a short window; generalization to non-coding knowledge work is assumed, not demonstrated.

## Open problems

Whether the planning/execution split and the modest expertise gap hold as agents take on more planning; how wages and training respond.

## My understanding

The durable insight is the relocation of human value from "can you implement it" to "do you understand the problem." It reframes automation anxiety: agents may absorb implementation while *increasing* the premium on domain understanding.
