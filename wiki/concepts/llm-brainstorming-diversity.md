## Definition

The study of how diverse (semantically varied) a pool of ideas generated by an LLM is, and methods to increase that diversity through prompt engineering. Diversity is typically measured via cosine similarity between idea embeddings (lower = more diverse) or unique idea count in the idea space.

## Intuition

LLMs are trained to produce high-probability completions — making them reliable for average-quality ideas but prone to generating semantically clustered, redundant pools. Innovation value depends on the best idea in a pool, not the average, which requires exploring diverse regions of the idea space. Prompting strategies can steer the LLM to traverse different areas of the solution landscape.

## Formal notation

Diversity(D) = 1 - mean(cosine_similarity(e_i, e_j)) for all i≠j pairs, where e_i is the embedding of idea i in pool D.

## Variants

- **Chain-of-Thought diversity**: decompose brainstorming into sub-tasks; highest empirical diversity
- **Persona prompting**: "think like X" (e.g., Steve Jobs); effectively shifts idea distribution
- **Creativity technique prompts**: structure prompts around HBR/SCAMPER tools; mixed results

## Known limitations

- Cosine similarity doesn't capture all aspects of semantic diversity
- Domain-specific: tested on product ideas for college students; may not generalize
- Diversity and quality may trade off; CoT papers haven't fully examined quality distribution

## Open problems

- Is there a diversity-quality frontier, and where does CoT sit on it?
- Can diversity methods generalize to research hypothesis generation, policy ideation, artistic tasks?
- How do multi-agent brainstorming approaches compare to prompt-level diversity methods?
