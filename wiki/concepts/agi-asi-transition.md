## Definition

The AGI-to-ASI transition is the segment of the machine-intelligence continuum between roughly median human-level artificial general intelligence (AGI) and artificial *general* superintelligence (ASI) — a system that far surpasses the performance of large human-expert collectives across virtually all tasks. It treats AGI and ASI not as sharp thresholds but as coarse regions on a continuous scale (formally grounded in the Legg-Hutter intelligence measure), and asks which technological pathways and frictions govern movement from one region to the other.

## Intuition

Most public discourse frames AGI as a single transformative step-change. The transition view reframes it: reaching human level is unlikely to be where progress stops, because all the advantages of digital intelligence (copyability, speed, parallelism, timescale flexibility) keep compounding with compute, and AI can begin to accelerate its own R&D. The interesting question becomes *how* the continuum is traversed — smoothly over decades, in an explosive intelligence-explosion, or stalled by hard bottlenecks — rather than whether AGI alone changes everything.

## Variants

Four largely independent, plausibly parallel pathways (Genewein et al. 2026):
- **Scaling** — extrapolating compute/data/model scaling laws past AGI.
- **Algorithmic paradigm shift** — evolution of or departure from the transformer + log-loss + RL-tuning + test-time-scaling paradigm.
- **Recursive (self-) improvement** — AI accelerating AI R&D in a feedback loop (overlaps with [[software-intelligence-explosion]]).
- **Multi-agent group-agent formation** — ASI as an emergent collective property (overlaps with [[multi-agent-scaling-laws]]).

## Comparison

- Versus [[software-intelligence-explosion]]: SIE is one *mechanism* (recursive self-improvement) that could drive the transition rapidly; the AGI-to-ASI transition is the broader landscape of which SIE is one of four pathways.
- Versus [[ai-normal-technology]]: the "normal technology" view holds AI impacts will be profound but adaptable-rate, implicitly downplaying a sharp post-AGI take-off; the transition framing keeps both smooth and explosive trajectories on the table.
- Versus [[marginal-returns-to-intelligence]]: marginal-returns analysis identifies *complementary bottlenecks* once intelligence is abundant; here those bottlenecks appear as the frictions (data wall, abstraction barrier, resource demand, research-gets-harder, etc.) that gate the transition.

## Known limitations

- The four-pathway / six-friction decomposition is explicitly non-exhaustive and likely incomplete.
- Whether each friction is a hard blocker or a mere slowdown is, at the time of writing, undetermined — the framing converts these into open research questions rather than resolving them.
- Heavily dependent on the (uncertain) assumption that effective-compute growth and recursive-improvement dynamics persist.

## Open problems

- Does AI progress plateau before AGI, transition smoothly to weak ASI, or undergo an intelligence explosion?
- Which frictions (data wall, abstraction barrier, resource demand, research-gets-harder, deliberate slowdown) become hard blockers vs. surmountable?
- How can the transition be forecast quantitatively (macro-quantity tracking, coupled ensembled models, inflection-point detection)?

## Relationship to foundations

Builds on long-standing intelligence-explosion / singularity arguments (Good 1965; Solomonoff 1985; Bostrom 2014; Kurzweil) but reframes them through a measurable, paradigm-agnostic lens.

## My understanding

A useful organizing concept precisely because it refuses to commit to a single trajectory: it makes "post-AGI" a research object with named pathways and measurable frictions instead of a binary singularity debate.
