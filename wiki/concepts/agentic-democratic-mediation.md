## Definition

The use of multi-agent AI to perform the *mediation* function in representative democracy — structuring, preserving, and relaying the reasoned outputs of organised citizen deliberation into formal political processes — without replacing human judgement or simulating a unified popular will. Mediation is treated as a property of the institutional arrangement, designed to preserve disagreement, compel answerability, and remain open to contestation.

## Intuition

Classic worry: AI either replaces decision-makers or manipulates voters. Agentic democratic mediation reframes AI's role as the connective tissue between citizen deliberation and legislative consequence — making sure reasons articulated in assemblies don't vanish after the event, and forcing the system to *answer* them. It responds to the decline of parties (which used to do this mediation) rather than to platforms arriving "from outside."

## Formal notation

Informal. Specified by design guarantees: plurality preservation, answerability (every reason is processed and responded to), and traceability/contestability of the algorithmic processing.

## Variants

- **Synthetic chamber** — a standing parliamentary organ that converts deliberative outputs into a contestable "parliamentary object" (Malý & Staunæs).
- **Mediation vs. simulation** — structuring real citizen deliberation (this concept) contrasted with simulating publics/opinion via persona agents.

## Comparison

Distinct from [[deliberation-based-llm-influence-evaluation]] (which evaluates how LLMs *influence* deliberators) and from social-simulation/silicon-sampling (which *simulate* publics). Here AI is an institutional mediator of genuine human deliberation, not a participant or a stand-in.

## When to use

Reasoning about institutional designs that embed AI in democratic processes while keeping legitimacy, disagreement, and accountability intact.

## Known limitations

No implementation yet; vulnerable to capture, false neutrality, and re-privatisation of the mediation layer.

## Open problems

Technical guarantees for traceability/contestability; legitimacy criteria for AI-mediated outputs; preventing manipulation.

## Key papers

- [[synthetic-chamber-agentic-mediation-representative-democracy]] — proposes the synthetic chamber as a constitutional response to the crisis of mediation.

## My understanding

A valuable reframing: the democratic payoff of AI may be in *answerability and mediation* rather than aggregation or simulation. Whether the guarantees can be engineered, not just theorised, is the open question.
