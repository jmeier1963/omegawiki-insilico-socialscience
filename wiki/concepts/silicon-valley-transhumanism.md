## Definition

The worldview, prevalent among prominent technology magnates, that technology should be used to transcend human limits — ending aging and death, enhancing cognition (brain–computer interfaces), uploading consciousness, expanding off-world — paired with an efficiency-maximizing, "optimize humanity" ethos. As an analytic concept here, it names the *ideological layer* of AI power: the values a narrow elite brings to deciding what AI is for.

## Intuition

Critics argue AI's direction is shaped not only by markets but by a specific elite belief system: that limits are problems to be engineered away. The counter-tradition (e.g., Pope Leo XIV's "limits make us human," and "a more moral AI is not enough if that morality is determined by a few") frames the contest as over *who* gets to define the human good that AI optimizes.

## Formal notation

Not formal; a cultural/ideological category used in political-economy critique.

## Variants

- **Efficiency transcendence** — Musk-style "optimize humanity," Neuralink "superpowers," multi-planetary expansion.
- **Immortality/uploading** — consciousness uploading, life extension (Altman, others).
- **Limits humanism (counter-position)** — dignity- and limit-centred traditions (Catholic social teaching) opposing the above.

## Comparison

Sits alongside the wiki's critical political-economy concepts ([[cloud-capitalism-business-model]], gradual-disempowerment) by foregrounding *ideology* rather than business model or control dynamics. Motivates pluralistic-alignment and democratic-governance arguments (the "determined by a few" problem).

## When to use

Analysing the values and legitimacy dimension of who steers AI, and critiques of concentrated moral/economic authority in tech.

## Known limitations

A contested, polemical category that lumps heterogeneous figures together; risks caricature and is hard to operationalize.

## Open problems

How much elite ideology actually shapes deployed AI vs. competitive/market forces; what institutions could broaden value-setting.
