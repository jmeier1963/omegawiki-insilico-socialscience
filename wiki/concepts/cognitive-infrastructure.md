## Definition

Cognitive infrastructures are AI systems — search engines, recommender systems, algorithmic curation platforms, LLMs — that function as foundational, often invisible layers conditioning what is knowable, discussable, and socially actionable in digital societies. Unlike tools used consciously, cognitive infrastructures operate *pre-consciously*, filtering and pre-selecting information before it enters human awareness. The term builds on Chiriatti et al.'s (2024) concept of "System 0" — a pre-conscious layer of distributed cognition outside the individual mind.

## Intuition

Traditional infrastructures (roads, electricity grids) transport matter or energy. Cognitive infrastructures transport *meaning* — they are semantic infrastructures that process, filter, and rank information before humans see it. Like physical infrastructure, they are invisible in normal operation (you don't think about the electricity grid when you turn on a light), become visible only upon breakdown, and are learned as part of membership in digital communities. Unlike physical infrastructure, they have *agency*: they make decisions about relevance, not just delivery.

## Formal notation

Cognitive infrastructure effect: a pre-processing function f: I → I' mapping raw information space I to a human-perceivable subset I', where f is learned from behavioral data and optimized for platform objectives. The locus of relevance judgment shifts from human attention (h: I → {attend, ignore}) to the infrastructure (f), operating before h can engage.

## Variants

- **Adaptive invisibility**: the more capable the infrastructure, the more seamlessly it integrates, making its influence harder to detect or resist
- **Anticipatory personalization**: continuous behavioral learning predicts and shapes future cognitive states before explicit queries
- **Infrastructure breakdown methodology**: proposed experimental technique — withdraw AI preprocessing after a period of habituation to reveal cognitive dependency

## Comparison

Distinct from *tools*: tools respond to conscious intent; cognitive infrastructures operate before conscious intent forms. Distinct from *media*: media channels content; cognitive infrastructure pre-selects, ranks, and filters content. Extends the System 1/2 dichotomy (Kahneman) by adding System 0: an infrastructural layer outside the individual mind that shapes System 1/2 inputs.

## When to use

Apply this framing when analyzing: how AI affects scientific reasoning and discovery; whether AI-mediated search changes what research questions seem salient; how LLMs reshape what counts as "obvious" or "well-established" in a field; how democratic deliberation is altered by filtered information environments.

## Known limitations

- Primarily a conceptual framework; limited empirical validation
- Adaptive invisibility makes the concept itself difficult to study empirically
- The agency claim (cognitive infrastructures "make decisions") may be contested — they optimize functions set by human designers

## Open problems

- Empirical measurement of relevance judgment automation and its cognitive effects
- Whether infrastructure breakdown experiments can reveal counterfactual cognition without AI preprocessing
- How to govern cognitive infrastructures given their invisibility and distributed operation

## My understanding

The cognitive infrastructure frame is powerful precisely because it shifts the unit of analysis from "AI as tool" to "AI as environment." This reframing has significant implications for how we evaluate AI risks and governance needs: you don't regulate an environment the same way you regulate a tool. The System 0 concept — pre-conscious distributed cognition — is the key conceptual move, extending Kahneman's framework outward to the technological context of cognition.
