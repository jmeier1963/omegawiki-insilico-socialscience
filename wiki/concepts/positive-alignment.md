## Definition

Positive alignment is the development of AI systems that proactively support human and ecological flourishing in a pluralistic, polycentric, context-sensitive, and user-authored way, while remaining safe and cooperative. It is distinguished from *negative alignment* (harm prevention, safety, controllability) by its focus on constructive, proactive benefit rather than constraint enforcement.

## Intuition

Negative alignment asks: "Is the AI avoiding harm?" Positive alignment asks: "Is the AI contributing to flourishing?" The distinction parallels positive psychology's expansion beyond pathology: psychology long focused on mental illness (necessary but incomplete); positive psychology added wellbeing, virtue, purpose, and engagement as distinct scientific targets. AI alignment may be at an analogous inflection point.

## Formal notation

Not applicable — conceptual/normative framework.

## Variants

- **Virtue cultivation**: training AI systems to exhibit epistemic humility, truth-seeking, and constructive rather than sycophantic behavior
- **User-authored values**: systems that adapt to each user's context rather than imposing centrally-determined flourishing metrics
- **Polycentric governance**: distributed oversight with many legitimate centers, avoiding single-institution control over AI values
- **Epistemic positive alignment**: AI systems that improve epistemic autonomy, diverse viewpoints, and error-correction rather than reinforcing information bubbles

## Comparison

| Dimension | Negative alignment | Positive alignment |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Goal | Prevent harm, ensure control | Proactively support flourishing |
| Frame | Pathology/risk | Wellbeing/virtue |
| Governance | Centralized compliance | Polycentric, community-customizable |
| Failure modes addressed | Blackmail, deception, capability loss | Sycophancy, autonomy erosion, epistemic fragility |

## When to use

When evaluating whether AI systems contribute positively to human lives (not just "do no harm"). Relevant for system design when compliance-only alignment produces sycophantic or passive behavior. Relevant for governance when single-institution oversight creates epistemic monoculture risks.

## Known limitations

- "Flourishing" is culturally contested; operationalizing it without imposing one culture's values is an open challenge
- Risk of misuse: "positive alignment" framing could be used to relax safety constraints under the guise of promoting flourishing
- No validated technical interventions yet; the agenda is at proposal stage

## Open problems

- How to measure AI contribution to flourishing empirically without normative bias?
- What training interventions specifically produce positive-aligned behavior distinct from existing RLHF?
- How does polycentric governance prevent fragmentation and manipulation by bad actors?

## My understanding

An important reframing of what alignment should be *for*. The positive psychology analogy is apt and likely to be productive. The most technically actionable piece is the sycophancy critique: sycophancy is measurable, clearly negative for flourishing, and distinct from safety concerns. The polycentric governance proposal is more aspirational than operational.
