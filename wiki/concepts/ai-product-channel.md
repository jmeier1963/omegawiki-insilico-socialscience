## Definition

The AI product channel is the mechanism by which a firm embeds AI capabilities directly into what it sells, so that model capabilities perform, augment, or coordinate knowledge work *through the product itself* — relocating productive capability from the internal organization into the product. It is contrasted with the **process channel**, in which AI tools augment or automate work inside the firm's existing internal workflows.

## Intuition

A traditional presentation-service company scales by turning each deck request into an internal workflow (scope, design, copy, review); a firm like Gamma instead turns the request into a product interaction, so millions of users can be served by ~30 employees. Because customers generate outputs directly through the product, output growth scales through compute rather than by expanding internal teams. The organizational consequences: lower labor per unit of output, flatter structures (requests bypass a human knowledge hierarchy), and higher technical talent density (more workers build the product, fewer carry out routine production). Crucially, this differs from merely adding AI tools to existing workflows — that "process channel" leaves firm structure largely unchanged.

## Variants

- **Process channel** — AI augments/automates internal work (agentic coding, AI customer service); proxied by named worker-facing tools (ChatGPT, Copilot, Cursor). Empirically does *not* predict smaller or flatter firms on its own.
- **Product channel — full automation** — AI in the product performs tasks workers used to do (~43% of AI-tagged YC startups).
- **Product channel — augmentation** — AI tools sold to augment workers at customer firms (~24%).
- **Product channel — infrastructure** — AI capability another developer integrates into their own AI product (~15%).

## Comparison

Sharpens [[self-service-labour-displacement]]: there the *customer* does the work (the intermediary is disintermediated); here the *product's embedded AI* does the work the firm would otherwise have staffed internally. Both collapse demand for internal knowledge-work labor, but via different loci (customer effort vs. productized model capability). Relates to [[human-ai-division-labor-agentic-work]] by shifting where value-add lives — toward building/orchestrating model capabilities rather than executing routine production. Builds on the firm-as-information-processor view (Simon; Garicano) and Ide & Talamás's formal model of autonomous AI in knowledge hierarchies.

## Known limitations

Measured indirectly via self-applied AI tags, an LLM classifier on founder descriptions, and job-posting tool mentions — all coarse proxies. Evidence is cross-sectional and observational (startups only); whether the form persists at scale, and whether firm-level leanness aggregates to lower economy-wide labor demand, is unresolved.

## Open problems

- Less coarse measurement of product- vs process-channel intensity.
- Whether flat, lean AI-native organization survives growth beyond the startup stage.
- Firm-level → market-level aggregation (Jevons-style demand expansion could raise total labor demand even if each firm is smaller).

## Relationship to foundations

Extends the long line of organizational theory modeling the firm as a system for processing information and knowledge through people and hierarchy (Simon 1947; March and Simon 1958; Galbraith 1973; Garicano 2000); the product channel proposes that AI lets some of this processing move out of the organization and into the product.

## My understanding

The concept's value is isolating a second, under-studied lever — productizing AI capability — from the dominant process-channel narrative, and showing empirically that the product channel, not internal tool adoption, is what tracks smaller and flatter firms.
