## Definition

Multi-agent scaling laws describe how the collective intelligence of a coordinated group of AGI-level agents grows with the size of the agent population, the density of their interaction, and the available compute. The associated pathway to ASI ("group-agent formation") holds that superintelligence can emerge as a collective property of orchestrated or self-organizing multi-agent systems — "Group Agents" with representational and motivational states distinct from their constituents — even if no individual agent exceeds human level.

## Intuition

Human general intelligence aggregates into more capable superorganisms (research institutions, corporations, markets) through cognitive division of labour. By analogy, large collectives of AGI agents — coordinated centrally (high-bandwidth communication, an "AGI CEO" that can talk to every member) or via decentralized market/economy dynamics — could bypass the limits of any single architecture (limited context windows, specialized training data) and reason in parallel at vast scale. The open empirical question is the *shape* of the scaling: does group intelligence grow linearly, super-linearly, or sub-linearly with more instances, and how does this depend on the form of organisation and task class?

## Variants

- **Centralized / orchestrated collectives** — goal- or outcome-coordinated, possibly copies of one base agent, communicating at high bandwidth.
- **Decentralized market coordination** — "virtual agent economies" where local-incentive decisions aggregate into higher-order intelligence via price signals.
- **Homogeneous vs. heterogeneous collectives** — diversity-via-specialization synergies vs. parallelization-only gains.
- **Mixed human-AI collectives** — asymmetric-intelligence groups raising steering and epistemic-resilience challenges.

## Comparison

- Versus [[societies-of-thought]]: societies-of-thought concerns multi-perspective deliberation *within or among* reasoning systems as a design principle for intelligence; multi-agent scaling laws concern the *quantitative* relationship between collective size/organisation and emergent capability, as a pathway to ASI.
- Versus [[software-intelligence-explosion]]: SIE is a self-improvement feedback loop; here the gain is from coordinated *parallel* agency and division of labour rather than recursive self-modification (though specialization-driven efficiency can feed recursive improvement).
- Distinct from naive instance-scaling: running more copies of one model raises collective, not individual, capability — multi-agent scaling laws try to quantify exactly that collective gain.

## Known limitations

- Emergence in complex multi-agent dynamical systems is poorly understood; the scaling form is unknown.
- It is unclear whether division of labour helps AI as much as humans, since foundation models already specialize cheaply via prompting/fine-tuning.
- Group alignment and epistemic robustness (against hijacking, hallucination spread, "self-delusions") in large or mixed collectives are unsolved.

## Open problems

- Develop "multi-agent scaling laws": how (much) does intelligence improve with more instances, and does the law depend on organisation form or task complexity?
- For which task classes do collectives become more capable than any individual agent, and for which does multi-agent scaling fail (efficiently)?
- When does increasing agent population beat making individual models larger, per unit compute?
- How to steer A(G)I groups (explicitly, or via mechanism design for markets) and ensure epistemic resilience in asymmetric human-ASI collectives?
