## Definition

Two coupled mechanisms deplete the stock of human expertise exactly when oversight of machine output becomes most valuable.

The **Codifier's Curse** operates on the senior end: experts articulate their tacit judgment into documentation, examples and labels, which enter training corpora and reproduce that judgment at zero marginal cost. Each act of codification is individually rational and collectively self-liquidating.

The **Missing Junior Loop** operates on the entry end: novice tasks are the most automatable ones, so the rungs by which novices became experts are removed. The pipeline is cut from below while the top is being copied.

## Intuition

Expertise is produced by doing work that is, at the time, inefficient to give to a novice. Automation removes precisely that work because it is the cheapest to automate. The result is not a shortage that a labour market can price away, because the good in question takes a decade to produce and its producers are being decommissioned in the meantime.

## Variants

- **Economic** (Catalini, Hui & Wu): the human experience stock S_nm declines while the codified knowledge stock K_IP rises.
- **Automation-research** (Bainbridge's ironies of automation): automation removes the easy cases and leaves the hard ones, while removing the practice that made handling hard cases possible.
- **Educational**: deliberate practice is displaced by tool use, so competence is measured under tool availability and collapses without it.

## Comparison

The Missing Junior Loop is the supply-side mechanism behind [[verification-bandwidth]]: it explains why verification capacity shrinks over time rather than staying constant. Distinct from ordinary deskilling in that the loss is intergenerational — the current cohort keeps its judgment, the next never acquires it.

## Known limitations

- Assumes tacit expertise is genuinely captured by codification; if the residual is large, the curse is weaker than stated.
- Historical automation waves also removed entry rungs and new ones appeared; the claim that this time the replacement fails needs its own argument.
- Empirical evidence is currently strongest in clinical settings and in individual learning studies, not at the level of a professional workforce.

## Open problems

- Is the loop reversible once broken, or does it exhibit hysteresis?
- Can synthetic practice substitute for real apprenticeship in producing verification-grade judgment?
- Which forms of expertise resist codification, and why?

## My understanding

The sharp part of this concept is that both mechanisms are *voluntary and locally sensible*. Nobody decides to dismantle a profession; every individual step — write the documentation, let the tool do the tedious part — is defensible on its own. That is what makes it a governance problem rather than a coordination failure to be solved by better incentives at the individual level.
