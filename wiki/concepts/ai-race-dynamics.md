## Definition

The competitive dynamics that emerge when multiple state actors and/or corporations simultaneously pursue advanced AI capabilities, creating incentive structures that prioritize speed over safety and undermine coordination.

## Intuition

Analogous to nuclear arms race logic: each actor's decision to accelerate is individually rational given uncertainty about rivals' progress, but collectively produces outcomes worse than coordinated development. The race dynamic arises from strategic interdependence — slowing down risks falling behind, while accelerating risks destabilizing the system.

## Formal notation

Not formally modeled in the literature; typically analyzed qualitatively through game theory (Prisoner's Dilemma, stag hunt) or via simulation exercises.

## Variants

- **State-level race**: US vs. China competing for strategic AI dominance
- **Corporate race**: frontier labs (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Baidu) competing for market position and talent
- **Mixed race**: interplay between national champions, state funding, and private firms — where corporate incentives and national security imperatives partially align but frequently diverge

## Comparison

- Unlike nuclear arms races, AI capabilities are harder to observe externally, making verification of agreements more difficult
- AI race dynamics operate on shorter timescales than nuclear buildup, compressing diplomatic response time

## When to use

When analyzing: AI governance failures, competitive timelines, international AI treaty prospects, safety-capability tradeoffs under competitive pressure.

## Known limitations

- Most modeling is qualitative or from simulation exercises; empirical data on actual race dynamics is limited
- "Race" framing may be self-fulfilling — publicizing AI race narratives could accelerate competitive dynamics

## Open problems

- How can race dynamics be measured empirically (compute, publication rates, investment)?
- Under what conditions do AI races produce stable deterrence vs. catastrophic equilibria?
- Can AI safety coordination survive intense commercial competition?

## My understanding

AI race dynamics are the central structural challenge for AI governance. The empirical evidence from simulation exercises (43 games of Intelligence Rising) consistently shows destabilization, suggesting that governance interventions must target the race incentive structure itself, not just downstream safety regulations.
