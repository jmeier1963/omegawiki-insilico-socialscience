## Definition

A family of proposals to share the value AI creates through durable *ownership stakes* rather than one-off transfers or dividends: **data equity** gives the people whose work trains a model a proportional stake in it, and **public equity** gives society as a whole a collectively-held stake in frontier models — potentially administered through partial common ownership (PCO) and public trusts.

## Intuition

A transfer (UBI, a dividend) lasts only as long as it stays politically convenient; a stake makes people part-owners on a footing that is hard to pull out from under them, giving the public a reason to support and shape AI rather than resent it, and giving creators a reason to make their work available rather than wall it off. The idea traces to a 2021 Anthropic memo proposing "guaranteed basic capital for the machine age."

## Variants

- **Data equity:** an antitrust safe harbor lets scattered rights holders collectively bargain for equity (fixing the training-data monopsony); pricing via mixture weights + scaling laws; paid in equity to keep creators invested.
- **Public equity — sovereign wealth fund:** a one-time stock tax moves model-maker equity into a fund paying a public dividend (e.g. Sanders' AI Sovereign Wealth Fund Act; OpenAI's own public-wealth-fund proposal).
- **Public equity — partial common ownership (PCO):** a lab self-assesses its model's price, pays a recurring fee on it, and must cede the model to a public taker at that named price (self-assessed Harberger-style pricing; Taiwan land 1954–1977 precedent).
- **Ownership vs. operation split:** a public trust holds ultimate, unsellable control and licenses deployment to safety-approved operators on takeable terms.

## Comparison

Distinct from [[scaling-law-data-compensation]], which prices data for *payment*; public equity converts that share into *ownership and control rights*. Broader than [[sharing-ai-prosperity]]'s predistribution framing in specifying concrete vehicles (safe harbor, PCO, public trust), but narrower than UBI-style redistribution. Direct share hand-outs to individuals are explicitly rejected (1990s Russian voucher privatization → oligarchy) in favor of collective, hard-to-alienate vehicles.

## Known limitations

- PCO's "highest bidder = best use" logic misfires for frontier AI, where maximal monetization may be exactly what the public wants to restrain.
- Collective vehicles grow less trustworthy as they scale; sovereign wealth funds invite capture.
- A nationally-bounded dividend mismatches globally-produced AI.
- Data equity depends on unresolved fair-use litigation and on passing the antitrust safe harbor.

## Open problems

- What governance vehicle can hold public equity at scale without capture?
- Does public ownership co-opt citizens into tolerating harmful AI uses?
- How to reconcile national AI dividends with global data provenance.

## My understanding

The productive shift is from *compensation* to *ownership*: a stake is stickier and better-aligned than a transfer. The novel, most contestable piece is PCO applied to AI models — self-assessed pricing works for land because ability-to-pay proxies best-use, but for frontier systems the highest bidder is the one who can monetize most aggressively, which may be the opposite of what the public wants. The "state-led middle-power AI stacks" are the likeliest first venue, since the taxpayer is already the investor.
