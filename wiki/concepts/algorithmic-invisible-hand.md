## Definition

A theory of the "Agent Economy": agentic AI that never sleeps, tires, or processes sequentially, and can transact with vast numbers of counterparties at once, removes the human cognitive friction that implicitly stabilized markets since Adam Smith. The framework reconceives friction as a stabilizer (Friction Economics) and holds that pricing power migrates from information arbitrage to layered trust (the Trust Standard), forcing a repricing of human capital.

## Intuition

Classical market theory implicitly relied on the physics of human cognition — latency, fatigue, sequential decision-making — as a damping mechanism. When cognition becomes near-free and continuous, that damping vanishes: markets can move faster and more correlated than the frictional institutions built around human limits, raising volatility and collapse risk. What stays scarce is trust (that a counterparty is who/what it claims, has a reputation, and holds a relationship), so trust becomes the basis of pricing power.

## Variants

- **Friction Economics:** human cognitive latency as a systemic damping mechanism whose removal increases volatility.
- **Trust Standard:** a three-layer model — verification trust, reputation trust, relational trust — replacing information arbitrage as pricing power.
- **Agent Economy:** the overall regime of near-zero cognitive marginal cost, algorithmic intermediation, and obsolescence of friction-dependent roles.

## Known limitations

- Conceptual and metaphor-driven; no formal model or empirical test of the volatility claim.
- Single-author, monograph-derived; contributions are programmatic.
- "Friction as stabilizer" is asserted rather than demonstrated.

## Open problems

- Can Friction Economics be formalized and tested (does friction removal measurably raise volatility)?
- What mechanism-design infrastructure operationalizes the Trust Standard?
- Which human roles retain value once cognitive friction is priced out?

## My understanding

The inversion is the interesting part: friction is normally the enemy of efficiency, and this reframes it as a hidden stabilizer whose removal raises collapse risk — which, if formalizable, would be a genuine contribution to how we think about fully-automated markets. The Trust Standard is the constructive complement (value migrates to verification/reputation/relational trust) and connects to the wiki's cognitive-abundance and cloud-capitalism threads. It is theory awaiting a model; treat the framing as a hypothesis, not a result.
