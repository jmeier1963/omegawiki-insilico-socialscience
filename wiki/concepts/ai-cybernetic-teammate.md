## Definition

The empirical finding that generative AI does not merely raise individual output but substitutes for specific benefits of human teamwork: an individual working with AI can match a two-person team's performance, AI bridges functional expertise silos (pulling specialists toward balanced solutions), and AI's conversational interface partly fills the social and motivational role a human teammate provides.

## Intuition

Teams help via three channels — combined performance, cross-functional expertise sharing, and social/motivational support. If AI can supply each channel, then the unit of productive work shifts: "individual + AI" becomes a substitute for a small team, which forces organizations to rethink how they structure collaborative work.

## Variants

- **Performance substitution:** individual + AI ≈ team without AI.
- **Silo-breaking:** AI moves domain specialists toward balanced, cross-functional solutions.
- **Social/affective role:** AI's language interface elicits positive emotional responses, partly replacing teammate motivation.

## Known limitations

- Evidence from a single firm/domain (P&G product innovation), short-run.
- Silo-breaking could shade into harmful homogenization of solutions.
- The social-role effect is self-reported; long-run effects on human teams unknown.

## Open problems

- Does AI-as-teammate erode the human expertise it substitutes for over time?
- How should organizations restructure teams if individuals+AI match small teams?
- Does cross-functional balancing improve outcomes or reduce beneficial diversity?

## Realized by

- [[cybernetic-teammate-field-experiment-generative-ai]] — pre-registered P&G field experiment across the three collaboration pillars.

## My understanding

The crisp claim is substitution for a *coordination* benefit, not just effort: individual+AI matching team-without-AI says AI supplies something teams supply, which is the org-design-relevant result. The silo-breaking effect is the double-edged one — good for integration, but "everyone converges to balanced solutions" is a homogenization risk worth watching, and it rhymes with optimization-induced-uniformity concerns elsewhere in the wiki.
