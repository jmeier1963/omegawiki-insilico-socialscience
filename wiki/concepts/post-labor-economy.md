## Definition

A macroeconomic steady state, reached when AGI (modeled as capital-embedded labor) substitutes for human labor across sectors, in which the aggregate labor income share converges to zero, wages vanish, income concentrates in the owners of AGI capital, and the economy nonetheless sustains a positive, capital-driven balanced growth path independent of demographic or labor-market constraints.

## Intuition

If AGI labor is a service flow derived from AGI-specific capital and is a gross substitute for human labor (CES elasticity with `ρ_1 < 1`), then as automation intensity saturates (`α_i → 1`) human labor input is driven toward zero and so is its marginal product — i.e. wages. Output keeps rising because the production function effectively becomes linear in AGI capital, so growth decouples from human labor. The result is a structural inversion: value-added accrues almost entirely to capital, inequality rises mechanically, and the wage-based fiscal base that funds the state erodes. The regime is not a collapse — output grows — but a redistribution and governance crisis, which is what motivates automation-contingent fiscal tools (an AGI-linked VAT rebated as targeted UBI).

## Variants

- **Laissez-faire post-labor steady state** — no redistribution: vanishing wages, rising Gini, income concentrated in AGI-capital owners.
- **Redistributed post-labor regime** — an AGI-VAT-financed targeted UBI offsets labor-income losses, preserving consumption and improving intertemporal welfare while the economy stays on the same growth path.
- **Post-labor balanced growth path (BGP)** — the formal limit with `L_{i,h} = 0`, where CES output collapses to linear-in-AGI-capital and growth is `g = s·Ξ − δ`, decoupled from population.

## Comparison

Distinct from [[self-service-labour-displacement]], which is a *micro/mechanism* account of how a particular technology removes a job (the customer self-serves); the post-labor economy is the *macro/general-equilibrium endpoint* of pervasive factor substitution. It is the negative scenario that the redistribution agenda of [[sharing-ai-prosperity]] responds to: where "sharing AI prosperity" names the policy goal and its mechanisms, "post-labor economy" names the equilibrium that makes those mechanisms necessary.

## Known limitations

- The vanishing-labor-share result depends on AGI and human labor being gross substitutes and on AGI capital being effectively unconstrained by population — both modeling assumptions, not established facts.
- In the originating model, capital is entirely firm-owned, which maximizes the concentration result; broadening capital ownership would soften the inequality conclusion.
- Smooth diffusion abstracts from takeoff discontinuities, capability thresholds, and the political feasibility of the transition.

## Open problems

- Empirically, how close is any real economy to the substitution elasticities and diffusion speeds the regime requires?
- What institutional/ownership arrangements (capital-broadening, sovereign AI funds, VAT–UBI) actually stabilize welfare in this regime?
- How to value and compensate the non-pecuniary loss of work in a world where wage income is no longer the primary distribution channel?

## My understanding

A useful named endpoint for AGI-economics discussions: it crystallizes "what is the equilibrium we are worried about" into a precise object (labor share → 0, growth decoupled from labor, concentration rising) that policy mechanisms can then be measured against. The concept is more robust than any single model's magnitudes, because it follows from a fairly general substitutability-plus-saturation argument.
