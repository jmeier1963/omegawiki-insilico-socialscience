## Definition

**Firm-level AI complementarities** capture why task-level AI productivity gains do not automatically
aggregate to firm-level performance. A firm's output depends on the joint quality of many
interconnected, complementary activities; a task-level improvement can be bottlenecked by unchanged
processes elsewhere, producing limited or no gains overall. Realizing AI's returns therefore requires
not just adopting the technology but reorganizing production around it — and where complementarities
are strong, the returns to AI compound across activities and concentrate in the upper tail.

## Intuition

This is the general-purpose-technology pattern: electricity demanded new factory layouts, the internet
required new distribution channels, and AI requires reorganizing production. Without the complementary
investments, partial automation preserves bottlenecks rather than relieving them. With them — when a
firm rethinks the full chain of tasks — gains in one activity unlock value in adjacent ones, which is
why the field experiment's revenue and investment gains were largest at the 90th percentile and above
rather than uniformly distributed.

## Relationship to the productivity paradox

Task-level evidence robustly shows AI productivity gains, yet aggregating them to the firm level had
remained empirically open because complementarities and intangible investments can offset or delay
returns, and existing firm-level evidence was largely correlational. This concept frames the
resolution: the gains *do* aggregate, but conditionally on reorganization, and the prior bottleneck to
reorganization is the [[mapping-problem]] (discovering where the complementarities lie).

## When to use

Use when reasoning about whether and when AI's measured task gains will show up in firm revenue,
productivity, or growth; when interpreting upper-tail-concentrated treatment effects; or when comparing
AI to earlier general-purpose-technology adoption dynamics.

## Key papers

- [[mapping-ai-into-production-field-experiment]] — provides causal evidence that AI's task-level gains
  aggregate to firm performance and that upper-tail concentration is consistent with complementarities
  amplifying returns.

## My understanding

The complementarity story is the standard GPT mechanism, but this paper supplies the missing causal,
firm-level link and shows the gains are broad-based across firm types while concentrated in the upper
tail — a non-trivial combination that points to complementarities (not mere cost reduction) as the
driver.
