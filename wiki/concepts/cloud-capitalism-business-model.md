## Definition

Cloud capitalism is a business model that has evolved from platform capitalism: dominant tech firms shift from asset-light data intermediation to asset-heavy compute infrastructure ownership. Key features: (1) massive capital investment in cloud/compute infrastructure, (2) semi-vertical integration with enterprise partners (rather than hierarchical dominance), (3) enterprise-facing rather than consumer-facing strategy, (4) structural "choke point" power over all digital industries that depend on their infrastructure.

## Intuition

Traditional platforms (Uber, AirBnB) own no physical assets and extract rents from network effects. Cloud capitalists (AWS, Azure, GCP) do the opposite: they own enormous physical infrastructure (data centers, chips, fiber) and rent access to it. The AI race has accelerated this shift because training frontier models requires capital-intensive compute that only a few firms can afford.

## Formal notation

No formal model; characterized empirically through financial analysis (capex/revenue ratios, vertical integration metrics).

## Variants

- **Hyperscaler cloud capitalism**: AWS, Azure, GCP operating at global scale with full AI stack integration
- **National cloud capitalism**: European or state-backed cloud providers with different ownership structures
- **Techno-nationalist cloud capitalism**: cloud firms aligned with state security apparatus (NVIDIA + US government, Huawei + Chinese state)

## Comparison

| | Platform capitalism | Cloud capitalism |
|---|---|---|
| Asset intensity | Light | Heavy |
| Supply chain | Hierarchical (platform controls) | Semi-vertical (collaboration) |
| Customer | Consumer | Enterprise |
| Power source | Network effects | Infrastructure control |
| Choke point | App stores, marketplaces | Compute access |

## When to use

When analyzing: AI market structure, compute concentration, AI governance, EU digital sovereignty, techno-nationalism, Big Tech political strategy.

## Known limitations

- Concept is recent (2025); may not persist if cloud model is disrupted by efficiency breakthroughs or antitrust action
- "Choke point" framing may overstate substitutability barriers (open-source models reduce lock-in)

## Open problems

- Can smaller cloud providers or national clouds break the concentration dynamics?
- Does cloud capitalism inevitably produce techno-nationalist fragmentation?

## My understanding

The cloud capitalism framing is one of the clearest frameworks for understanding why AI development concentrates power in a small number of firms and why those firms pursue techno-nationalist alliances. Important for social science analyses of AI's political economy.
