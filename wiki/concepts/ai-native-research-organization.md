## Definition

An AI-native research organization is a scientific institution designed from scratch around human–machine collective intelligence and **recursive collective self-improvement (RSI)** — improving not only individual AI agents but the entire lab system (models, infrastructure, training data, resource allocation, collaboration norms) through compounding feedback loops, with humans remaining in the loop by "living within the experiment."

## Intuition

Bolting AI agents onto legacy high-modernist labs is like replacing a factory's steam engine with a dynamo while keeping the same vertical belt-driven layout. The Inherent Labs manifesto argues that realizing AI's scientific promise requires **process redesign** as radical as early-20th-century factory electrification: modular workflows, new human–machine teaming interfaces, and institution-level RSI inspired by cultural evolution rather than only agent-level capability gains.

## Formal notation

Not formalized in source material. Conceptually:

$$\mathcal{L}_{t+1} = \mathcal{F}(\mathcal{L}_t, \mathcal{M}_t, \mathcal{H}_t)$$

where $\mathcal{L}$ is the lab system (organization + tooling), $\mathcal{M}$ are machine capabilities, $\mathcal{H}$ are human collaboration patterns, and $\mathcal{F}$ is a recursive improvement operator applied at collective scale.

## Variants

- **Public Benefit Corporation governance** — legal structure prioritizing societal benefit over profit maximization (Inherent model).
- **Open-ended research agents** — systems optimized for hypothesis exploration rather than narrow metric maximization.
- **Autonomous lab + organizational RSI** — physical automation coupled with institutional redesign (stated goal of Inherent).

## Comparison

| Aspect | Legacy lab + AI tools | AI-native research organization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design target | Automate tasks within existing workflows | Redesign workflows around AI affordances |
| RSI scope | Individual models or agents | Whole institution (collective) |
| Human role | Optional oversight gates | Co-evolving participants ("living within the experiment") |
| Optimization | Narrow benchmarks | Open-ended discovery + legibility to humans |
| Governance | Standard corporate / academic | PBC + societal-benefit board (proposed) |

## When to use

- Framing debates about **institutional** vs. **tool-level** AI-for-science adoption.
- Contrasting autonomous end-to-end pipelines with human–machine collective intelligence visions.
- Discussing governance of recursive improvement in research settings.

## Known limitations

- Only articulated in a 2026 manifesto; no operational blueprint or empirical validation.
- Collective RSI safety properties undefined (reward hacking, alignment at institutional scale).
- Risk of productivity gains at expense of worker well-being (authors acknowledge industrial-revolution parallel).

## Open problems

- Benchmarks for institution-level RSI.
- Training curious agents without controllable reward signals.
- Interfaces for many-humans–many-agents collaboration.
- Recursive improvement of alignment and fairness in collective decision-making.
