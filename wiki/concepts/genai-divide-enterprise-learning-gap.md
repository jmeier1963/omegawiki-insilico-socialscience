## Definition

The stark bimodal split between the minority of enterprise GenAI deployments (~5% of integrated pilots) that extract measurable P&L value and the majority (~95%) that get zero return — a divide determined by *approach* rather than model quality, regulation, or talent, whose core barrier is a **learning gap**: most GenAI systems do not retain feedback, adapt to a specific process, or improve over time.

## Intuition

Generic chat tools (ChatGPT, Copilot) diffuse widely and lift individual productivity but rarely move P&L, while custom/enterprise systems stall in a funnel (evaluated → piloted → production) because they are brittle, don't fit workflows, and don't learn. Firms that cross the divide demand process-specific customization, evaluate on business outcomes not benchmarks, and buy systems that improve with use — often via external partnerships that outperform internal builds.

## Variants

- **Buyer side:** enterprises/mid-market/SMB — success from process-specific customization and outcome-based evaluation.
- **Builder side:** startups/vendors/consultancies — winners deliver learning-capable, integrated systems.
- **Shadow AI economy:** employees using personal AI tools off-book, high individual adoption without organizational P&L capture.
- **Enterprise paradox:** large firms lead in pilot volume but lag in scale-up.

## Comparison

A firm-level, ROI-framed counterpart to [[firm-level-ai-complementarities]] (value requires complementary process/organizational change) and [[ai-adoption-pressure-corporate-organizations]] (adoption pressure vs. realized value). ## Known limitations

- Evidence is a preliminary, anonymized mixed-methods report; the "95% zero return" figure is definition-sensitive.
- The disruption index is a constructed composite with subjective indicators.
- The external-partnership advantage may reflect selection, not causal capability transfer.

## Open problems

- Is the learning gap fundamental or an engineering problem (memory, feedback loops, RAG, fine-tuning)?
- Will the 95% cross the divide as tooling matures, or is the divide durable?
- How much of the shadow AI economy converts into organizational value?

## My understanding

The contrarian, useful claim is that the constraint is *learning*, not model capability: systems that don't retain feedback or adapt to a process stall no matter how good the base model. That aligns the enterprise-ROI story with the firm-complementarities literature — value comes from the wrapper (process fit, feedback loops, organizational change), not the model. The headline 95% number is rhetorically loaded and definition-dependent; the deployment funnel and the partnership advantage are the parts worth carrying forward.
