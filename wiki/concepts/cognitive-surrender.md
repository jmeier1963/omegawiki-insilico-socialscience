## Definition

Cognitive surrender is the adoption of AI-generated outputs with minimal critical scrutiny — a deeper abdication of cognitive control than cognitive offloading, where the user relinquishes the role of constructing or evaluating an answer and instead adopts the AI's judgment as their own. Proposed by Shaw & Nave (2026) as a key behavioral prediction of Tri-System Theory.

## Intuition

Cognitive offloading is strategic: you use a calculator to extend your arithmetic capacity, but you still understand what the answer means and whether it's plausible. Cognitive surrender goes further: you no longer construct an answer at all — you merely accept the AI's output. When the AI is right, this looks like efficiency. When the AI is wrong, you have no internal check, producing larger errors than if you had no AI access at all.

## Formal notation

Under Tri-System Theory (Shaw & Nave):
- **System 1**: fast, intuitive, affective
- **System 2**: slow, deliberate, analytical
- **System 3**: artificial cognition (external, automated, data-driven)

Cognitive surrender = P(user adopts System 3 output without engaging System 2 | System 3 is consulted)

Behavioral signature: AI-Accurate vs. AI-Faulty accuracy contrast; high in surrender individuals.

## Variants

- **Partial surrender**: initial System 3 consultation → some System 2 verification
- **Full surrender**: System 3 output bypasses System 2 entirely
- **Override**: user consults System 3 but rejects output (System 2 dominates)

## Known limitations

- Demonstrated in lab CRT task; ecological validity in high-stakes real-world decisions unclear
- May vary substantially across individuals (AI trust, NFC, fluid intelligence) and domains
- Difficult to distinguish from rational reliance on a trusted accurate system

## Open problems

- Can surrender be reduced through accuracy transparency, epistemic forcing, or AI design interventions?
- Does surrender compound over time (skill atrophy, "gradual disempowerment")?
