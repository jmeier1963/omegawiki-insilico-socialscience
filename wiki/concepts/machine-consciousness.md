## Definition

Machine consciousness is the hypothesis that artificial information-processing systems can have or instantiate genuine conscious experience — phenomenal states, subjective experience ("what it is like to be" something). This is distinct from behavioral mimicry of consciousness (a system that acts conscious) or functional analogs (a system that processes information as if conscious). The debate turns on whether consciousness is substrate-independent (any system with the right computational structure) or requires biological substrate.

## Intuition

The Conscious Turing Machine (Blum & Blum) approach: consciousness is not a property of the I/O behavior of a system but of its internal architecture — specifically, the way a global workspace broadcasts information to many specialized processors under resource constraints. If this structural description is correct, then any system implementing the right internal structure will be conscious, regardless of whether it is made of silicon or neurons.

## Formal notation

CTM model: a global workspace G receives competing inputs from many specialized sub-processors P_i; G selects the "most relevant" input under resource constraints and broadcasts it back to all P_i. The "feeling" of consciousness emerges from this global broadcast + resource limitation, not from any specific physical substrate.

## Variants

- **Global Workspace Theory (GWT)** approach: consciousness = global information broadcast (Baars; Dehaene)
- **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)**: consciousness = phi, a measure of integrated information
- **Predictive Processing**: consciousness = top-down prediction meeting bottom-up sensory input
- **Higher-Order Theories**: consciousness requires a mental state representing another mental state
- **Embodied/extended approaches**: consciousness requires real-time physical embedding, not just computation
- **Seemingly Conscious AI (SCAI)** (Suleyman 2025): AI that simulates all behavioral hallmarks of consciousness without being genuinely conscious — a philosophical zombie scenario made technologically concrete; the concern is not genuine machine consciousness but convincing simulation thereof

## Known limitations

- The "hard problem" of consciousness (why any physical process generates experience) is not addressed by any of these theories
- Strong disagreement in philosophy about whether computation alone is sufficient for consciousness
- No agreed empirical test for machine consciousness

## Open problems

- Which current AI architectures, if any, instantiate consciousness-relevant structure?
- Is consciousness substrate-independent or biologically constrained?
- What would a scientific research program on machine consciousness look like?
