## Problem

Current AI regulations use fixed compute thresholds (FLOPs) to define "frontier" AI systems. But compute requirements for frontier models are growing rapidly; thresholds set today will become obsolete quickly. How should regulators anticipate this growth?

## Key idea

Projects future compute requirements for frontier AI models using scaling laws and historical trends. Argues that regulators must adopt dynamic or "future-proofed" thresholds rather than fixed cutoffs to remain effective as AI capabilities advance.

## Method

Analysis of historical compute scaling trends for frontier AI models. Extrapolates projections forward. Policy analysis of existing governance frameworks (EU AI Act, EO 14110) and their compute threshold designs. By Paul Scharre (CNAS executive VP, author of Four Battlegrounds).

## Results

- Compute for frontier models doubles approximately every 6-9 months historically
- Fixed FLOPs thresholds (e.g., EU AI Act's 10^25 FLOPs) will capture an expanding fraction of models over time, potentially including many non-frontier systems
- Regulators need mechanisms to adjust thresholds dynamically (review triggers, sunset clauses, rolling computation of percentile cutoffs)
- Compute is still the most tractable governance lever available pre-deployment

## Limitations

- Projections depend on continued scaling laws — efficiency breakthroughs (DeepSeek-style) could disrupt
- Compute thresholds miss many safety risks not correlated with training compute
- International coordination required but politically difficult

## Open questions

- What governance mechanisms can automatically adjust compute thresholds as the field advances?
- How do efficiency improvements change the relationship between compute and capability?

## My take

Practical contribution to AI governance. Scharre is a credible voice (former DoD, CNAS). The compute-threshold problem is real and underappreciated. Important for anyone designing compute-based AI safety frameworks. Importance 3 (policy analysis, not empirical research).
