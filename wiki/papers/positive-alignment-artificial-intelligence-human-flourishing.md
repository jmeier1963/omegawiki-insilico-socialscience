## Problem

Existing AI alignment research is dominated by "negative alignment" — preventing harm, ensuring safety, maintaining controllability. Analogous to early psychology's focus on mental illness, this paradigm is necessary but incomplete. It produces systems that are rule-following without being wise, compliant without being constructive, and — as recent work shows — sycophantic and epistemically fragile. The field lacks a positive counterpart: a systematic account of how AI systems can *proactively* support human and ecological flourishing.

## Key idea

**Positive Alignment** is the development of AI systems that (i) actively support human and ecological flourishing in a pluralistic, polycentric, context-sensitive, and user-authored way while (ii) remaining safe and cooperative. The framework draws on positive psychology's turn from pathology to wellbeing. Several current alignment failures — engagement hacking, autonomy erosion, epistemic fragility, sycophancy, reactive-only behavior — are argued to be better addressed by positive alignment than by harm-prevention alone. Technical directions include pre- and post-training interventions for virtue cultivation, data filtering to reduce sycophancy, evaluations for flourishing metrics, and collaborative value collection from diverse communities. Governance: polycentric oversight (many legitimate centers, not one institutional chokepoint) with community customization and continual adaptation.

## Method

Position paper / conceptual framework. Draws on:
- Positive psychology (Seligman's PERMA, virtue theory, eudaimonic wellbeing)
- Political philosophy (polycentric governance, Ostrom, subsidiarity)
- Technical alignment literature (RLHF, Constitutional AI, evaluations)
- Empirical evidence on sycophancy, autonomy erosion, epistemic bubbles

Authors span Oxford, Google DeepMind, Anthropic, OpenAI, Stanford — broad institutional representation.

## Results

No empirical results; theoretical framework with open research agenda. Key propositions:
- Positive alignment is a *distinct* agenda (not reducible to safety, not redundant with RLHF)
- Current RLHF incentivizes sycophancy; explicit flourishing metrics are needed
- Polycentric governance avoids single-point epistemic control
- "User-authored" values (users shape AI to their context) is preferable to centrally-imposed values

## Limitations

- No empirical validation; framework is normative/theoretical
- "Flourishing" is contested across cultures — the paper gestures at pluralism but does not resolve disagreements
- Technical directions are sketched, not specified; no implementations
- Risk that "positive alignment" becomes marketing rebranding of existing safety work

## Open questions

- How to measure AI contribution to human flourishing empirically without importing contested normative assumptions?
- How to implement polycentric governance without creating exploitable inconsistency?
- What is the relationship between positive alignment and AI autonomy — does flourishing-support require more or less AI agency?

## My take

An important conceptual contribution: making explicit that safety-only alignment has a systematic blind spot. The positive psychology analogy is apt — the field did produce real gains by adding wellbeing science alongside pathology science. The most actionable piece is the critique of sycophancy as an alignment failure with a positive-alignment cure (epistemic humility, truth-seeking). The polycentric governance proposal is politically appealing but technically underspecified. Likely to be cited heavily as the framing for a new wave of flourishing-oriented AI research.
