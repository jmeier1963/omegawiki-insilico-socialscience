## Problem

Little empirical evidence on how students experience and perceive the process of co-writing academic essays with ChatGPT.

## Key idea

Case study integrating ChatGPT into a university writing assignment. Students collaborate with ChatGPT to draft essays, then reflect on the experience. Both experiences (what students found helpful/problematic) and perceptions (attitudes, concerns) are captured.

## Method

Case study design, course-level integration, qualitative analysis of student reflections. Utrecht University and KTH collaboration.

## Results

Students reported ChatGPT useful for overcoming writer's block and generating initial drafts, but expressed concerns about authenticity, original voice, and dependency. Co-writing highlighted tensions between AI assistance and personal intellectual contribution.

## Limitations

Single course, single institution, qualitative, no control group, small sample.

## Open questions

- Does co-writing with AI systematically change the development of writing skills?
- How should co-writing be structured to maximize learning while maintaining academic integrity?

## My take

Captures an important early student-level perspective on human-AI collaborative writing. The tension between productivity and authenticity recurs across multiple education contexts.
