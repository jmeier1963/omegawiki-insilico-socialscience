## Problem

California State University system (the largest public university in the US) bet $17M on becoming the nation's "largest AI-empowered" university through partnerships with Amazon and OpenAI.

## Key idea

Investigative NYT piece by Natasha Singer on Cal State's ambitious AI integration initiative: faculty adoption, student skepticism, equity concerns, and the tension between institutional boosterism and on-the-ground mixed experiences.

## Method

Investigative journalism; interviews with faculty, students, and administrators; on-campus reporting.

## Results

Mixed adoption: some faculty enthusiastic, others skeptical; student distrust significant; demand for AI training exceeded supply; equity concerns about which students benefit. A case study in institutional top-down AI mandates meeting grassroots ambivalence.

## Limitations

Journalistic piece, not systematic study.

## Open questions

- What institutional governance structures best mediate between top-down AI mandates and faculty/student agency?

## My take

An important case study in institutional AI adoption at scale. The equity dimension (who benefits?) links directly to the AI and disadvantaged communities literature.
