## Problem

Is Meta's decision to open-source LLaMA (its large language model) wise or dangerous — and what are the implications for AI safety and competition?

## Key idea

NYT reporting (May 2023) by Cade Metz and Mike Isaac on Meta's decision to release LLaMA as open source, allowing anyone to build AI chatbots. Rivals including Google argue this is risky: once released, open-source models cannot be recalled if misused. Meta's rationale: democratizing AI accelerates beneficial innovation, and Meta lacks OpenAI's distribution advantage so open-sourcing builds ecosystem. The article documents the open-source vs. proprietary AI safety debate that LLaMA triggered.

## Method

News journalism. 6 pages. Reporting from San Francisco. Cade Metz (AI beat) and Mike Isaac (Meta beat).

## Results

- Meta released LLaMA open-source February 2023; chatbots built within weeks
- Google and OpenAI rivals argue: open-source AI is dangerous — can't be recalled, enables misuse
- Meta rationale: democratization, ecosystem building, lack of distribution moat
- LLaMA spawned rapid derivative community (Alpaca, Vicuna) proving open-source velocity thesis
- Triggered industry-wide debate on open vs. closed AI safety models

## Limitations

- News journalism; limited technical depth
- 2023; landscape has changed significantly (LLaMA 2, 3, etc.)

## My take

Captures the pivotal moment when open-source AI became a central debate. Useful as historical document. The open/closed debate framed here continues to shape AI governance. Importance 2 (news journalism; historically significant moment).
