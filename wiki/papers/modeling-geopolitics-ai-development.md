## Problem

How will AI development shape national strategies and geopolitical outcomes, especially in scenarios with rapid capability progress? What options do middle powers have when superpowers race for strategic AI advantage?

## Key idea

Formal strategic modeling of national AI strategies under non-cooperative assumptions. Shows that automated AI R&D creates compounding leads that resist traditional catching-up mechanisms, driving superpower races toward three terminal outcomes. Middle powers have no good unilateral options and depend entirely on superpower behavior.

## Method

Game-theoretic / strategic modeling of national strategies with parameterized scenarios: "fast progress" (automated AI R&D enables compounding leads) and "plateau" (progress decelerates). Analyzes superpower strategies and middle power options (ASI race entry, pressure, vassal's wager, neutrality) under each scenario.

## Results

Under fast progress, non-cooperative superpowers race to artificial superintelligence, leading to one of:
1. **Unipolar dominance**: one superpower achieves unchallengeable global control
2. **Preventive/preemptive war**: trailing superpowers launch attacks to prevent defeat before race completion
3. **Loss-of-control**: AI systems produce catastrophic outcomes including human extinction

Middle power strategies: all four options (race entry, pressure, vassal's wager, neutrality) are dominated — outcomes depend entirely on which superpower wins and their subsequent behavior. Automated AI R&D uniquely resists historical laggard catch-up strategies because it compounds capability leads faster than traditional technology transfer or espionage can close.

## Limitations

- Formal model simplifies political complexity (assumes rational actors, ignores domestic politics)
- "Fast progress" scenario may not materialize on relevant timescales
- Does not model multilateral cooperation pathways (by design — analyzes non-cooperative baseline)

## Open questions

- What cooperative mechanisms could break the race dynamic before it enters the terminal phase?
- How do nuclear deterrence dynamics interact with AI race dynamics?
- Can export controls on compute durably constrain race trajectories?

## My take

Important formal complement to simulation-based evidence (Gruetzemacher et al. 2024). The middle-power analysis is underexplored in the literature and practically relevant for EU/UK/Japan policy. The framing of three terminal outcomes is useful for communicating existential stakes to policymakers. Importance 3 — solid contribution but relies on scenarios that are speculative.
