## Problem

Are voluntary AI commitments (White House 2023 commitments from major AI companies) adequate governance, and what specific measures are actionable?

## Key idea

Andrew Ng's weekly newsletter (The Batch, DeepLearning.AI, July 2023) commenting on: (1) White House voluntary AI commitments from 7 AI companies, (2) ChatGPT performance drift, (3) Apple's generative AI approach. Key argument: most voluntary commitments are vague enough to be meaningless, but the watermarking commitment is concrete and measurable — enabling actual verification of whether soft regulation works.

## Method

Newsletter commentary, 14 pages. Personal analysis by Andrew Ng (AI researcher, Google Brain co-founder, Coursera co-founder).

## Results

- White House 2023 voluntary commitments: 7 AI companies committed to safety measures
- Most commitments vague; watermarking commitment is exception: concrete, testable
- AI-generated content watermarking: technically feasible, socially beneficial, commercially costly for platforms
- ChatGPT behavior drift: documented performance changes between versions suggest model instability
- Recommendation: use watermarking as test case for whether voluntary regulation can work

## Limitations

- Newsletter format; advocacy perspective
- 2023 analysis; watermarking landscape has evolved significantly

## Open questions

- Has watermarking proven effective as a voluntary commitment? (Mixed results since)
- Are voluntary commitments generally sufficient for AI governance?

## My take

Andrew Ng's practical take on AI policy. The watermarking-as-test-for-voluntary-regulation argument is analytically useful. Importance 2 (newsletter from credible practitioner; useful for voluntary regulation debates).
