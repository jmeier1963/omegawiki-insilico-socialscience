## Problem

Existing political economy frameworks (platform capitalism, knowledge economy) were developed for asset-light, data-monetization firms. They fail to capture how leading tech firms have transformed into asset-heavy cloud infrastructure providers, creating a new business model that concentrates structural power differently.

## Key idea

Documents the emergence of "cloud capitalism" — a new business model distinct from platform capitalism. Cloud firms have shifted from asset-light intermediation to asset-heavy infrastructure (massive compute investment), from hierarchical to semi-vertically integrated supply chains, from consumer-facing to enterprise-facing strategies. The AI race has accelerated this transformation and forged techno-nationalist alliances between cloud firms and states.

## Method

Historical and comparative political economy analysis. Traces the evolution from platform capitalism to cloud capitalism across major US cloud providers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud). Analyzes financial data, corporate strategy documents, and policy developments to characterize the new model's features and political implications.

## Results

- Cloud firms have become structural "choke points" in the economy: controlling compute access gives them unprecedented leverage over all digital industries
- Semi-vertical integration: cloud firms collaborate with (rather than dominate) partner firms in enterprise markets, unlike consumer-platform winner-takes-all dynamics
- The AGI narrative sustains investment momentum and justifies capital concentration — but commercialization (revenue vs. capex) remains far from justifying scale investments
- Techno-nationalist alliances have emerged: cloud firms leverage national security framing to secure state support and resist regulatory pressure
- DeepSeek-style efficiency gains (Jevons Paradox) may actually increase compute demand rather than undermining cloud capitalism
- Power-distributional effects: cloud capitalism concentrates rents at the infrastructure level, with limited spillovers to workers or society

## Limitations

- Analysis is US-centric; EU and Chinese cloud capitalism may evolve differently
- Written during the rapid-change period (2024–2025); some claims may already be outdated
- Does not fully model the counter-power dynamics (EU regulation, antitrust, open-source models)

## Open questions

- Can regulatory intervention (EU AI Act, antitrust) break cloud capitalism's choke-point power?
- Will the AGI investment bubble correct, and how would that reshape cloud capitalism?
- Does cloud capitalism produce a new techno-nationalist fragmentation of the global economy?

## My take

Important political economy contribution. Thelen is one of the leading political economists studying tech capitalism. The "cloud capitalism" framing is useful and empirically grounded. The Jevons Paradox application to AI efficiency is particularly insightful. The analysis of techno-nationalism is timely. Importance 4 — influential for policy and social science researchers.
