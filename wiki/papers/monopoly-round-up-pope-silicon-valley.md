## Problem

A political-economy newsletter essay framing a "spiritual" dimension behind AI-era political economy: who decides the values that govern powerful technology, and what happens when economic and moral authority concentrate in a few tech magnates. Hook: Pope Leo XIV — "A more moral AI is not enough if that morality is determined by a few."

## Key idea

Contrast two worldviews. **Elon Musk** (whose net worth passed $1tn after SpaceX's public listing) embodies a **transhumanist, efficiency-maximizing ideology** — the "ultimate expression of the Chicago School" — that seeks to *optimize humanity*, transcend human limits (Neuralink "superpowers," ending aging/death, multi-planetary expansion), and become "God-like." **Pope Leo XIV** offers the older, social-justice tradition that **limits are what make us human**. Stoller's argument: the dominant institutional setup channels moral and economic power to a narrow transhumanist elite, and AI is the latest vehicle for that concentration.

## Method

Op-ed/commentary plus a monopoly-news round-up (Iran/Strait of Hormuz, Warner/Paramount, etc.). The featured essay is ideological/cultural analysis, not empirical research.

## Results

- Documents the scale and structure of concentrated tech wealth/control (Musk owns ~half of SpaceX, controls ~80% of voting shares; SpaceX repositioned as an AI/compute firm in the "AI-tech-crypto" boom).
- Characterizes a shared elite ideology: Musk's "legion"/eugenics-tinged pronatalism, Altman's consciousness-uploading immortality — a recurring "Fountain of Youth" transhumanism among tech magnates.
- Frames the political stakes as a contest over whose conception of "the human soul" rules shape — efficiency/transcendence vs. limits/dignity.

## Limitations

- Polemical newsletter, not scholarship; selective and rhetorical.
- Conflates several figures/ideologies under "transhumanism"; little engagement with counter-arguments.
- AI is one thread among monopoly-news items.

## Open questions

- How much does elite ideology actually shape deployed AI's values vs. market/competitive forces?
- What institutional mechanisms could broaden who determines AI's "morality"?

## My take

Useful as a cultural-criticism data point on the **ideology** layer of AI power — the "morality determined by a few" worry that motivates pluralistic-alignment and democratic-governance arguments elsewhere in the wiki. Thin as analysis, but names a real phenomenon: AI's direction is entangled with a specific Silicon Valley transhumanist worldview.
