## Problem

What legislative framework should the US Congress adopt to govern AI development, ensuring American leadership while protecting children and national security?

## Key idea

White House (Trump Administration) National Policy Framework for AI Legislative Recommendations, March 2026. 4-page document outlining the administration's priorities for Congressional AI legislation: protecting children from AI harms, enabling AI infrastructure buildout, national security applications, and preempting burdensome state-level AI regulations. Reflects a light-touch deregulatory approach combined with targeted child-safety and national security requirements.

## Method

Executive branch policy document with legislative recommendations to Congress. Organized thematically: (1) Protecting children and empowering parents, (2) Energy and infrastructure, (3) Fraud protection, (4) National security, (5) Preemption of state AI laws.

## Results

Key recommendations:
- Age-assurance requirements and child-safety features for AI platforms likely accessed by minors
- Streamlined federal permitting for AI data center construction
- Ratepayer protection from increased electricity costs due to AI infrastructure
- Agencies with capacity to assess frontier AI national security implications
- Preemption of patchwork state AI regulations to avoid compliance fragmentation
- No open-ended liability for content moderation (limiting excessive litigation)

## Limitations

- Short 4-page document; lacks technical specificity
- Reflects single administration's priorities; bipartisan consensus absent
- Light-touch approach may be insufficient for frontier AI risks
- Omits major AI safety, transparency, and labor displacement concerns

## Open questions

- Will Congress adopt a comprehensive federal AI law or defer to executive action?
- How to balance preemption of state laws (reducing fragmentation) vs. states as laboratories?
- Is child-safety + national security framing sufficient, or do frontier risks require more?

## My take

Important document as the first Trump-era White House AI legislative framework. Signals a deregulatory-with-targeted-carve-outs approach: minimal general regulation, specific child safety requirements, national security carve-outs. Importance 4 (official White House legislative recommendations, directly shapes legislative agenda).
