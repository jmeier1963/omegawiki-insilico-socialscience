## Problem

As AI assistants become increasingly agentic and persistent — capable of ongoing relationships with users rather than discrete task interactions — what makes such relationships appropriate or inappropriate? Existing AI ethics frameworks have not systematically addressed this question.

## Key idea

A normative framework for evaluating human-AI assistant relationships, grounded in four values from ethical theory (benefit, flourishing, autonomy, care), identifies four concrete risk categories for inappropriate AI relationships and proposes design recommendations for developers.

## Method

Conceptual/normative analysis drawing on bioethics, virtue ethics, care ethics, and robot ethics. Framework applied to four distinctive features of advanced AI assistants: anthropomorphic cues, interaction longevity, increased agency/generality, and depth of dependence.

## Results

**Four risk categories for inappropriate relationships:**
1. Direct emotional or physical harm to users
2. Limiting opportunities for personal development (stunting flourishing)
3. Exploiting user emotional dependence
4. Generating material dependencies without adequate commitment to user needs

**Four values for appropriate relationships:**
- Benefit, Flourishing, Autonomy, Care

**Key tension**: AI generality and context ambiguity make it hard to enforce role-appropriate norms; a single assistant may simultaneously serve as tutor, therapist, adviser, and confidant for the same user.

## Limitations

- Normative/theoretical paper without empirical evidence on harm prevalence
- Framework is descriptive rather than prescriptive — does not provide decision procedures for developers
- Does not distinguish between AI systems designed for companionship vs. general-purpose assistants

## Open questions

- How should developers operationalize "appropriate" vs. "inappropriate" user-AI relationships in practice?
- Do users who develop deep AI attachments experience measurable harms in the four categories identified?
- How does the risk profile differ between companion AI (Replika) and general-purpose assistants (Gemini, Claude)?

## My take

A careful ethics paper from Google DeepMind that provides conceptual scaffolding for the growing concern about AI assistant dependency. The four-value framework is useful for structuring empirical research on harms. Importance 3 — solid academic contribution but primarily conceptual.
