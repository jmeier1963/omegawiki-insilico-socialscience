## Problem

AI race dynamics between states and corporations create destabilizing pressures that increase catastrophic risk. Existing policy and foresight work lacks empirical grounding in how stakeholders actually navigate these dynamics in realistic multi-actor simulations.

## Key idea

Documents strategic insights from 43 runs of "Intelligence Rising" — a tabletop simulation exercise modelling AI race dynamics between US, China, and major AI developers — over four years (Sept 2020–July 2024). Uses structured facilitator elicitation to identify recurring patterns in governance decision-making under AI race conditions.

## Method

Three lead facilitators conducted structured elicitation with an independent party after 43 games. Participants simulate state leaders and corporate executives navigating AI development races. Three research questions examined: (1) key insights about AI's role over 10 years, (2) recurring strategies and decision-making patterns, (3) effectiveness of game design for exposing governance complexity.

## Results

- AI races are consistently destabilizing: competitive dynamics erode cooperation thresholds and increase catastrophic risk probabilities
- International agreements tend to emerge but remain fragile, especially under endgame uncertainty about safety research states
- Cybersecurity is a critical bottleneck that frequently disrupts AI development trajectories
- Corporate and national interests systematically misalign: firms optimize differently from state actors
- Coordination on AI safety research cannot be left until the endgame; early safety commons are essential even if some actors free-ride
- Participants from US/Europe are systematically more comfortable portraying Western actors — introducing roleplay bias
- Participant background knowledge in AI risk/safety has increased over the 4-year period, improving game quality

## Limitations

- Intelligence Rising is not a validated predictive model; insights are qualitative and facilitator-interpreted
- Participant pool skewed toward Western academic/policy backgrounds
- Game mechanics may not capture all relevant AI race dynamics (e.g., hardware supply chains, diffusion patterns)
- Structured elicitation from facilitators introduces retrospective bias

## Open questions

- How do AI race dynamics change when hardware and compute constraints are modeled explicitly?
- Can formal wargaming methods produce quantifiable risk estimates for different governance regimes?
- How does the safety free-rider problem scale with number of state actors?

## My take

Valuable empirical contribution to AI governance foresight research. The 43-game dataset gives this study more grounding than typical scenario-planning exercises. The insight that safety coordination cannot be deferred to the endgame is particularly important for policymakers. Importance 3 (solid field contribution, not a methods breakthrough).
