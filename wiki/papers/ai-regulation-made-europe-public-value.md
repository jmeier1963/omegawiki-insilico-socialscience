## Problem

Does the EU AI Act adequately serve the common good, and how should AI regulation be evaluated through a public value lens rather than just economic or technical criteria?

## Key idea

Conference paper (HHL Leipzig, Public Value Fest 2024) by Wittmann and Roloff (supervised by Timo Meynhardt) applying Public Value Theory to evaluate the EU AI Act. Argues that AI regulation should be assessed by its contribution to public value—societal well-being, democratic legitimacy, and common good—not only innovation protection or risk mitigation. Finds the EU AI Act partially but incompletely addresses public value dimensions.

## Method

Theoretical framework application. Public Value Theory (Meynhardt) applied to EU AI Act analysis. 47-page conference paper. Sections: AI ethics, Public Value Theory, EU AI approach.

## Results

- EU AI Act reflects compliance-oriented approach; strong on risk categories but weaker on positive public value creation
- Public value requires AI to actively contribute to human flourishing, not merely avoid harms
- AI ethics frameworks often remain at principle level without implementation mechanisms
- Recommends integrating public value assessment into AI regulatory review processes
- European approach offers democratic legitimacy advantages over US market-led and Chinese state-led models

## Limitations

- Conference paper; not peer-reviewed journal publication
- Public Value Theory framework not universally accepted
- Normative analysis; limited empirical testing

## Open questions

- How to operationalize "public value" in AI regulatory assessment?
- Can the EU AI Act be amended to include positive public value requirements?

## My take

Valuable contribution to the normative debate about what AI regulation should achieve beyond risk avoidance. Public Value framing is a useful counterpoint to pure economic efficiency or safety-only perspectives. Importance 3 (substantive theoretical analysis from legitimate academic institution).
