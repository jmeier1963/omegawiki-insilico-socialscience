## Problem

How does technological innovation—especially AI—determine geopolitical power, and what should the US do to maintain strategic advantage?

## Key idea

Eric Schmidt (former Google CEO) argues in Foreign Affairs (March/April 2023) that technological capability is now the decisive factor in geopolitical competition, displacing traditional military and economic metrics. Ukraine's use of technology against numerically superior Russian forces illustrates the "innovation power" thesis. Schmidt calls for the US to treat AI, semiconductors, and defense technology as core national security infrastructure, accelerating adoption and closing the gap with China.

## Method

Policy analysis essay, Foreign Affairs March/April 2023. Schmidt draws on his background as former Google CEO and chair of the National Security Commission on AI.

## Results

- "Innovation power" — technological capability — is now primary geopolitical currency
- Ukraine case: tech advantage (drones, Starlink, targeting AI) enabled resistance despite 2:1 military disadvantage
- China's military-civil fusion doctrine makes AI a national security asset by design
- US must: accelerate defense AI adoption, reform DARPA/DoD procurement, invest in semiconductor supply chain, maintain AI talent pipelines
- Recommends treating AI and critical technology as infrastructure requiring public investment, not just private sector output

## Limitations

- Written from a US-centric perspective; does not engage deeply with other powers' technological strategies
- May overstate technology's role relative to logistics, morale, and political factors in Ukraine
- "Innovation power" framing may justify military AI escalation

## Open questions

- Can democracies maintain AI advantage long-term given adversaries' state-directed R&D?
- Does "innovation power" framing risk militarizing civilian AI development?

## My take

Influential argument from a significant figure (Schmidt co-founded the National Security Commission on AI). The Ukraine evidence is compelling for the "technology determines outcomes" thesis, though causal complexity is understated. Importance 3 (significant policy analysis from highly credible practitioner, Foreign Affairs).
