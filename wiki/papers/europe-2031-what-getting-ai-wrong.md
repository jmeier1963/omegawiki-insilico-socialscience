## Problem

European AI policy is failing to register the pace and magnitude of AI's advance ("like the early days of Covid"): AI already writes most software at leading labs, is starting to run its own research, and is redefining cybersecurity (Claude "Mythos Preview"). The authors warn that on its current trajectory Europe loses the ability to shape its own future — economically and politically sidelined, with welfare systems it can no longer fund and a Union that cannot hold.

## Key idea

Written as a near-future **novella-scenario** (Jan 2025 → spring 2031, epilogue 2034) following a French Commission official (Caroline) and a German founder-emigré (Christian). The thesis: Europe's slide into irrelevance is plausible but *not inevitable* — averting it requires the "most ambitious political agenda in post-war European history," begun in 2026, centred on **sovereign AI compute** and coalition-building, not regulation alone.

## Method

Scenario/narrative method: an internally consistent, "technically sound," traceable-to-today storyline meant to make transformative-AI dynamics *felt* rather than abstract. Not a prediction; a plausibility warning to galvanise political will.

## Results

Counterfactual levers the epilogue identifies (available in June 2026):
- **Compute on European soil** — "the metal on our soil," tens of gigawatts, anchored under EU law beyond Washington's reach; **Special Compute Zones** cutting permitting from ~2 years to ~3 months, concierge teams, converting decommissioned power plants, building generation "like you mean it." (Europe held only ~5% of global compute; could have reached 15–20%.)
- **Partner with US hyperscalers but "bolt to the floor"** — accept that Europe can't build all datacentres itself; ensure those built are jurisdiction-anchored. Speed, not subsidies, was the real currency.
- **Coalition of middle powers** — NL, DE, FR, Norway, UK, Canada, Japan, South Korea coordinating supply-chain, talent, and energy leverage instead of negotiating as 27 (the ASML failure).
- **Robotics/industrial AI** — unlock data, set up industry partnerships, screen foreign investment (else incumbents buy the researchers).
- **Flexicurity** — Danish-style wage insurance + retraining + hiring/firing flexibility to enable deep AI adoption.
- **A positive vision** — the decisive failure was narrative: only "negative visions" of what Europe was losing, never a compelling picture of the good future.

## Limitations

- Explicitly fictional/scenario; dates and events are illustrative, not forecasts.
- Advocacy document with a strong prior (Europe must act drastically); selection of plausible dynamics is authorial.
- Heavy reliance on contested assumptions about AI's pace ("Industrial Revolution in years not decades").

## Open questions

- Is the compute-sovereignty window already closed, or still open in 2026?
- Can a middle-power coalition actually coordinate against two "empires"?
- What is the "positive vision" Europe could credibly offer?

## My take

The most operationally concrete item is the **Special Compute Zones / "bolt it to the floor"** framing — sovereignty as jurisdictional anchoring of compute rather than autarky. Sits inside [[digital-sovereignty]] and is a vivid instantiation of the [[ai-policy-pacing-problem]] (Europe perpetually two years behind its own directors' understanding). The "we never had a positive vision" diagnosis is the sharpest political insight.
