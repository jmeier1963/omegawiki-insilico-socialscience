## Problem & Context

As enterprises move from isolated pilots to coordinated, enterprise-wide agent deployments, the cost, governance and workforce implications become harder to manage.

## Key idea

Three themes: (1) the **economics of AI** (cost visibility lags deployment); (2) **agent orchestration expands** from standalone applications to coordinated enterprise-level workflows; (3) **aligning usage to meaningful outcomes**: incentives focused on usage metrics risk reinforcing the wrong behaviors.

## Method

Online survey conducted 28 April to 25 May 2026 of 204 US C-suite and business leaders at organizations with annual revenue of $1 billion or more; reported as descriptive percentages and quarter-on-quarter comparisons.

## Experiment & Results

Most firms have foundational cost governance but lack full real-time end-to-end cost visibility; controls reported include AI cost monitoring dashboards (66%), cost review in approval processes (61%), architecture or prompt design standards (47%) and usage or token budgets (36%). Agent deployment is steady overall while multi-agent workflows grow, and employees increasingly feel strain from added complexity and workload.

## Limitations

Vendor-commissioned pulse survey with a modest sample of large US firms, self-reported and descriptive; percentages were read from chart layouts in a PDF and should be checked against the source before citing.

## Open questions

How will cost accounting for tokens and agents mature? Which incentive designs actually improve outcomes rather than usage?

## My take

Low-importance supporting evidence for the point that cost and incentive design, not model capability, are emerging bottlenecks in agent rollouts.
