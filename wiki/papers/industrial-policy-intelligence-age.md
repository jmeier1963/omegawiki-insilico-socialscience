## Problem

OpenAI argues a transition toward **superintelligence** (AI outperforming the smartest humans even when those humans are AI-assisted) is already underway. The promise is enormous (cheaper goods, scientific breakthroughs, new work), but so are the risks: job/industry disruption, misuse, misalignment/loss of control, anti-democratic deployment, and **concentration of power and wealth**. Today's policy toolkit is inadequate; without policy keeping pace, the institutions and safety nets needed for the transition fall behind.

## Key idea

The transition needs a **new industrial policy / renegotiated social contract** — analogous to how the Progressive Era and New Deal modernized the social contract for the Industrial Age — aimed at three outcomes: (1) **share prosperity broadly**, (2) **mitigate risks** (safety scales with capability), and (3) **democratize access and agency** (participation shouldn't require access to the most powerful models). Markets alone are insufficient when new technology outpaces existing institutions.

## Method

A normative, "intentionally early and exploratory" policy agenda (US-focused starting point), organized into (1) building an open economy and (2) building a resilient society. Emphasizes public–private iteration: nongovernmental institutions pilot, governments scale what works via procurement/regulation/investment — explicitly to avoid regulatory capture and centralized control.

## Results

Representative proposals (open-economy section):
- **Worker voice** — formal worker–management collaboration so AI improves job quality/safety; let workers prioritize deployments that remove dangerous/repetitive tasks; set limits on uses that intensify workloads or erode autonomy.
- **AI-first entrepreneurs** — "startup-in-a-box" (microgrants, revenue-based finance, shared back-office, model contracts) to turn domain expertise into firms.
- **Right to AI** — treat affordable, reliable access to foundational models as basic infrastructure (like literacy/electricity/internet), with free/low-cost baseline access.
- **Share returns** — expand access to capital and distribute AI-driven gains more widely; modernize economic security for transitions.
- **Near-term asks** — datacentres should pay their own energy way and generate local jobs/tax; "common-sense" regulation (protect children, national security, encourage innovation) without entrenching incumbents.

## Limitations

- Authored by a frontier lab with direct commercial stakes (acknowledges gains may "concentrate within a small number of firms like OpenAI").
- Deliberately non-committal ("initial ideas," no costing or mechanisms detailed).
- US-centric; assumes a contested superintelligence trajectory.

## Open questions

- What concrete mechanisms would actually broaden returns (equity stakes, sovereign wealth funds, taxes)?
- Can "Right to AI" be funded and governed without entrenching the model providers?
- How to give workers real voice rather than consultative cover?

## My take

Notable mainly as a frontier lab endorsing predistribution and a New-Deal-scale social contract. Substantively overlaps Amodei's macro section ([[policy-ai-exponential]]) and the Economist's "how to share AI riches" — all converge on the prosperity-distribution problem. The "Right to AI" framing is the most novel item; the document is thin on mechanism.
