## Problem

The dominant human capital theory of education (orienting schools toward labor market preparation) has failed: PISA results are flat, equity gaps persist, and mental health crises are growing. AI accelerates this crisis by threatening to make existing human capital less relevant while also posing existential risks.

## Key idea

Education must be reoriented from human capital development toward "human flourishing" — rooted in Aristotle's eudaimonia (happiness, meaning, relationships, accomplishment) and complemented by Ubuntu, Confucian, and Buddhist emphases on care for others. The framework defines five core competencies that AI cannot trivially replicate and that prepare humans to live meaningfully in an AI-saturated world.

## Method

Conceptual framework development by the OECD Education 2030 project. Synthesizes philosophy (Aristotle, Ubuntu, Confucianism), cognitive science, pedagogy, and policy analysis across PISA high-performing systems. 147 pages with competency models, learning environment design, and teacher workforce implications.

## Results

Five competencies for human flourishing:
1. **Acting in the world** — finding purpose, setting intent, achieving appropriate actions
2. **Adaptive problem-solving** — solving ill-defined problems in complex, uncertain environments
3. **Ethical competence** — moral reasoning and value-based decision-making
4. **Understanding the world** — reconciling competing worldviews and building meaning
5. **Appreciating the world** — aesthetic, spiritual, and ecological sensibility

Architecture: foundation (wellbeing, identity, inclusion) → competencies → flourishing outcomes at individual, social, and planetary levels.

AI specifically: near-term AI threatens human meaning, agency, and security; longer-term superintelligent AI may threaten human survival. Human flourishing education is positioned as the best societal response.

## Limitations

A normative/conceptual framework, not an empirical study. Implementation guidance is aspirational; how to measure "appreciating the world" or translate "acting in the world" into curriculum remains underdeveloped.

## Open questions

- How do the five competencies get operationalized into assessable curriculum standards across different national contexts?
- Can human flourishing education scale to under-resourced systems or does it presuppose institutional capacity?
- How should AI tools be integrated into human flourishing classrooms without undermining the very capacities they aim to develop?

## My take

A major OECD policy intervention that explicitly invokes the Bildung tradition (human self-formation) and extends it into the AI era. The framing of AI as a threat to human meaning, not just employment, is an important escalation from earlier OECD education discourse. Worth reading alongside Bieger & Kolmar and the Mollick frameworks.
