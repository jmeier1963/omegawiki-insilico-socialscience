## Problem

Rapid adoption of ChatGPT raised immediate questions about its impact on academic libraries and scholarly research workflows.

## Key idea

Overview of ChatGPT technology (GPT architecture, training process) plus a simulated interview with ChatGPT about its own potential impact on libraries. Covers: improved search/discovery, reference services, cataloging/metadata generation, content creation, scholarly paper writing, and ethical considerations (bias, privacy).

## Method

Descriptive overview + qualitative interview with ChatGPT itself (early example of using LLMs to reason about their own impact).

## Results

ChatGPT identified potential benefits for libraries across search, reference, cataloging, and writing support; it also flagged bias, privacy, and accuracy risks. The paper is primarily descriptive rather than empirical.

## Limitations

Written in January 2023 (GPT-3.5 era), rapidly outdated. No empirical study. The ChatGPT-interviewing-itself methodology is methodologically novel but unvalidated.

## Open questions

- How have the predicted library impacts materialized as LLMs improved?
- What institutional policies for libraries emerged from early ChatGPT deployment?

## My take

A snapshot of the first months of ChatGPT adoption in academic libraries. Useful as historical baseline, not as research contribution.
