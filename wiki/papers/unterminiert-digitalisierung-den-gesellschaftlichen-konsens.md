## Problem

Does digitalization undermine the shared reservoir of values, orientations, and convictions that grounds social cooperation and the legitimacy of collective rules?

## Key idea

Drawing on Max Weber's rationalization thesis and Goethe's sorcerer's apprentice metaphor, Suchanek argues that digital technologies — especially AI — accelerate the processing of complexity in ways that increasingly escape human understanding and control. The paper asks whether this undermines the social consensus needed for legitimate collective governance. Key tension: digitalization offers immediate benefits (convenience, efficiency) while generating negative externalities that erode the shared normative foundations needed to regulate those externalities.

## Method

Philosophical/normative analysis. German-language working paper from a business ethics perspective. Engages Weber, Rawls, Luhmann, and social contract theory. Explicitly marked "work in progress."

## Results

Preliminary argument: digitalization creates a structural problem for social consensus by:
1. Fragmenting information environments (filter bubbles, algorithmic curation) → divergent factual worldviews
2. Accelerating complexity beyond the cognitive processing capacity of individuals and institutions
3. Shifting agency from humans to algorithmic systems, eroding the experienced sense of collective authorship of social rules

## Limitations

Work in progress; explicitly speculative and incomplete. German-language only. No empirical data.

## Open questions

- Can existing institutions of democratic deliberation adapt fast enough to maintain legitimacy under algorithmic complexity?
- What institutional designs could restore experienced collective agency in an algorithmic society?

## My take

A philosophically grounded contribution to AI governance debates from a German business ethics tradition. The "sorcerer's apprentice" framing and the consensus-legitimacy nexus are insightful even if underdeveloped. Relevant as a philosophical counterpart to empirical AI governance studies.
