## Problem & Context

Three years after gen AI tools launched, AI use is nearly universal, but it is unclear whether and how organizations capture enterprise-level value, and how agents change that.

## Key idea

Breadth is high but depth is low: most organizations are still experimenting or piloting. High performers differ by setting growth and innovation objectives beyond efficiency and by **redesigning workflows**.

## Method

McKinsey Global Survey of respondents across organizations, reported as descriptive shares with exhibits by function, phase of use and expectations; analysis of high performers (those attributing material EBIT impact to AI).

## Experiment & Results

88% report regular AI use in at least one function (78% a year earlier); nearly two-thirds have not begun scaling across the enterprise; 62% are at least experimenting with agents; 64% say AI enables innovation, but only 39% report any EBIT impact; 80% set efficiency as an objective, while top performers add growth or innovation, and half of them intend to transform the business; workforce expectations: 32% decrease, 43% no change, 13% increase.

## Limitations

Self-reported survey by a consultancy with selection and attribution issues; EBIT impact is respondent-attributed, not measured.

## Open questions

What share of the pilot-to-scale gap is explained by workflow redesign vs. data and governance? Will workforce decrease expectations materialize?

## My take

A standard reference for adoption statistics; best used with caution alongside measured evidence (RCTs, usage telemetry) because the outcome variables are perceptions.
