## Problem

The literature on LLM-generated synthetic participants is fragmented, methodologically heterogeneous, and lacks a comprehensive framework for understanding when and why synthetic participants succeed or fail. Existing reviews either are too narrow, focus on non-LLM synthetic data, or do not systematically assess empirical findings across the field.

## Key idea

A systematic literature review of 182 high-quality publications on LLM-generated synthetic participants, synthesizing findings into a 4-category taxonomy of systematic issues and a proposal to frame synthetic participants as **heuristic-like tools** (rather than substitutes for human participants). The heuristic framing sets appropriate expectations: useful for exploration and approximation, but not reliable for high-stakes decisions where ground-truth human data is required.

## Method

- Hybrid search: Scopus, Web of Science, IEEE, ACM (database search) + backward/forward reference search (Zotero)
- Initial corpus: 4,400 publications → 3,134 after deduplication → 145 after title/abstract screening (LLM-assisted, GPT-4) → 182 final after quality assessment and reference search
- Quality criteria: relevance (must involve LLM synthetic participants in experiment), experiment conducted, full text available, journal Q3+ or conference ICORE B+ (for published), quality checklist for preprints (≥15/19 items)
- Two independent researchers for all screening stages; third adjudicator for disagreements
- Data extraction and coding: domains (RQ1), tasks (RQ2), issues (RQ3), prompt engineering (RQ4), participant modeling (RQ5), model selection (RQ6)

## Results

**4-category taxonomy of synthetic participant issues:**
1. **Cognitive misalignments**: LLMs lack psychological and neurophysiological processes present in humans (emotions, motivations, embodied cognition) — responses miss cognitive dimensions that drive human behavior
2. **Distortions and biases**: Systematic skews in representation (Western/liberal bias, demographic overregularization, political bias, cultural homogenization)
3. **Misleading believability**: LLM responses are often highly plausible-sounding but factually incorrect or behaviorally misaligned — "stochastic parroting" of training data patterns that superficially resemble but do not replicate human cognition
4. **Overfitting and contamination**: LLMs may have been trained on the same data they are being tested against, inflating apparent fidelity; fine-tuning can introduce overfitting to specific personas

**Domains**: Most prevalent: social/political sciences, psychology, HCI (predictably human-centric)
**Methods**: Fidelity improvements from prompt engineering (persona specification, chain-of-thought) are real but modest
**Heuristic framing**: Proposes "supplemental heuristic-like approach" — useful for exploration, pre-testing, cost reduction, augmenting small samples; not valid for high-stakes policy or clinical decisions

## Limitations

- Preprint (not yet peer-reviewed)
- Literature search executed 30/06/2025 — may miss more recent work
- "Synthetic participant" definition excludes some related work (e.g., purely synthetic tabular data generation)
- LLM-assisted screening may have introduced systematic exclusion patterns

## Open questions

- Under what specific conditions do synthetic participants achieve behavioral validity sufficient for high-stakes applications?
- Can augmentative approaches (combining real and synthetic data) reliably correct for known biases?
- How should ethical guidelines evolve to govern use of synthetic participants in policy and clinical research?
- Does the "heuristic" framing provide sufficient guidance for practitioners to determine appropriate use cases?

## My take

One of the most rigorous systematic reviews of the synthetic participant field: 182 papers, multi-database, rigorous quality assessment, independent double-coding. The 4-category taxonomy is practically useful and the heuristic framing is a valuable epistemic correction to overclaims. The main limitation is that it captures the state of 2023-2025 literature; given the pace of LLM development, some findings may already be outdated. The finding that fidelity improvements from better prompting remain modest is the most important empirical result.
