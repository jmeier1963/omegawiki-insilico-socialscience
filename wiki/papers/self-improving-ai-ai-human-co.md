## Problem

Pure AI self-improvement (AI autonomously improving itself) is technically difficult and safety-fraught. Is there a more achievable and safer path to superintelligence?

## Key idea

**Co-improvement as the target**: rather than autonomous AI self-improvement, maximize *co-improvement* — collaboration between human researchers and AI systems to conduct AI research together (from ideation to experimentation). This produces "co-superintelligence": symbiotic human-AI capability that is both achievable sooner and safer than fully autonomous AI self-improvement.

## Method

- Position paper from Meta FAIR
- Surveys the landscape of AI self-improvement (parameterized weight optimization → data generation → code improvement → autonomous architecture design)
- Argues that including human researchers in the improvement loop provides:
  - Safety (human oversight maintained throughout)
  - Speed (can leverage current AI capabilities immediately)
  - Generality (humans provide high-level research intuition AI currently lacks)

## Results

- Theoretical position; no empirical experiments
- Co-improvement is argued to dominate both pure human research and pure AI self-improvement on the current capability frontier
- Path to co-superintelligence: AI systems that work *with* human researchers to design experiments, generate hypotheses, analyze results, and write code — gradually increasing the AI's autonomous contribution

## Limitations

- Position paper without experiments
- "Safer" claim is asserted but not rigorously demonstrated
- Does not specify what mechanisms prevent co-improvement from transitioning to uncontrolled self-improvement

## Open questions

- What governance ensures co-improvement stays co-improvement and doesn't drift to autonomous self-improvement?
- Which AI research tasks are most tractable for AI autonomy vs. human direction?
- How does co-improvement interact with the software intelligence explosion scenario?

## My take

A pragmatic and safety-conscious alternative framing to the self-improvement narrative. The co-improvement frame is useful for policy: it offers an accelerationist path that is not synonymous with uncontrolled AI autonomy. However, the argument needs more rigorous safety analysis. Importance 3 — influential position from Meta FAIR researchers.
