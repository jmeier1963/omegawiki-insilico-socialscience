## Problem

The US Office of Management and Budget (OMB) released AI safety implementation guidance for federal agencies in March 2024, requiring extensive compliance processes for "rights-impacting" and "safety-impacting" AI systems. The question: will these regulations effectively safeguard the public, or will they primarily impede AI adoption in government?

## Key idea

Argues that OMB's AI safety requirements — impact assessments, independent evaluation, community consultation, human review, real-world testing, ongoing monitoring — layer excessive compliance burden onto federal AI projects. Given historical failures of government IT regulation, these requirements will delay or entirely halt AI adoption for most covered systems.

## Method

Policy analysis and commentary. Reviews OMB memo requirements and draws on government IT procurement history to assess implementation likelihood. Written by Matthew Mittelsteadt (Digital Spirits Substack).

## Results

- "Rights-impacting" and "safety-impacting" AI categories are defined so broadly as to cover most substantive federal AI use
- Compliance requirements involve extensive bureaucratic processes that historically paralyze government IT projects
- Risk: beneficial AI applications in healthcare, social services, and fraud detection will be blocked
- Alternative view (not endorsed): requirements create appropriate accountability for high-stakes government AI

## Limitations

- Opinion piece, not empirical research
- Does not model compliance costs quantitatively
- Counter-argument (that safety requirements are appropriate) is not engaged seriously

## Open questions

- What is the actual implementation rate of compliant AI systems under the OMB framework?
- Can streamlined "safe harbor" processes preserve safety without blocking adoption?

## My take

Useful policy commentary from an AI governance critic. Represents the "innovation costs of regulation" perspective. Importance 2 — opinion piece without empirical grounding.
