## Problem

Faculty members face multiple barriers when integrating ChatGPT into university curricula but also perceive numerous enablers. Systematic documentation was lacking.

## Key idea

Phenomenological qualitative study with experienced Iranian university faculty. Identifies two categories: executive challenges (8 obstacle types: content, cognitive, psychological, technical, human, communicational, structural, strategic) and enablers (9 enabler types across similar dimensions).

## Method

Qualitative phenomenological design, criterion-based purposive sampling, semi-structured interviews until saturation, Claysey's seven-step thematic analysis. Iranian universities.

## Results

8 obstacle categories and 9 enabler categories identified. Most prominent barriers include technical limitations, content accuracy concerns, and structural/institutional resistance. Enablers include pedagogical benefits, organizational support, and cognitive advantages for students.

## Limitations

Single country (Iran), faculty perspective only (no student data), no empirical outcome measures, qualitative saturation-based approach limits generalizability.

## Open questions

- How do faculty-reported obstacles map to actual learning outcome impacts?
- Which obstacle categories are most amenable to institutional intervention?

## My take

Adds the faculty-side, non-Western perspective to ChatGPT adoption literature. The obstacle taxonomy is usefully granular and complements student-centered studies.
