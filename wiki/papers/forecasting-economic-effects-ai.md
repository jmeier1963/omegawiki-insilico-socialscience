## Problem

There is widespread expert disagreement about AI's economic impact. Systematic evidence on what top economists and AI experts actually predict — and why they disagree — is lacking.

## Key idea

Large-scale expert elicitation of AI economic forecasts: 69 leading economists, 52 AI industry/policy experts, 38 superforecasters, 401 general public. Tracks predictions about AI progress scenarios for 2030 and their economic consequences (GDP, TFP, labor force participation, wealth inequality).

## Method

Survey/forecasting study. Respondents assign probabilities to four AI progress scenarios for 2030 (stagnation, slow, moderate, rapid) and predict economic indicators under each scenario. Collaborative effort: Forecasting Research Institute, Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago, Yale School of Management, Stanford University, University of Pennsylvania.

## Results

- 47% mean probability: moderate AI progress by 2030 (semi-autonomous research labs, 5-day coding tasks, competent robots)
- 14% mean probability: rapid AI progress by 2030 (AI outperforms humans at most tasks, CEO-level management)
- Combined 61% probability for moderate or rapid progress — economists are moderately optimistic on capabilities
- Despite capability optimism, economists predict GDP/TFP/labor force participation will remain near historical trends (GDP growth near-normal even under moderate progress)
- Rapid progress scenario: large GDP increase, large wealth inequality increase, large labor force participation decrease
- Policy finding: job retraining programs could add ~1pp to labor force participation in rapid-progress scenario

## Limitations

- Survey of experts — subject to anchoring, social desirability, strategic framing
- Scenarios may not capture the full range of AI development pathways
- "Moderate" and "rapid" scenario definitions may bias predictions toward AI optimism

## Open questions

- Why do economists predict high AI capability but low macroeconomic impact? Is this consistent?
- How does the timeline to capability thresholds affect economic outcomes?

## My take

Systematic expert elicitation is valuable for understanding AI economic uncertainty. The disconnect between high capability expectations and low macroeconomic projections is the key finding — economists may be underestimating complementarity or overestimating adaptation costs.
