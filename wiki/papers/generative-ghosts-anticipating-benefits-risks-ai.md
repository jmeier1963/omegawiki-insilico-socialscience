## Problem

Generative AI is enabling the creation of agents that simulate specific individuals — including deceased people ("grief bots"). A boom in startups offering such services has outpaced academic analysis of the design space, benefits, and risks of these systems.

## Key idea

**Generative Ghost**: an AI agent trained on data from a specific person (text, voice, image) that can generate novel responses in that person's style after their death. Distinct from "parrot" systems that only replay recorded content. The paper: (1) introduces the "generative ghost" concept and design space, (2) analyzes benefits (grief support, legacy preservation, knowledge continuation) and risks (psychological harm, consent violations, manipulation, misinformation), and (3) proposes a research agenda for responsible development.

## Method

- Historical review of digital afterlife technologies (pre-AI photo albums to AI grief bots)
- Design space analysis: taxonomy of implementation approaches (training data types, access controls, agency levels)
- Ethical analysis framework covering consent, dignity, psychological impact, misuse potential
- Literature review of HCI, philosophy, and ethics scholarship on death and digital legacy

## Results

- Identifies several categories of potential benefits: grief support, legacy preservation, training/research applications, knowledge continuation from deceased experts
- Identifies several categories of risks: psychological harm (impeding grief), consent and dignity violations, privacy breaches, misuse for misinformation or manipulation
- No empirical study — design and ethics contribution

## Limitations

- Primarily speculative/prospective — no user study of actual generative ghost interactions
- Design space may evolve rapidly as technology advances
- Western-centric ethical framework may not generalize to all cultural contexts around death and legacy

## Open questions

- How do people who interact with generative ghosts experience grief compared to those who do not?
- What consent mechanisms are sufficient for posthumous AI agent creation?
- Should generative ghosts be regulated as products or services, and by whom?

## My take

An important early position paper on a real and growing phenomenon. The "generative ghost" framing is clear and useful. The paper's main contribution is the design space taxonomy and ethical framework, not empirical findings. Will become more important as the technology matures. Importance: 3 — well-placed at CHI, useful conceptual contribution.
