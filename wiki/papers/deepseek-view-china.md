## Problem

DeepSeek-V3 and R1's release in late 2024 caused global market shock (Nvidia stock drop). How did the Chinese AI community respond? What are the strategic implications for AI competition?

## Key idea

Translation and analysis of Chinese AI community response to DeepSeek from a closed-door session (January 26, 2025). Core conclusion from Chinese insiders: compute demand remains strong and no company has enough compute — efficiency gains (DeepSeek) stimulate rather than reduce total demand (Jevons Paradox logic).

## Method

ChinaTalk newsletter synthesis of translated insights from a VC-hosted closed-door session with dozens of Chinese AI researchers, investors, and industry insiders. Published January 28, 2025.

## Results

- Before December 2024, DeepSeek was barely known in China's AI community; V3/R1 changed this
- Chinese community reaction: DeepSeek proved Chinese labs can compete technically despite export controls
- Key conclusion: efficiency gains don't reduce compute demand — cheaper models create more demand (Jevons Paradox)
- Compute constraints remain the central strategic challenge for Chinese AI development
- DeepSeek showed that intelligence can be derived from efficiency rather than scale alone

## Limitations

- Based on translated insider session; may not represent full range of Chinese views
- ChinaTalk has editorial perspective; selection bias in what was translated/published

## Open questions

- Does DeepSeek-style efficiency generalize, or is it specific to DeepSeek's training approach?
- How does export-control pressure on Chinese compute shape future AI development trajectories?

## My take

Important for understanding Chinese AI community dynamics and compute strategy. The Jevons Paradox argument about compute demand is independently confirmed by Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella (same reasoning in the Tan & Thelen paper). Importance 2 (newsletter analysis; valuable but not peer-reviewed).
