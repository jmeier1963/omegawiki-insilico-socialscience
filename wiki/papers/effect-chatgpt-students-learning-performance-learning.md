## Problem

Conflicting findings across individual studies make it unclear whether ChatGPT meaningfully improves student learning. A meta-analysis synthesizing available evidence is needed to establish the overall effect size and identify moderating conditions.

## Key idea

**ChatGPT has a large positive effect on learning performance (g=0.867)**: Meta-analysis of 51 studies (Nov 2022–Feb 2025) finds that ChatGPT produces a large positive effect on learning performance (Hedges' g=0.867), moderate positive effects on learning perception (g=0.456) and higher-order thinking (g=0.457). Effects are moderated by course type, learning model, and intervention duration. Problem-based learning and durations of 4–8 weeks produce the strongest effects.

## Method

- Systematic review and meta-analysis: 51 studies, Nov 2022–Feb 2025
- Effect measure: Hedges' g (corrected for small sample bias)
- Outcomes: learning performance, learning perception, higher-order thinking
- Moderator analysis: QB statistic for subgroup differences
- Moderators tested: grade level, course type, duration, learning model, ChatGPT role, application area
- Authors: Hangzhou Normal University, China

## Results

- Learning performance: g=0.867 (large effect), p<0.001
- Learning perception: g=0.456 (moderate effect), p<0.001
- Higher-order thinking: g=0.457 (moderate effect), p<0.001
- Significant moderators for learning performance: course type (QB=64.249, p<0.001), learning model (QB=76.220, p<0.001), duration (QB=55.998, p<0.001)
- Problem-based learning consistently associated with the strongest effects
- Duration 4–8 weeks recommended for stable effects (vs. ≤1 week or >8 weeks which showed more variance)

## Limitations

- Limited to studies published 2022–2025; likely publication bias toward positive findings
- Most studies used college-level STEM or language learning contexts; generalizability to other settings uncertain
- Heterogeneity across individual study effect sizes is high (values range from g≈0.04 to g>2.0)
- Few studies on lower grade levels
- "ChatGPT" as a category is broad; model versions vary substantially

## Open questions

- Do effects persist beyond the study period? Are there long-term learning gains?
- How do effects vary across different ChatGPT/LLM versions as capability improves?
- What specific pedagogical designs maximize benefits for higher-order thinking?
- What are the effects on academic integrity and dependency?

## My take

Useful aggregation of the first wave of ChatGPT-in-education research. The large effect on learning performance (g=0.867) is striking — larger than most educational technology interventions. Key caveat: high inter-study heterogeneity suggests effects are highly context-dependent. Importance 4 — Nature-family publication, strong evidence for AI education policy discussions.
