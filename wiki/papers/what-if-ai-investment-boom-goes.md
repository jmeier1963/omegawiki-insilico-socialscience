## Problem

Given the scale of AI investment ($3 trillion forecast by 2028 for data centers alone), what happens if the technology fails to deliver sufficient economic returns?

## Key idea

Economist leaders article analyzing the risk of the AI investment boom — already among the largest in modern history — failing to generate adequate returns. US large tech firms spending ~$400bn annually on AI infrastructure. OpenAI and Anthropic combined valuation approaching $500bn. Article asks: even if AI achieves its potential, many investors will lose money; and if it doesn't, losses will be staggering. Three risk scenarios: technology disappoints, technology succeeds but value accrues to users not investors, concentration among a few winners leaves most losers.

## Method

Editorial analysis, The Economist. 5 pages.

## Results

- Scale: ~$400bn/year US tech AI infrastructure spend; global data center spend forecasted to exceed $3trn by 2028
- Risk scenarios: (1) technology underdelivers, (2) technology delivers but competition eliminates margins, (3) concentration makes most investments worthless
- Historical parallel: internet investment boom/bust; long-run transformative, short-run destructive for many investors
- Productivity paradox: economists expect gains eventually, but timing uncertain (see: [[economists-expect-ai-capability-gains-without]])
- Policy implication: investment boom will proceed regardless; question is whether governance can manage systemic risk

## Limitations

- Editorial analysis; no empirical forecasting
- Framing as "boom going wrong" may understate genuine transformation

## My take

Clear Economist framing of the AI investment risk. The three failure modes are analytically useful. Connects to productivity paradox debate. Importance 2 (editorial analysis; useful for investment risk framing).
