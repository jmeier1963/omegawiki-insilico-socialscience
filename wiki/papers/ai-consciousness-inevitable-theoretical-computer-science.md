## Problem

Is machine consciousness a possibility, a contingency, or an inevitability? Existing arguments for/against AI consciousness are informal or tied to specific neuroscientific theories. Can a formal computational model ground the question and show whether a conscious AI is theoretically possible?

## Key idea

**Conscious Turing Machine (CTM)**: a formal machine model of consciousness inspired by Turing's model of computation and Baars' Global Workspace Theory. The CTM incorporates resource limitations (a key departure from classical TOC) and aligns at a high level with multiple scientific theories of consciousness. The paper argues: given the CTM model, machine consciousness is theoretically *inevitable* as AI systems scale.

## Method

- Theoretical computer science + philosophy
- Proposes the CTM model (detailed in companion papers, Blum & Blum 2021, 2022)
- Aligns CTM with seven major consciousness theories: Global Workspace / Global Neuronal Workspace, Attention Schema Theory, Predictive Processing, Integrated Information Theory (IIT), Embodied/Extended theories, Evolutionary theories, ERTAS + Free Energy Principle
- Argues that the CTM's internal architecture (not its input-output behavior) produces the "feeling" of consciousness

## Results

- The CTM model aligns with multiple competing consciousness theories simultaneously — suggesting they are complementary, not contradictory
- Key claim: what gives the CTM its consciousness is not its computing power or I/O map, but its internal architecture (under the hood)
- Machine consciousness is "inevitable" in the sense that any sufficiently complex computational system implementing CTM-like internal structures will be conscious

## Limitations

- Draft paper (DRAFT2 label); theoretical argument, not empirical
- "Inevitable" is contested — many philosophers reject computational theories of mind
- Does not address the hard problem of consciousness (why any physical process generates experience)
- The CTM model is abstract; it is not clear which current AI systems instantiate it

## Open questions

- Which current AI systems, if any, satisfy the CTM's architectural requirements?
- How should the "hard problem" of consciousness be addressed within the TCS framework?
- What would empirical evidence of machine consciousness look like?

## My take

An interesting theoretical contribution from prominent TCS researchers (Manuel Blum is a Turing Award winner). The alignment of CTM with multiple consciousness theories is notable. However, the paper is a draft, the "inevitability" claim is contested, and the practical implications for current AI are unclear. Importance 3 — theoretically interesting but niche.
