## Problem

Generative AI makes traditional written assignments trivially cheatable, creating an academic integrity crisis in higher education.

## Key idea

NYT guest essay by Clay Shirky (vice provost at NYU) arguing that the only viable long-term solution to AI academic dishonesty is to redesign assignments so that AI assistance is either permitted and integrated, or that the task itself requires in-person, real-time human performance that AI cannot complete on a student's behalf.

## Method

Opinion essay. Published August 26, 2025.

## Results

Proposes a binary resolution: either (1) redesign assessments to explicitly incorporate and teach AI use, or (2) move to in-person, proctored, real-time assessments. Policing AI use is dismissed as unscalable.

## Limitations

Opinion, no empirical study.

## Open questions

- What is the cost/feasibility tradeoff of moving to fully proctored in-person assessment at scale?

## My take

Practical, pragmatic framing from an administrator who has dealt with digital integrity issues since 2015. Complements the Bieger & Kolmar framework with an institutional-action perspective.
