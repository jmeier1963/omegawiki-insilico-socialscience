## Problem

Existing frameworks in human-AI interaction cannot explain how AI systems reshape human cognition *before* conscious awareness — in the pre-conscious preprocessing layer. This blind spot prevents understanding how distributed cognition is being restructured at individual, collective, and cultural scales.

## Key idea

AI systems (search engines, recommender systems, LLMs) function as *cognitive infrastructures* — foundational, often invisible systems that condition what is knowable and actionable in digital societies. They are not merely tools but semantic infrastructures that automate *relevance judgment*, shifting the *locus of epistemic agency* to non-human systems. The paper proposes "Cognitive Infrastructure Studies" (CIS) as a new interdisciplinary domain to study this transformation.

## Method

Conceptual framework paper; no empirical study. Draws on:
- Infrastructure theory (Bowker & Star, Star & Ruhleder)
- "System 0" concept (Chiriatti et al. 2024, *Nature Human Behaviour*): AI as a pre-conscious layer of distributed cognition, distinct from Kahneman's System 1/2
- Narrative scenarios at individual, collective, and societal scales
- Methodological innovation: "infrastructure breakdown methodologies" — experimentally withdrawing AI preprocessing after habituation to reveal cognitive dependencies

Distinguishes cognitive infrastructures from physical infrastructures: they have *agency* (semantic filtering), operate through *anticipatory personalization*, and exhibit *adaptive invisibility* (become more seamlessly integrated as they improve).

## Results

Three key properties of cognitive infrastructures:
1. **Adaptive invisibility**: sophisticated systems integrate seamlessly, making influence harder to detect
2. **Anticipatory personalization**: continuous behavioral learning predicts and shapes future cognitive states
3. **Relevance judgment automation**: what is worth attending to is increasingly decided by non-human systems

Three scales of impact (via narrative scenarios):
- Individual: cognitive dependency, identity reshaping, reduced autonomous thought
- Collective: democratic deliberation reshaped by filtered information environments
- Societal: governance systems operating on pre-selected information landscapes

CIS addresses gaps that no single discipline can fill: cognitive science lacks population-scale preprocessing analysis; digital sociology cannot access individual cognitive mechanisms; computational approaches miss cultural transmission dynamics.

## Limitations

- Primarily conceptual; narrative scenarios are illustrative, not empirical
- "Cognitive Infrastructure Studies" as a new field is aspirational — no established methods or results yet
- Infrastructure breakdown methodology is proposed but not implemented
- Does not engage with technical mechanisms of how LLMs specifically implement relevance judgment

## Open questions

- What are the measurable signatures of cognitive dependency on AI preprocessing?
- Can infrastructure breakdown experiments reveal the extent of pre-conscious AI influence on reasoning?
- How does adaptive invisibility scale with model capability — do more capable models become more or less detectable?

## My take

Conceptually rich paper that provides a useful reframing: AI is not a tool you use but an infrastructure you think through. The "System 0" extension of Kahneman's framework is provocative and the adaptive invisibility / relevance judgment automation concepts are well-named. The paper is better at diagnosis than prescription — CIS as a field proposal is underdeveloped, and the infrastructure breakdown methodology is more a research agenda than an implementable design. Relevant to the wiki's core concern about AI's effects on scientific reasoning and epistemic formation.
