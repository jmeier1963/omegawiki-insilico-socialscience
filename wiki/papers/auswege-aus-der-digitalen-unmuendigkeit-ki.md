## Problem

AI adoption in German universities threatens the epistemic and institutional autonomy of higher education — universities become dependent on external tech platforms and their algorithmic choices.

## Key idea

FAZ opinion piece by Amrei Bahr and Maximilian Mayer arguing that AI adoption in German universities risks "digital Unmündigkeit" (digital immaturity/heteronomy) — a Kantian framing invoking the inability to use one's own understanding without the guidance of another. Universities cede epistemic authority to AI systems.

## Method

Newspaper opinion essay (Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung, January 10, 2024).

## Results

Normative argument for university digital sovereignty: universities should critically select and govern AI tools rather than passively adopting commercial platforms. Kantian Aufklärung framing: digital maturity requires independent epistemic judgment.

## Limitations

Opinion piece, no empirical data.

## My take

The "digitale Unmündigkeit" framing (Kant's Sapere aude applied to AI adoption) is intellectually rich and underused in English-language education-AI discourse. Links naturally to Suchanek's consensus argument and the human flourishing framework.
