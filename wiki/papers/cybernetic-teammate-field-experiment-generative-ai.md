## Problem & Context

AI's effect on knowledge work is usually studied at the individual level, but real work is collaborative. This paper (Dell'Acqua, Ayoubi, Lifshitz, Sadun, E. Mollick, L. Mollick, Han, Goldman, Nair, Taub, Lakhani) asks how AI transforms the three core pillars of collaboration — performance, expertise sharing, and social engagement — via a pre-registered field experiment at Procter & Gamble.

## Key idea

AI acts as a "cybernetic teammate": it doesn't just boost individual output but replicates specific benefits of human collaboration — matching team-level performance, bridging functional expertise silos, and partially filling the social/motivational role a human teammate provides.

## Method

Pre-registered 2×2 field experiment: 776 P&G professionals on real product-innovation challenges, randomly assigned to work with or without AI, and individually or in a two-person new-product-development team. Outcomes: solution quality/performance, whether proposals leaned technical (R&D) vs. commercial, and self-reported emotional responses.

## Experiment & Results

- **Performance:** individuals with AI matched the performance of teams without AI — AI replicates collaboration's performance benefit.
- **Silo-breaking:** without AI, R&D professionals proposed more technical solutions and Commercial professionals more commercial ones; with AI, both produced balanced solutions regardless of background.
- **Social role:** AI's language interface elicited more positive self-reported emotional responses, suggesting it partly fills the motivational role of a human teammate.

## Limitations

- Single firm (P&G) and task domain (product innovation); external validity uncertain.
- Short-run experiment; no long-term skill or team-dynamics effects.
- Self-reported emotional responses; funded in part by P&G (disclosed).
- Working paper, not peer-reviewed.

## Open questions

- Does AI-as-teammate erode the human expertise it currently substitutes for over time?
- If individuals+AI match teams, how should organizations restructure collaborative work?
- Does the silo-breaking effect persist or homogenize solutions harmfully?

## My take

The headline that individual+AI matches team-without-AI is the crisp, org-design-relevant result: it says AI substitutes for a specific coordination benefit of teams, not just for effort. The silo-breaking finding is subtler and arguably more important — AI pulls specialists toward balanced solutions, which is good for integration but raises a homogenization question. The emotional-response result gestures at AI absorbing the social function of teammates, which is the part with the least obvious long-run consequences.
