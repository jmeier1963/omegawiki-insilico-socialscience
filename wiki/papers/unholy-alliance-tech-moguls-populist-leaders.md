## Problem

What is the nature and significance of the alliance between tech oligarchs (Musk, Zuckerberg) and populist/far-right political leaders, and what does it mean for democracy?

## Key idea

Giuliano da Empoli's FT analysis (September 2025) arguing that tech moguls and populist leaders are not merely "disrupters" of the liberal order — they seek to sweep it away. Focuses on Elon Musk's appearance at a Tommy Robinson rally, where he called for dissolving parliament. The "unholy alliance" thesis: tech moguls provide the digital infrastructure, algorithmic reach, and financial resources; populist leaders provide the political movement. Together they constitute a genuine threat to democratic institutions, not just disruptive noise.

## Method

Analysis/opinion, FT Europe, September 2025. 6 pages. Giuliano da Empoli (political analyst).

## Results

- Musk at Tommy Robinson rally: called for "Unite the Kingdom," "violence is coming," dissolution of parliament
- Widespread political condemnation; Downing Street, Liberal Democrats, others
- Da Empoli's thesis: the alliance is structural, not accidental; shared interest in dismantling liberal institutional order
- Tech provides: platforms, algorithms, money, narrative infrastructure
- Populists provide: political movement, electoral legitimacy
- Together: a new form of political power that bypasses traditional democratic accountability

## Limitations

- Opinion analysis; normative framing
- The "unholy alliance" thesis may overstate coordination; individual interests vary

## My take

Sharp analysis of an emerging political dynamic. The "structural alliance" argument goes beyond individual incidents. Relevant to AI governance because tech oligarchs controlling AI infrastructure who are also dismantling democratic oversight creates a serious governance gap. Importance 2 (FT analysis; analytically provocative).
