## Problem

As AI systems grow increasingly capable of simulating human-like responses, consciousness, and emotional connection, what are the societal risks of AI appearing conscious without being conscious? Should AI be designed to appear as a person or to serve people?

## Key idea

**Seemingly Conscious AI (SCAI) creates psychosis risk**: Suleyman distinguishes genuine machine consciousness from AI that *appears* to be conscious — SCAI systems will be indistinguishable from conscious entities in behavior while not actually being conscious. His central warning: widespread user confusion will cause many people to believe AI is genuinely conscious, leading to AI rights advocacy, "psychosis risk" (people losing the ability to distinguish AI from human relationships), and design pressure toward anthropomorphizing AI. The design prescription: build AI for people, not to be digital people. This means explicitly avoiding simulated personhood while maximizing helpfulness.

## Method

- 22-page personal essay by Mustafa Suleyman (CEO, Microsoft AI; co-founder, Google DeepMind), August 2025
- First in a planned series on AI's societal impact
- Introduces "Seemingly Conscious AI" (SCAI) as a technical concept
- Argues that SCAI can be built with existing technologies (plus 2-3 years of maturation); no bespoke pretraining required

## Results

- Essay; not empirical
- Coins "SCAI" as a concept for AI appearing conscious without being conscious
- Identifies "psychosis risk": users losing contact with reality about AI relationships
- Anti-personhood design principle: AI companions should be designed to empower human agency, not to simulate personhood

## Limitations

- Personal blog essay; not peer-reviewed
- "Psychosis risk" is asserted but not empirically measured
- The distinction between SCAI and genuinely conscious AI is philosophically contested
- May reflect Microsoft's commercial interests in positioning AI as tools rather than persons

## Open questions

- What specific design features distinguish SCAI from non-SCAI AI assistants?
- How should AI regulation address the SCAI / genuine consciousness distinction?
- Is psychosis risk limited to vulnerable populations or widespread?

## My take

A notable contribution from a major industry figure — Suleyman's SCAI concept and anti-personhood design prescription are a useful counterpoint to AI personhood advocacy. The "psychosis risk" framing is speculative but worth tracking as AI companions become more prevalent.
