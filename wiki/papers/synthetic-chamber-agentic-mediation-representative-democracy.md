## Problem

Representative democracy is strained by the decline of political parties — the institutions that once aggregated interests, organised conflict, and translated demands into programmes. As parties hollow out, the *mediation* function (connecting citizens, public reasoning, and legislative politics) has been displaced into proprietary systems: polling, segmentation, recommendation engines, campaign analytics, and conversational AI. The authors argue the central democratic question raised by AI is not institutional efficiency but how representative systems can regain mechanisms for articulating, testing, and answering political claims.

## Key idea

Propose the **synthetic chamber**: a standing parliamentary organ of opinion-formation and reason-structuring that acts as a public counter-institution to the privatisation of democratic mediation. It uses multi-agent AI not to replace human judgement or simulate a unified popular will, but to **structure, preserve, and relay** the reasoned outputs of organised citizen deliberation (assemblies, mini-publics) into parliament — "auxiliary in power but constitutional in effect."

## Method

Conceptual/constitutional design, grounded in literatures on party decline, deliberative democracy, and representation. The architecture is extrapolated from a research-creation setting that made *plurality, conflict, and traceability* operable as AI deliberation: the chamber receives outputs from recurring deliberative forums, converts publicly articulated reasons into a new "parliamentary object," and organises reason-giving as a property of the arrangement rather than of individual speakers.

## Results

Design properties the synthetic chamber is meant to guarantee:
- **Preserve disagreement** — does not collapse plurality into a single will.
- **Compel answerability** — reasons must be processed and answered, not lost when a deliberation event ends.
- **Keep processing contestable** — algorithmic mediation remains open to challenge (traceability).
It targets the *internal* hollowing of parties rather than the "external shock" view of platforms eroding democracy from outside.

## Limitations

- Purely conceptual; no implementation or empirical test of the AI mediation pipeline.
- Risks of capture, manipulation, or false neutrality in the AI structuring of reasons are acknowledged but not resolved.
- Feasibility of constitutionalising such an organ is left open.

## Open questions

- How to technically guarantee traceability and contestability in multi-agent reason-structuring?
- How to prevent the synthetic chamber from becoming another privatised mediation layer?
- What legitimacy criteria should govern AI-mediated deliberative outputs?

## My take

A serious political-theory counterpoint to "AI for civic efficiency" framings: it locates AI's democratic value in *mediation* (answerability, preserved disagreement) rather than preference aggregation or simulation. Complements the wiki's deliberation/influence work by supplying an institutional-design target, and connects to debates on whether AI should simulate publics vs. structure real ones.
