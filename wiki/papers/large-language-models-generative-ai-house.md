## Problem

Large language models and generative AI are rapidly being adopted across UK society and economy. Parliament's Communications and Digital Committee assessed what is known, what is uncertain, and what regulatory frameworks are needed.

## Key idea

Comprehensive parliamentary inquiry report on LLMs and generative AI (published February 2024). Reviews capabilities, risks, and governance implications. Makes specific recommendations to government on AI regulation, safety research, and economic opportunity.

## Method

Parliamentary inquiry: oral and written evidence from AI experts, industry, civil society. 95-page report from House of Lords Communications and Digital Committee (Session 2023–24, HL Paper 54).

## Results

- LLMs represent a qualitative advance requiring updated regulatory thinking
- UK should not regulate specific technologies but capabilities and risks
- Need for mandatory incident reporting, model evaluation requirements, compute thresholds
- UK has opportunity to build AI Safety Institute capacity into global governance
- Risks highlighted: misinformation, economic displacement, IP/copyright, safety failures
- Economic opportunity: productivity gains, health, climate applications

## Limitations

- Parliamentary reports reflect committee views; implementation depends on government
- Report predates rapid 2024 capability advances (GPT-4o, Claude 3, Gemini 1.5)
- UK-specific context; does not fully engage with EU AI Act alignment

## Open questions

- How should incident reporting work for closed-weight models?
- How does UK AI Safety Institute coordinate with international equivalents?

## My take

Well-regarded parliamentary report with balanced evidence base. The House of Lords inquiry process brings in diverse expert views. Useful reference for UK AI policy debates. Importance 3 (influential official report).
