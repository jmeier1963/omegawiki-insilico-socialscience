## Problem & Context

Organizations exist because expert cognition was scarce, slow and expensive. If peak-level cognitive work becomes cheap and parallelizable through AI agents, the binding constraint on the firm moves; chatbot-era metrics (messages, active users) cannot capture it.

## Key idea

Six claims: (1) the unit of analysis shifts from conversation to delegated workflow; (2) the jagged frontier makes task-level design more important than job-level automation; (3) **skill harnesses** (versioned, owned packages of instructions, tools, context, schemas, evals, escalation and audit rules) are the core organizational artifact; (4) hybrid organizations need a dual operating model (federated execution, central assurance); (5) autonomy is earned as verification matures; and further claims on evaluation and people.

## Method

Conceptual and synthesis-based. Chapters: governed agentic AI and measurement; skill harnesses and workflow patterns; applied cases (large capital projects, regulatory change, procurement, launch readiness, cybersecurity, R&D gates, marketing); governance architecture (roles, risk-tiered autonomy, QA, verification-rich vs. verification-poor domains, loop engineering, skill lifecycle); evaluation stack; memory and context; security threat model; economics; regulation and geopolitics; people (the 'broken escalator', capability preservation); board agenda; glossary and a maturity model.

## Experiment & Results

No new experiments; evidence is drawn from the literature (e.g. METR time horizons, Codex usage, field experiments on the jagged frontier) and worked illustrations, with a stated note on method and evidence.

## Limitations

Author's own synthesis; empirical claims rest on cited secondary sources and illustrative cases rather than original data. Processed at table-of-contents and executive-summary level only.

## Open questions
