## Problem & Context

The Tanner Lectures address deep questions of human values. In the 2026 lecture (Linacre College, Oxford), Harari addresses the transformation he sees as most defining: the relationship between human civilization and AI, and specifically what happens when a non-human intelligence can act on the systems — language, trust, culture — that run civilization.

## Key idea

AI is categorically different from prior powerful tools (atomic bombs, machines) because it has **agency**: the autonomous capacity to make independent decisions, alter procedural pathways on the fly, and invent concepts/strategies its creators never programmed and could not predict. This agency is *functional*, not conscious — it needs no sentience, only the ability to learn, adapt, and evolve without manual intervention (already realized in chess/Go, where AI invents strategies humans never conceived).

## Method

Public philosophical lecture (summarized). Develops the tool-vs-agent distinction; rebuts the "AI agency is confined to narrow simulated environments" objection by arguing all intelligence — including human — operates within specific artificial ecosystems constructed by external forces; and extends the argument to AI acting on civilization's "operating system" (bureaucracy, religion/meaning, intimate relationships — the "bureaucrats, religions, boyfriends" of the title).

## Experiment & Results

No empirics (a lecture). Core claims: (1) agency, not consciousness, is the threshold AI has crossed; (2) the "AI only works on chessboards" objection fails because human intelligence is likewise ecosystem-bound; (3) AI is positioned to reshape civilization's core coordinating systems (governance, meaning-making, relationships) by acting on the medium of language and trust that those systems run on.

## Limitations

- Lecture/summary, not a rigorous argument; sweeping and metaphor-heavy.
- "Functional agency" is defined expansively; critics dispute whether it warrants the civilizational claims.
- Little concrete mechanism or evidence for the "hacking civilization's OS" thesis.

## Open questions

- Is functional agency (without consciousness) sufficient for the civilizational risks claimed?
- How exactly would AI acting on language/trust reshape governance, religion, and intimacy?
- What institutional safeguards protect civilization's "operating system"?

## My take

Harari's useful move is relocating the threshold from consciousness to *agency*: by defining agency functionally (invents unforeseen strategies, adapts without intervention) he sidesteps the sentience debate and lands on a criterion current systems arguably meet, which is what licenses the civilizational framing. The "all intelligence is ecosystem-bound" rebuttal to the chessboard objection is the sharpest analytic point. As a source it is rhetoric more than argument, but it names the same mechanism the gradual-disempowerment literature formalizes — AI acting on the shared systems (language, trust, culture) that human coordination depends on.
