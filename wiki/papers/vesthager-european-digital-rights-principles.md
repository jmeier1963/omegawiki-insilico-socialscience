## Problem

What should a European Declaration on Digital Rights and Principles achieve, and what values should it enshrine?

## Key idea

Opinion piece by Margrethe Vestager (then EU Commission Executive VP for Digital) published in Der Tagesspiegel. Argues for a European digital rights declaration that "connects rather than divides" — affirming European values of data protection, network access, digital competence, carbon footprint awareness, and online/offline equivalence of rights. Frames European digital governance as expressing European identity and democratic values.

## Method

Opinion essay. 2 pages (newspaper op-ed format). German language.

## Results

- European digital declaration should affirm: affordable network access, digital skills, data control rights, CO2 transparency for digital consumption, online safety equivalent to offline
- Framing: "how we treat the digital world says a lot about who we are"
- European approach: rights-based rather than market-led or state-directed

## Limitations

- Short opinion piece; no analytical depth
- Vestager's perspective as EU executive reflects institutional interests
- German text; limited accessibility for non-German readers

## My take

Brief statement of European digital rights philosophy from a key EU digital policy figure. Useful as primary source evidence of European values-based approach to digital governance, contrasted with US market-led and Chinese state-directed approaches. Importance 2 (op-ed from senior EU official; brief but historically relevant).
