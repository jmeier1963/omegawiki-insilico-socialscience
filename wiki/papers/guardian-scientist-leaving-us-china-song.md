## Problem

Why do prominent AI scientists leave US institutions for China, and what does this reveal about the global AI talent competition?

## Key idea

Guardian longform (September 2025) by Chang Che on Song-Chun Zhu — one of the world's most cited computer vision/AI researchers — who left UCLA to return to China in 2020, founding the Beijing Institute for General Artificial Intelligence (BIGAI). Zhu's story: born during Cultural Revolution, spent half his life in the US, then took a one-way ticket to Beijing. His reasoning: China offers scale, resources, government support, and a chance to shape AI development at the national level. His potential to determine "who wins the global AI race."

## Method

Longform journalism. 13 pages. Chang Che (Guardian). Based on extensive interviews with Zhu.

## Results

- Song-Chun Zhu: highly cited AI/CV researcher; former UCLA professor; founded BIGAI (Beijing Institute for General Artificial Intelligence)
- Reasons for return: scale of Chinese AI investment, government support, ability to pursue AGI without commercial constraints
- BIGAI mission: general artificial intelligence research with Chinese characteristics
- Implications: US AI talent pipeline is leaking to China at senior level; not just juniors seeking work but eminent researchers choosing China
- Counter-narrative: not about espionage but about research opportunity and national identity

## Limitations

- Longform journalism; profile of one individual; limited generalizability
- Zhu's case may be exceptional (cultural ties, specific career trajectory)

## Open questions

- How many senior AI researchers are considering similar moves?
- What US policy responses could address senior researcher outmigration?

## My take

Compelling profile of a significant talent migration story. The "I have to do it" framing suggests genuine conviction, not mercenary motivation. Important for understanding AI talent dynamics as geopolitical factor. Importance 2 (journalism; individually important case study).
