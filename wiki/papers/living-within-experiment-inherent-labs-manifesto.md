## Problem

Scientific progress has slowed (rising cost per drug discovery, more scientists per breakthrough, older Nobel laureates) while legacy research institutions and workflows constrain how AI can be deployed. Using AI within traditional labs leaves breakthrough potential unrealized and creates unnecessary ethical risks. Current AI excels at well-specified questions but lacks open-ended scientific curiosity and aesthetic judgment over hypotheses.

## Key idea

**Inherent Labs** proposes a new research institution built around **recursive collective self-improvement (RSI)**: applying RSI not to individual agents but to the entire lab — models, infrastructure, data, resource allocation, and human–machine teaming co-evolve. The manifesto argues this requires **AI-native organizational redesign** (analogous to factory electrification replacing steam-driven vertical layouts) rather than bolting AI onto high-modernist institutions.

Core principles:

- AI should **augment** science via collective human–machine intelligence, not automate it away.
- Inventive AI needs **scientific intuition** and taste — knowing which questions to ask, not only answering specified ones.
- RSI at the institution level binds ethics and efficacy by keeping humans "living within the experiment."
- Incorporated as a **Public Benefit Corporation** with a planned societal-benefit board.

## Method

Vision document (manifesto), not empirical study. Three sections:

1. **Reinventing the Factory** — historical analogy (1890→1925 factory electrification); argues AI-driven discovery needs comparable process redesign; PBC governance structure.
2. **A New Aesthetic of Science** — generative design example (75% material reduction joint); Enlightenment scientific method vs. slowing progress; foundation models + in silico/physical lab automation; open-ended research agents vs. narrow metric optimization.
3. **Recursive Collective Self-Improvement** — cultural evolution as archetypal RSI; lab-level compounding across discussions, allocation, hardware, data, algorithms; human–machine teaming as dual thinking modes; open research questions on curiosity, benchmarks, alignment, fairness.

## Results

No empirical results — programmatic claims and research agenda. Announces:

- Mission to equip global R&D teams with AI peers for creativity, cooperation, and acceleration.
- Development of general-purpose inventive AI and next-generation autonomous labs.
- Societal-benefit board membership announcement within six months (from document date).
- Open application for joining "the experiment."

## Limitations

- Not peer-reviewed; no citations, benchmarks, or reproducible evidence.
- RSI at collective level is asserted as "meta-solution" without operational definition or safety guarantees.
- Factory analogy is illustrative; transfer to science organizations is speculative.
- "Scientific intuition" and "AI taste" remain undefined.
- Conflicts of interest: authors are founding the institution they advocate for.
- PDF is a web capture (inherentlabs.ai, dated 02.06.26) with minor OCR/encoding artifacts.

## Open questions

- What does rigorous benchmarking look like for institution-level RSI?
- How to train agents that follow their own curiosity without reward-hacking?
- What initial conditions yield collective-level RSI?
- How to preserve interpretability and human joy in discovery while accelerating counter-intuitive findings?
- What is the right interface for many-humans–many-agents research collaboration?
- How to recursively improve alignment and fairness of collective decision-making?

## My take

Useful as an **organizational counterpoint** to fully autonomous pipelines (AI Scientist, Agent Laboratory): the emphasis on institutional co-evolution, human agency, and open-ended hypothesis exploration addresses real gaps in automation-first narratives. Weak as evidence — it is fundraising/vision prose. The cultural-evolution framing connects cleanly to [[machine-culture]]; the collective RSI variant extends but does not duplicate [[software-intelligence-explosion]]'s software-capability feedback loop.
