## Problem

What does AI use in schools actually do to students' cognitive development — and can AI be used as a "learning turbo" without causing cognitive damage?

## Key idea

Der Spiegel (April 18, 2026) long-form investigation by Claus Hecking examining AI's dual potential in German schools: as a powerful learning accelerator if used correctly, but also as a cognitive off-loader that can impede genuine learning and brain development if used passively.

## Method

Investigative journalism with expert interviews (educators, neuroscientists, pedagogical researchers).

## Results

Expert consensus: AI in schools has significant potential if actively and critically engaged; passive use risks cognitive atrophy, especially for foundational skills (reading comprehension, mathematical reasoning, writing). Neural plasticity concerns for developing brains.

## Limitations

Journalistic, not a systematic review.

## My take

The neurological angle (effect on developing brains) adds an empirical dimension to the cognitive debt debate. The "Lernturbo vs. Gehirnschaden" framing is characteristically Spiegel but captures a real tension.
