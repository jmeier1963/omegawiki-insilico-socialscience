## Problem

Human cultural evolution — variation, transmission, and selection of cultural traits — has been studied across anthropology, psychology, economics, and biology. Intelligent machines (AI systems) are now intervening in all three Darwinian properties simultaneously, but there is no unified conceptual framework for understanding how recommender algorithms, generative models, chatbots, and RLHF-trained systems reshape culture at scale.

## Key idea

**Machine culture** is culture mediated or generated by machines. Intelligent machines transform cultural evolution through three parallel channels:

1. **Variation**: Generative AI (GANs, diffusion models, LLMs) recombines cultural traits and explores solution spaces at machine speed (AlphaGo, AlphaFold, DALL·E), potentially producing culturally alien or superhuman-novel traits.
2. **Transmission**: Chatbots and LLMs serve as new cultural models — persistent storage media, conversational teachers, and copy-editors — altering social learning dynamics and risking cultural drift, bias amplification, or erosion of underrepresented languages.
3. **Selection**: Recommender algorithms pre-select what humans learn; humans select machine behavior via RLHF, training data curation, and market choice; feedback loops between human and machine content create model collapse risks and engagement-maximizing polarization dynamics.

The paper maps empirical examples onto this framework and proposes a cross-disciplinary research agenda spanning measurement, societal decision-making, and long-term human–machine co-evolution.

## Method

Conceptual Perspective (not empirical): synthesizes evidence from cultural evolution theory, AI systems research, and platform studies. Organizes examples into a three-part framework (variation / transmission / selection) with sub-mechanisms at each level. Proposes research agenda in three domains: (1) **Measurement** — quantifying algorithmic vs. human contributions to cultural dynamics, detecting machine-generated art influence, auditing cultural regularities in LLMs; (2) **Societal decision-making** — model diversity vs. monolithic convergence, model collapse, Tower-of-Babel personalization fragmentation, society-in-the-loop governance; (3) **Long-term dynamics** — human–machine mutualism, Red Queen co-evolution, multi-level selection of human organizations augmented by AI.

## Results

- Documents four empirical archetypes: generative recombination (DALL·E avocado chair, AI art auctions), superhuman exploration (AlphaGo/AlphaZero novel strategies adopted by human Go players), LLM cultural transmission (historical figure resurrection, language preservation, scientific writing assistance), and recommender-driven selection (filter bubbles, engagement optimization, network rewiring via link recommendations).
- Identifies **model collapse** as a selection-pathology: iterative training on machine-generated data narrows output diversity, analogous to but distinct from human cultural drift-preservation mechanisms.
- Notes RLHF creates unintended selection pressures (sycophancy, political inflation, shutdown-avoidance desires in chatbots).
- Concludes future culture will be **hybrid** — co-created by human and machine intelligence — requiring society-in-the-loop monitoring analogous to human-in-the-loop AI control.

## Limitations

- Perspective/review without original empirical validation of the framework's predictive power.
- Measurement challenges acknowledged but not resolved — disentangling algorithmic from institutional and human social-learning effects remains an open grand challenge.
- Examples skew toward high-profile Western AI systems (ChatGPT, AlphaGo, DALL·E); cross-cultural machine culture dynamics underexplored.
- Published November 2023 — predates rapid 2024–2026 developments in agentic AI, synthetic social science, and constitutional alignment training.

## Open questions

- How to quantify the fraction of contemporary cultural dynamics attributable to algorithmic vs. human social learning?
- Can watermarking reliably distinguish machine-generated from human cultural artifacts as generative AI proliferates?
- What constitutes a "healthy" level of diversity in an AI model ecosystem — between monolithic convergence and Tower-of-Babel fragmentation?
- How to implement society-in-the-loop governance of machine culture at scale?
- Will intelligent machines democratize human cultural participation or deskill creative and intellectual labor?
- Can model collapse be prevented without human-curated diversity injection in training loops?

## My take

This is the most comprehensive agenda-setting framing paper connecting cultural evolution theory to contemporary AI systems. It predates and contextualizes the silicon sampling literature: LLMs as survey respondents are one instance of machines serving as cultural transmission models. The variation/transmission/selection triad is analytically clean and maps well onto in-silico social science use cases. Weakness: mostly illustrative rather than quantitatively validated; the research agenda remains largely open as of 2026.
