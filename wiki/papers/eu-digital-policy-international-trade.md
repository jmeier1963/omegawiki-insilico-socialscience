## Problem

The EU's digital policy agenda ("A Europe fit for the digital age") pursues regulatory objectives around data protection, platform accountability, and digital market competition. How do these policies interact with US trade interests and the transatlantic relationship?

## Key idea

Congressional Research Service analysis of EU digital policy from a US Congressional perspective. Reviews GDPR, Digital Single Market initiatives, and emerging platform regulations and their trade implications for US firms.

## Method

Policy analysis by Congressional Research Service (CRS R46732, March 2021). Reviews EU digital regulatory framework and US trade policy concerns. Non-partisan congressional analysis.

## Results

- EU has led globally in data privacy regulation (GDPR) and is developing Digital Markets Act, Digital Services Act
- EU regulation creates compliance costs for US digital firms but also shapes global standards
- "Brussels Effect": EU standards export globally as firms harmonize toward strictest jurisdiction
- Key US concerns: data flows, algorithmic liability, AI regulation, platform competition rules

## Limitations

- Published 2021; predates EU AI Act finalization, DMA/DSA implementation
- CRS provides analysis for Congress; balanced but US-centric perspective
- Does not address subsequent policy developments (EU AI Act 2024)

## Open questions

- Has EU AI Act created comparable trade friction to GDPR?
- Does the Brussels Effect operate for AI regulation as strongly as for data privacy?

## My take

Solid reference document for EU-US digital policy relationship. Useful for understanding regulatory divergence and convergence dynamics. The Brussels Effect concept is well-established and important. Importance 3 (authoritative reference analysis).
