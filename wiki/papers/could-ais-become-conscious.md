## Problem & Context

The leader of a cover package published 20 August 2026. Its premise is that the consciousness question has left the seminar room: as systems become more convincing conversational partners, the practical question is not what they are but how they will be treated.

The piece opens on the human-exceptionalism intuition — *Homo sapiens* evolved to be conscious, many animals feel pain and pleasure, some appear self-aware, but the human mind's capacity for a sense of self is taken to mark the species for special treatment. It notes that Pope Leo's May encyclical upheld that line explicitly, holding that AI systems do not undergo experiences or feel joy or pain, and that many people regard conferring personhood on AIs as an abomination.

## Key idea

The subtitle carries the argument: *even if they don't, they might be treated as such — to humanity's great cost*. The risk to be managed is not machine suffering but the social and political consequences of attributing moral status where there is none, and the difficulty of adjudicating the question with the evidence available.

## Method

Editorial argument.

## Experiment & Results

No results. The claims are normative and predictive: that attributions of consciousness will be made on behavioural evidence that cannot settle the question; that such attributions have costs (legal, political, and in the allocation of moral concern); and that institutions are unprepared to adjudicate.

## Limitations

- Leader-length editorial, not analysis; the argument is asserted rather than defended in depth.
- The costs of misattribution are stated but not specified or weighed against the costs of under-attribution.
- Relies on the accompanying briefing for the empirical substance.

## Open questions

- What evidence could bear on machine consciousness at all, given that behavioural evidence is what the systems are optimized to produce?
- Who would have standing to adjudicate the question institutionally?
- Does the cost asymmetry the leader assumes hold, or is under-attribution the larger risk?

## My take

For an argument about the science system, the relevant transfer is not the consciousness question but the evidential one. A system optimized to produce the outward signs of an inner state provides no evidence about that state — which is the same structure as a system that produces the outward signs of understanding without a comprehending act underneath. In both cases the performance is what the system was built to deliver, so the performance cannot be the evidence.
