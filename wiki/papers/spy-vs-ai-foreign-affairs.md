## Problem

How will AI transform intelligence collection, analysis, and counterintelligence operations, and how should democracies respond to adversaries exploiting the same technologies?

## Key idea

Anne Neuberger (former NSC Deputy National Security Adviser for Cyber) argues that AI—especially LLMs—will revolutionize intelligence operations: enabling faster and more comprehensive analysis, automating collection tasks, and transforming counterintelligence. But adversaries gain the same capabilities, creating a two-edged sword. The US must lead AI adoption in intelligence while maintaining democratic values and transparency.

## Method

Policy analysis from a senior national security practitioner with direct operational intelligence experience (NSA, NSC). January 2025, Foreign Affairs. 9 pages.

## Results

- AI enables "more, better, faster" intelligence: automated processing of vast datasets, real-time insights, reduced analytical latency
- Counterintelligence risk: adversaries can use AI to identify and compromise US intelligence assets faster than ever
- Democracies face a harder constraint: must use AI ethically and transparently, unlike authoritarian adversaries
- US must invest in deep technological and cultural change within intelligence agencies
- Recommends: AI integration in intelligence workflows, clear ethical frameworks, public communication of AI use principles

## Limitations

- Written from a US national security perspective; may underestimate allied coordination needs
- Does not address open-source AI proliferation making adversary capability harder to contain
- Ethical constraints on democracies are real but may be competitively costly

## Open questions

- Can US intelligence agencies adopt AI fast enough given procurement/bureaucracy constraints?
- How to prevent AI from enabling authoritarian governments to identify dissidents globally?
- What ethical framework for AI use in covert intelligence collection?

## My take

Strong practitioner perspective on AI and national security intelligence. Neuberger's operational background gives this credibility beyond typical policy analysis. The "democracies face harder constraints" argument is analytically important. Importance 3 (significant policy analysis by senior practitioner, Foreign Affairs).
