## Problem

Limited understanding of how postgraduate students at historically disadvantaged South African universities perceive and use ChatGPT for academic success.

## Key idea

Qualitative phenomenological study (N=10) exploring postgraduate students' experiences using ChatGPT at two historically disadvantaged universities in South Africa. Thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews.

## Method

Qualitative, purposive sampling, 10 master's students at two South African universities. Semi-structured interviews, thematic analysis.

## Results

Students found ChatGPT beneficial for: refining research topics, identifying grammatical errors, paraphrasing academic writing, and general research support. All participants reported perceived benefits for academic success.

## Limitations

Very small sample (N=10), single country, qualitative only, no learning outcome measurement, no control group.

## Open questions

- Do perceived benefits translate to actual learning outcome improvements?
- How do access and digital literacy gaps moderate ChatGPT utility in developing-nation contexts?

## My take

Small qualitative study from a historically disadvantaged institutional context — adds an underrepresented perspective to the ChatGPT-in-education literature. Findings directionally consistent with meta-analyses but not statistically comparable.
