## Problem

When did Silicon Valley's political realignment toward Trump begin, and what does it reveal about the tech industry's character?

## Key idea

Kara Swisher's Washington Post opinion (adapted from "Burn Book: A Tech Love Story") tracing the Silicon Valley-Trump relationship back to December 2016, when tech CEOs trooped into Trump Tower for a summit. Argues this early accommodation set the pattern for later tech-political realignment. "It was capitalism after all" — financial incentives over democratic values.

## Method

Opinion essay. 4 pages. Kara Swisher (tech journalist, podcast host).

## Results

- December 2016: major tech CEOs summoned to Trump Tower; most attended (accommodation)
- Swisher's framing: this was the moment the tech industry chose capitalism over stated democratic values
- Pattern established: tech companies will align politically when financial interests are at stake
- Connects to later Musk/Zuckerberg political realignments (post-2024)

## Limitations

- Opinion journalism; highly partisan framing
- "Burn Book" excerpt: promotional context

## My take

Useful cultural commentary from a long-time Silicon Valley observer. The "capitalism after all" thesis is blunt but analytically consistent with subsequent events. Importance 1 (op-ed; cultural context but no analytical depth).
