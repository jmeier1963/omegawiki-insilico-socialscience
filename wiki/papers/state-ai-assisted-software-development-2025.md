## Problem & Context

By 2025 the question for technology leaders is no longer whether to adopt AI but how to realize value; local productivity gains are often lost to downstream chaos.

## Key idea

**AI is an amplifier**: it magnifies the strengths of high-performing organizations and the dysfunctions of struggling ones. Returns come less from tools than from the organizational system (internal platform quality, workflow clarity, team alignment).

## Method

Global survey (13 June to 21 July 2025) of nearly 5,000 professionals plus over 100 hours of qualitative data; statistical analysis including clustering into seven team profiles; introduces the DORA AI Capabilities Model of seven foundational practices (e.g. a clear AI policy, a healthy data ecosystem, user-centric focus, quality internal platforms).

## Experiment & Results

Adoption is near universal (90% use AI, over 80% believe it raised productivity), yet 30% report little or no trust. AI adoption now improves software delivery throughput, a change from last year, but it still increases delivery instability. Platform engineering and value stream management are reported as conditions for converting local gains into organizational ones.

## Limitations

Self-reported survey data with correlational design and sponsor involvement; perceived productivity can diverge from measured productivity (see METR RCT). Read at executive-summary level.

## Open questions

Which of the seven capabilities cause gains? How does instability evolve as agents take on more delivery work?

## My take

Good practitioner-side evidence for the complementarity argument: AI returns depend on the surrounding system. Weigh self-reported productivity against the RCT evidence.
