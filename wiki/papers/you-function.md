## Problem

As AI systems automate more cognitive work, the dominant cultural response is to optimize humans for remaining useful (reskilling, credentialism, functional adaptability). The essay argues this response is a trap that accepts a false premise: that human value is justified by function.

## Key idea

Drawing on Wilhelm von Humboldt's concept of *Bildung* — the free, harmonious development of a human being's powers into a complete whole through encounter with the world — the essay argues that education should aim at *formation* rather than *function*. Humboldt's insight in 1809 Prussia (a defeated nation that wanted more engineers and administrators) was that broadly cultivated judgment is more resilient and more humane than technical skill resting on nothing. The AI moment rhymes with Humboldt's: technological pressure is again pushing education toward the purely practical, and the same error is at risk.

## Method

Essay / narrative argument; no empirical study. Uses historical analogy (Prussia 1809, Humboldt's university design), philosophical argument (tree vs. steam engine metaphor from Mill), and cultural diagnosis. Author: Brendan McCord, Cosmos Institute blog, April 2026.

## Results

Key argumentative moves:
- Bildung "wins" even on functional terms: broadly cultivated judgment produces better doctors and engineers than technical training alone
- But if Bildung is justified only by output, it becomes merely a roundabout way of producing skilled professionals — losing its essential character
- The race to stay useful (reskilling for AI-complementarity) concedes that humans are justified by function, which is the trap
- The correct response to AI is not to optimize human function faster but to insist on the priority of formation over function
- Credentialism has already corrupted the university by treating it as a vendor of certificates; AI intensifies this pressure

## Limitations

- Blog post; not peer-reviewed and not empirically grounded
- Argument is normative and historical, not empirical
- Does not engage with how Bildung could be operationalized in contemporary educational institutions under AI pressure
- Does not address differential access to Bildung-style education

## Open questions

- What institutional forms can sustain Bildung in AI-saturated educational environments?
- Is "encounter with resistance" (a Bildung requirement) achievable when AI can remove most cognitive difficulty?
- Does the Bildung critique apply equally to K-12, undergraduate, and professional/graduate education?

## My take

The argument is philosophically well-grounded and the historical analogy is apt. The core insight — that accepting the "race to stay useful" framing concedes the very premise that needs challenging — is sharp. The practical gap is significant: Humboldt was designing a university from scratch in a crisis; we are trying to reform entrenched institutions under market pressure. But as a diagnostic frame and a standard against which to measure AI-era educational proposals, Bildung is more useful than most alternative framings. Particularly relevant to the wiki's concern about AI's effect on scientific formation and epistemic agency.
