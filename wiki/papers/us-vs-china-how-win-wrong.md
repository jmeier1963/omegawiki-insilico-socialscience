## Problem

Is the US-China AI competition actually an "AI race" for the smartest models, and is that the right framing?

## Key idea

Sangeet Paul Choudary (Platforms Newsletter, January 2026) argues the US is running the wrong race: the US frames AI competition as "who builds smarter models," while China is betting that intelligence will become abundant (a commodity) and that power will flow to whoever can most reliably convert intelligence into economic value and coordination. China's real strategy is about coordination infrastructure — reliable AI deployment at economic scale — not frontier model intelligence. The US may win the model race while losing the economic and coordination race.

## Method

Platform economics analysis. Newsletter format, 12 pages (email newsletter saved as PDF).

## Results

- US frames AI competition as intelligence race (who has the smartest LLM)
- China's actual strategy: bet on intelligence becoming abundant; focus on coordination infrastructure
- China's advantage: ability to rapidly integrate AI into production processes, supply chains, and industrial coordination
- US risk: winning frontier model competition while losing at economic integration and deployment
- Recommends US reframe AI strategy from "model leadership" to "coordination leadership"

## Limitations

- Newsletter format; advocacy framing without systematic evidence
- May overstate China's coordination coherence; Chinese AI deployment faces real barriers
- Platform economics lens may miss security and military dimensions

## Open questions

- Is the intelligence-abundance hypothesis correct? (Depends on whether frontier scaling continues)
- Can the US build coordination infrastructure as efficiently as China?

## My take

Interesting contrarian perspective on US-China AI competition. The "coordination vs intelligence" framing is analytically useful and underrepresented in US policy discourse. Importance 2 (newsletter analysis, not peer-reviewed, but analytically substantive).
