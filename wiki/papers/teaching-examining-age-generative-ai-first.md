## Problem

The sudden availability of generative AI (ChatGPT) in late 2022 rendered many existing teaching and examination formats — especially written essays — trivially passable by AI. Higher education institutions (IHEs) had no established framework for responding, and early reactions (banning tools) were compared to forbidding calculators or books.

## Key idea

IHEs must treat generative AI as a "cognitive extension" analogous to previous technologies (books, calculators, printing press) and fundamentally reassess three questions: (1) what skills will be valuable post-AI, (2) how students learn most effectively with AI tools, and (3) what examination formats remain meaningful. The paper argues for more interactive, individualized, personality-development-focused teaching.

## Method

Analytical essay with institutional perspective. No empirical study. Authors are senior professors/administrators at the University of St. Gallen with direct experience redesigning programs.

## Results

Three-part framework for IHE response:
1. **Skill redefinition**: Focus on competencies that complement AI rather than compete with it — judgment, creativity, social skills, ethical reasoning
2. **Teaching reform**: Shift toward interactive, individualized, Socratic formats that AI cannot trivially replicate; integrate AI as a learning tool
3. **Examination reform**: Move away from essays and written deliverables toward oral examinations, portfolio-based assessment, and in-person collaborative tasks

Warns of a widening gap between low-cost IHEs (focused on basic knowledge AI can replicate) and high-quality institutions investing in human-intensive learning.

## Limitations

Written in January 2023, early in the GPT-3.5 era — some predictions may be outdated. No empirical data; purely normative and analytical. Primarily relevant to European and business school context.

## Open questions

- How do IHEs determine which competencies are durably non-automatable vs. transiently safe?
- What examination formats reliably assess genuine understanding vs. AI assistance?
- How do quality gaps between institutions evolve as AI becomes more capable?

## My take

An early thoughtful response from established educators who move beyond panic to policy proposals. The "cognitive extension" framing is useful: the question is not whether to ban AI but what skills must be taught that AI cannot replace. Dated by now but historically relevant as a 2023 baseline.
