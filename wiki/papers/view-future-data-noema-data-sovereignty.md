## Problem

How should individuals and communities collectively govern their data in the era of surveillance capitalism, moving beyond both individual data ownership and corporate extraction?

## Key idea

Matt Prewitt (RadicalxChange Foundation, co-author of the Data Freedom Act) argues for "data coalitions" as a collective governance model for data. Individual data sovereignty is insufficient — data derives value from collective patterns, so governance must be collective. Data coalitions would allow groups to negotiate collectively with platforms, share revenues, and retain meaningful control. Alternative to both surveillance capitalism and naive individual ownership models.

## Method

Essay, Noema Magazine, February 2021. 12 pages. Prewitt draws on RadicalxChange philosophy and data policy analysis.

## Results

- Critique of "data as property" framing: individual ownership doesn't work because data's value is collective
- Data coalitions: groups form collective bargaining entities for data governance with platforms
- Historical parallel: labor unions as collective bargaining for workers; data coalitions for data subjects
- Data Freedom Act framework provides legal infrastructure for such coalitions
- Implications: alternative path to surveillance capitalism that maintains collective agency

## Limitations

- 2021 essay; pre-dates major generative AI developments that changed data valuation
- Data coalitions face collective action problems; organizing cost may be prohibitive
- Platform incentives strongly opposed to such arrangements

## Open questions

- Can data coalitions overcome collective action problems at scale?
- How do data coalitions apply to AI training data specifically?

## My take

Useful conceptual contribution to data governance debates. The "collective value of data requires collective governance" argument is analytically sound and underrepresented in policy discourse. Importance 2 (important essay but from 2021, pre-GenAI, and conceptual rather than empirical).
