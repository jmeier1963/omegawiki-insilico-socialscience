## Statement

If advanced AI generates large productivity gains, those gains will concentrate (in a few firms and capital owners) unless deliberate policy intervenes. Achieving broad prosperity therefore requires a renegotiated social contract — predistribution (worker voice, capital access, universal AI access) and/or redistribution (income support funded by taxes on AI firms or capital) — rather than reliance on markets alone.

## Evidence summary

OpenAI's "Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age" lays out an open-economy agenda premised on the insufficiency of markets, echoing Progressive-Era/New-Deal institution-building; it acknowledges the risk that gains "concentrate within a small number of firms like OpenAI."

## Conditions and scope

US-focused starting point; superintelligence-transition framing; mechanism-level details unspecified.

## Counter-evidence

The [[ai-normal-technology]] view questions whether the assumed large, fast gains even materialize; market-liberal critics doubt industrial policy's efficacy and warn of capture by the very firms proposing it.

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

Which concrete mechanisms broaden returns without entrenching incumbents? Predistribution vs redistribution — which carries the load?
