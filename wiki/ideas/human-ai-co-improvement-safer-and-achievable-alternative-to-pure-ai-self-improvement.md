## Statement

Instead of targeting fully autonomous AI self-improvement (AI independently redesigning and retraining itself), the research community should target human-AI co-improvement: AI systems that work with human researchers to conduct AI research, from ideation through experimentation. This is argued to be both achievable sooner (using current AI capabilities) and safer (human oversight is maintained throughout the improvement loop), producing "co-superintelligence" through human-AI symbiosis.

## Evidence summary

Weston & Foerster (2025, Meta FAIR) make this argument in a position paper. The evidence is primarily argumentative, not empirical.

## Conditions and scope

Position paper; no empirical validation. The key unstated assumption is that co-improvement stays co-improvement — mechanisms to prevent autonomous drift are not specified.

## Counter-evidence

- The boundary between co-improvement and autonomous self-improvement may be difficult to maintain as AI capabilities increase
- Current AI systems may not be capable of the long-horizon research tasks required for effective co-improvement
- Safety benefits are asserted but not formally analyzed

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- What formal mechanisms maintain the "co" in co-improvement as AI systems become more capable?
- Is co-improvement compatible with competitive AI development incentives?
- How does this interact with the software intelligence explosion scenario?
