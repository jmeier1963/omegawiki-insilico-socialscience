## Statement

AI has crossed from speculative to demonstrated frontier risk, so governance should move beyond transparency to binding, FAA-style pre-deployment regulation: mandatory third-party safety testing of sufficiently capable models, with authority to block or reverse deployment when unacceptable risks are found.

## Evidence summary

The essay cites demonstrated cyber capabilities as proof that frontier models are of strategic consequence, and lays out a concrete legislative proposal (compute-threshold testing, scoped block authority, regulatory-markets option, weight security, incident reporting).

## Conditions and scope

Frontier models above a compute threshold; four enumerated risk areas; US-centric but argued to generalize.

## Counter-evidence

The US light-touch framework ([[us-ai-governance-uses-light-touch]]) recommends against broad AI regulation and favors preemption of state laws — a direct contrast. Critics note incumbent-entrenchment and regulatory-capture risks in CEO-authored proposals.

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

Can binding regimes target real risks without favoritism or entrenching incumbents? Where is the right compute/capability threshold?
