## Statement

In organizations that successfully extract value from AI transformations, the majority (~70%) of that value is driven by getting the *human* elements right — people having genuine agency, skills, emotional readiness, and authentic buy-in — rather than by the quality or sophistication of the AI technology itself.

## Evidence summary

BCG (Dhar et al., 2026) synthesizes observations from AI transformation programs across industries. Supporting data points: only 5% of companies achieve substantial AI value (BCG 2025 study); a 76%/31% executive/employee excitement gap from BCG's "AI at Work" survey; only 36% of employees report receiving adequate AI training.

## Conditions and scope

- BCG consulting research; inherent selection bias toward their own engagement portfolio
- The 70% figure is internally derived, not published in a peer-reviewed context
- May not generalize to small organizations or highly technical (vs. knowledge-work) AI applications

## Counter-evidence

- The Meta AI mandate case (employees reporting misery under compulsory AI use) could be read as evidence that *technology compulsion* without behavioral support creates more misery than value — consistent with this claim
- However, some evidence suggests that usage mandates do increase adoption rates even if satisfaction falls

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- Is the 70% people vs 30% technology split consistent across industries and AI application types?
- Does the split shift as AI systems become more capable and easier to use?
