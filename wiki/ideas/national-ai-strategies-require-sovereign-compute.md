## Statement

National AI strategies that focus only on AI adoption and regulation — without investing in domestic compute infrastructure — leave countries strategically dependent on foreign cloud and AI providers. Effective sovereignty requires physical infrastructure investment alongside regulatory frameworks.

## Evidence summary

Multiple government strategy documents (UK, EU/EuroStack) identify compute as a foundational requirement. Confidence low: this is primarily a policy claim; empirical evidence for compute sovereignty outcomes is limited.

## Counter-evidence

- Cloud access (not domestic ownership) may be sufficient for most national AI use cases
- High capital requirements for domestic compute may be inefficient compared to purchasing cloud access

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- At what scale of AI adoption does domestic compute become economically necessary?
- How does public compute investment crowd-in or crowd-out private investment?
