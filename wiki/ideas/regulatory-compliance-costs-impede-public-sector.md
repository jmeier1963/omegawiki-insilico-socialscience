## Statement

Overly prescriptive AI safety regulations for public sector use — such as those in the OMB AI governance memo — impose compliance costs (impact assessments, consultation requirements, testing mandates) that historically have paralyzed government IT projects, risking the same outcome for beneficial AI applications in healthcare, social services, and fraud detection.

## Evidence summary

Based on opinion/commentary by Mittelsteadt (2024). No empirical evidence; draws on general government IT regulatory history. Confidence low: argument is plausible but unsubstantiated empirically; counter-argument (that regulations create appropriate accountability) is not examined.

## Conditions and scope

- Applies specifically to OMB AI Executive Order implementation memo (March 2024)
- May not generalize beyond US federal government context

## Counter-evidence

- Regulatory requirements may be appropriate given high-stakes nature of government AI (rights impacts, safety)
- Chief AI Officer structure may streamline rather than burden compliance

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- What is the actual compliance rate and AI adoption rate under the OMB framework?
- Do streamlined "innovation tracks" within regulatory frameworks preserve safety while reducing burden?
