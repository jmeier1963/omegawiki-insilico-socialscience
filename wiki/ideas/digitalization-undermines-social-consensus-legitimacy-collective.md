## Statement

Digitalization — and especially AI-driven algorithmic mediation of information and decision-making — accelerates social complexity in ways that fragment shared worldviews, reduce the experience of collective agency, and thereby undermine the normative consensus on which the legitimacy of collective rules depends.

## Evidence summary

Currently supported only by philosophical argument (Suchanek 2024). Empirical evidence on filter bubble effects, political polarization, and trust in institutions is relevant but not yet integrated into a unified causal account.

## Conditions and scope

Liberal democratic societies with contested public spheres. Effects may be moderated by media literacy, institutional strength, and platform governance.

## Counter-evidence

Digitalization also enables new forms of collective deliberation and global information sharing. Whether net effect is consensus-eroding depends on institutional design choices.

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- What measurable indicators of social consensus erosion are most clearly attributable to algorithmic mediation vs. other causes?
- Can algorithmic systems be designed to support rather than fragment shared normative foundations?
