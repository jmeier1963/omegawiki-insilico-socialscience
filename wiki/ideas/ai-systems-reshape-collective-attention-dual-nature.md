## Statement

AI systems — including recommendation algorithms, generative media, LLMs, and social bots — can systematically distort collective attention (via misinformation amplification, echo chambers, temporal compression of discourse) but also hold the potential to enhance it (by improving access to reliable knowledge and reducing information processing burdens). Neither purely destructive nor purely beneficial, AI reshapes the epistemic commons in domain- and design-dependent ways.

## Evidence summary

Synthesized from: false news diffusion studies (Vosoughi et al.); algorithmic curation and user-choice research (Facebook cross-party exposure study); moral contagion analysis; collective attention duration trends. Enhancement potential is more speculative; no strong empirical evidence directly tested at scale.

## Conditions and scope

- Distortion pathway: strong empirical support from social media studies
- Enhancement pathway: plausible but empirically underspecified
- Claim applies at the ecosystem level; individual-use effects may vary substantially

## Counter-evidence

Some research suggests AI-assisted fact-checking and recommendation diversity have limited effectiveness against entrenched polarization (Bail et al. cross-party exposure experiment showing backfire effects).

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- Under what design conditions do AI systems tip toward net enhancement vs. net distortion of collective attention?
- Can the enhancement pathway be engineered at the same scale as distortion currently operates?
