## Statement

It is possible to design an AI-mediated institution that strengthens representative democracy by structuring and relaying organised citizen deliberation — preserving plurality, compelling answerability, and remaining open to contestation — rather than replacing human decision-makers or simulating a popular will.

## Evidence summary

The synthetic chamber proposal articulates such a design and its required guarantees, grounded in deliberative-democracy and party-decline theory, with an architecture extrapolated from a research-creation deliberation setting.

## Conditions and scope

Representative-democracy contexts with existing deliberative forums (assemblies, mini-publics); claim is design-level, not yet implemented or tested.

## Counter-evidence

Skeptics argue any AI mediation layer is liable to capture, manipulation, and false neutrality, reproducing the privatised-mediation problem it aims to solve; no working system demonstrates the guarantees.

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

Can traceability/contestability be technically guaranteed? What legitimacy criteria validate AI-mediated deliberative outputs?
