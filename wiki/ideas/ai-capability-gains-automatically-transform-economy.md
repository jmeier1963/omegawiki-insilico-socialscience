## Statement

Advances in AI capability do not, by default, translate into mass labor displacement or lower consumer costs. What gates real-world impact are adoption and structural bottlenecks — regulatory barriers, adversarial dynamics, the persistence of decision/delivery work, and human accountability — not benchmark performance.

## Evidence summary

Two "AI as Normal Technology" essays: the software-engineering case shows aggregate demand resilient and "AI-driven" layoffs largely mislabeled; the legal-services case identifies three bottlenecks (regulatory, adversarial, human) that prevent automatic cost reduction.

## Conditions and scope

US knowledge-work professions, 2026; structural/default-trajectory claim.

## Counter-evidence

"Country of geniuses in a datacenter" forecasts ([[policy-ai-exponential]]) and threshold-driven disruption narratives predict faster, larger transformation; the disagreement is about whether bottlenecks hold as agents take on decide/deliver layers.

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

Which bottlenecks erode first as agents improve? How to empirically measure adoption-gated (vs capability-driven) impact?
