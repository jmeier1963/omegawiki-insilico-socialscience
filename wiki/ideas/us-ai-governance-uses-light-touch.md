## Statement

The US approach to AI governance under the Trump Administration (2025-2026) prioritizes light-touch deregulation — minimizing broad regulatory mandates — while carving out targeted requirements for child safety, national security, and preemption of state-level AI regulations. This reflects a deliberate policy choice to enable private sector AI leadership over precautionary governance.

## Evidence summary

Directly evidenced by the White House National Policy Framework for AI Legislative Recommendations (March 2026): 4-page document explicitly recommending Congress avoid "open-ended liability" and "burdensome" standards while adding specific child-safety and national security provisions. Confidence 0.65 (single source, though authoritative).

## Conditions and scope

- Applies to Trump Administration legislative recommendations only
- Does not reflect enacted law (as of early 2026)
- Contrasts with EU AI Act comprehensive risk-based approach
- May face bipartisan modifications in Congress

## Counter-evidence

- Previous bipartisan AI Safety Act proposals in Senate suggest Congressional preferences may differ
- State-level AI laws continue developing even as federal preemption is sought

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- Will Congress adopt the White House's deregulatory framework or impose additional requirements?
- Does light-touch regulation leave adequate protection for frontier AI risks?
