## Statement

Based on the observed trajectory of AI capability gains (measured by the Epoch Capabilities Index and benchmark achievements) and the scale of compute investments already committed, AI systems capable of building their own successors — initiating recursive self-improvement — may emerge within 2 years (by ~2028), not on a decade-scale horizon.

## Evidence summary

Clark (2026, Cosmos HAI Lab Lecture, Oxford) makes this prediction based on: (1) the consistent, predictable growth in AI capability as a function of compute and data investment; (2) hundreds of billions of dollars in committed infrastructure investment meaning "some amount of future progress is already locked in"; (3) personal experience watching the capability frontier from inside Anthropic.

## Conditions and scope

- Low confidence (0.35): timeline predictions for transformative AI capabilities are extremely difficult and historically unreliable
- Strong selection bias: Anthropic co-founder has incentives (fundraising, narrative) aligned with asserting rapid progress
- "Recursive self-improvement" is not precisely defined — the claim is qualitative

## Counter-evidence

- Kapoor & Narayanan's AINT framework argues AI diffusion is slow and labor market impacts remain modest despite years of capability gains
- Diminishing returns arguments suggest current LLM paradigm may plateau before reaching self-improvement capability
- Historical AI timeline predictions have consistently been too optimistic (Minsky, McCarthy, etc.)
