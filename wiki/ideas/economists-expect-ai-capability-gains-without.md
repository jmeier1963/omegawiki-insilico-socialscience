## Statement

Leading economists assign high probability (~61%) to significant AI capability progress by 2030 (moderate or rapid scenarios) but nevertheless predict that macroeconomic indicators (GDP growth, total factor productivity, labor force participation) will remain near historical trends — suggesting economists believe short-term macroeconomic impact will be modest even given substantial capability advances.

## Evidence summary

Forecasting Research Institute survey (2025): 69 economists, 52 AI experts, 38 superforecasters, 401 public. Systematic elicitation with defined scenarios. Moderate confidence: large expert sample, rigorous methodology; but subject to anchoring and scenario design effects.

## Conditions and scope

- Predictions conditional on 2030 time horizon
- Rapid-progress scenario (14% probability) would produce major economic disruption
- Does not capture heterogeneous sectoral impacts within aggregate indicators

## Counter-evidence

- Optimistic forecasters (AI experts) assign higher probability to rapid progress
- Historical analogy with electricity/ICT adoption suggests decade-long lags before macroeconomic impact materializes

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- Why do economists predict high capability but low macro impact? Adjustment costs, skill complementarity, or model uncertainty?
- How does this expert disagreement pattern change as AI capabilities advance post-2025?
