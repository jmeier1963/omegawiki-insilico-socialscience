## Statement

Competitive AI development races between state actors and corporations are inherently destabilizing: they erode cooperation incentives, accelerate timelines beyond safety margins, and increase the probability of catastrophic outcomes relative to coordinated development regimes.

## Evidence summary

Supported by simulation evidence from 43 runs of Intelligence Rising (Gruetzemacher et al. 2024): race dynamics consistently produced endgame instability, fragile international agreements, and coordination failures on safety research. Confidence moderate because evidence is simulation-based, not observed from real-world AI race events.

## Conditions and scope

- Holds most strongly when: capability progress is rapid, verification of agreements is difficult, and actors have heterogeneous safety cultures
- May not apply if strong early coordination mechanisms are established before race dynamics take hold

## Counter-evidence

- Some argue competition accelerates beneficial innovation and that race framing overstates risk
- Historical tech races (semiconductors, internet) did not produce catastrophic outcomes

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- Does the destabilization effect depend on the number of actors, or is a two-actor race sufficient?
- Can compute governance (export controls, chip monitoring) structurally alter race dynamics?
