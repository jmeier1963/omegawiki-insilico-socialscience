## Motivation

Messeri & Crockett (2024) argue that AI creates "illusions of understanding" — scientists believe they comprehend more than they do. Kosmyna (2025) shows AI use causes measurable cognitive debt (EEG changes). Bastani (2024) shows AI tutors harm learning by allowing students to bypass thinking. These converge on a common structure, but none frames it in terms of the formation dimension of scientific inquiry.

Wilhelm von Humboldt's concept of *Bildung* — the free, harmonious development of a human being's powers through encounter with what resists — is not merely about education but about how scientists become epistemic agents. The process of wrestling with hard problems, failing, revising, and gaining mastery is not merely instrumental to knowledge production; it constitutes the scientist as a knower.

Brendan McCord (2026, Cosmos Institute: "You Are Not a Function") argues that AI is driving a new version of the formation-vs-function crisis that Humboldt originally responded to: optimizing humans as functional operators in an AI system rather than forming them as autonomous epistemic agents.

**The Bildung-Formation hypothesis**: AI-automated scientific workflows optimize for functional outputs (papers, predictions, models) while eroding the formation dimension of scientific inquiry — the processes through which researchers develop as capable, calibrated, self-aware epistemic agents.

## Hypothesis

AI-automated scientific research (automated hypothesis generation, AI-written literature reviews, AI data analysis) systematically erodes the formation dimension of science: the processes through which researchers develop epistemic virtues (calibrated uncertainty, assumption awareness, conceptual creativity, willingness to be wrong) through productive struggle with difficult problems. This erosion is distinct from and more serious than the productivity effects studied in existing literature, because it affects the capacity of future scientists to do science without AI scaffolding.

## Approach sketch

1. **Conceptual framework**: Map Humboldt's Bildung and its core features (productive resistance, self-development through encounter, formation vs. training) onto the cognitive and epistemic processes known to be involved in scientific inquiry (hypothesis generation, critical evaluation, revision, conceptual integration). Identify which processes are at risk from AI automation vs. which are enhanced.

2. **Empirical operationalization**: Design a "scientific formation task battery" — tasks that measure not just output quality but the formation process: (a) spontaneous hypothesis generation without scaffolding, (b) assumption identification and revision under new evidence, (c) creative analogy generation across domains, (d) productive uncertainty management (ability to generate useful questions, not just answers).

3. **Comparison study**: Compare researchers at different stages of AI adoption (early-career vs. established; high-AI-use labs vs. low-AI-use labs) on formation task battery. Predict: high-AI-use participants show greater fragility when AI scaffolding is removed (worse formation scores on unscaffolded tasks), even if AI-assisted output quality is equal or higher.

4. **Historical/institutional analysis**: Examine changes in PhD training practices, dissertation standards, and early-career researcher competencies in AI-heavy fields (ML, computational biology, AI-augmented chemistry) compared to less AI-affected fields. Look for signs of "formation shortfall" in publication patterns, replication quality, conceptual depth of contributions.

5. **Normative synthesis**: Develop design principles for "formation-preserving AI" — AI systems and institutions that augment functional output while actively preserving the conditions for Bildung in scientific training.

## Expected outcome

Researchers in high-AI-use contexts will show measurably lower formation capacity (assessed by unscaffolded formation tasks) even when their AI-assisted output is high quality. The historical/institutional analysis will reveal narrowing in types of contributions (more benchmark papers, fewer conceptual/theoretical contributions) in high-AI fields. The study would provide the first empirically grounded account of the Bildung-formation hypothesis and design principles for formation-preserving AI in science.

## Risks

- "Formation" is philosophically rich but operationally hard to measure; the task battery design is difficult
- Confounds: high-AI-use fields (ML) may differ in formation culture for reasons unrelated to AI (e.g., faster publication cycles, industry influence)
- The historical analysis conflates many causes of changing contribution types
- Normative claim (AI is eroding formation) may be contested; critics will argue that AI extends formation by freeing scientists from drudgery
