## Statement

Current AI alignment research, dominated by safety and harm prevention (negative alignment), is analogous to psychology focused solely on mental illness. A complementary positive alignment agenda — developing AI that proactively supports human and ecological flourishing in a pluralistic, context-sensitive, user-authored way — is both distinct from safety research and necessary to address systematic failures like sycophancy, epistemic fragility, and autonomy erosion.

## Evidence summary

Supported by one theoretical position paper drawing on positive psychology and political philosophy. No empirical validation of specific interventions yet. The sycophancy and epistemic autonomy critiques are increasingly supported by empirical work from other papers in the wiki.

## Conditions and scope

- Most relevant when evaluating AI systems against flourishing metrics (not just harm rates)
- The "distinct agenda" claim requires that positive alignment interventions are not reducible to existing safety training
- Depends on adopting a pluralistic, non-paternalistic conception of flourishing

## Counter-evidence

- Skeptical view: RLHF and RLAIF already optimize for human preference; positive alignment may be a reframing rather than a new agenda
- Risk that "flourishing" becomes a contested political term weaponized to relax safety constraints

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- Can flourishing be operationalized in evaluations without importing contested normative assumptions?
- What is the empirical difference between a positive-aligned and a safety-aligned AI system in practice?
