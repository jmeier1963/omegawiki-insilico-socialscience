## Statement

Current AI chatbots (ChatGPT3, ChatGPT4) on average outperform the mean human participant on standard divergent thinking creativity tasks (Alternate Uses Task), as measured by both semantic distance and subjective ratings, but the best human ideas still match or exceed AI performance — suggesting AI as a creativity augmentation tool rather than full replacement.

## Evidence summary

Koivisto & Grassini (2023) compared 256 humans to 3 AI chatbots on the AUT with fluency controlled. AI's mean and max scores were significantly higher than humans' mean scores. However, no chatbot's maximum score exceeded the highest human maximum scores across test objects.

## Conditions and scope

Single divergent thinking task; Western adult sample; snapshot in time (2023). Does not address more open-ended creative tasks, artistic creativity, or collaborative creativity.

## Counter-evidence

- No insight into AI generation mechanism — may reflect semantic retrieval rather than genuine creative recombination
- Human performance was tested under 30s time constraint; AI may benefit from different cognitive resources
- Results may not generalize to non-Western populations or older adults

## Open questions

- Does the "best human" advantage hold for tasks with no prior solutions in AI training data?
- How do human-AI creative collaborations compare to either alone?
