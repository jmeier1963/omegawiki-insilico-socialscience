## Statement

When AI systems act as cognitive infrastructures — filtering, ranking, and pre-selecting information before it enters conscious awareness — they automate the task of *relevance judgment* that previously belonged to human attention and will. This shifts the *locus of epistemic agency* from individual human reasoners to non-human algorithmic systems, with implications for individual cognition, collective deliberation, and cultural knowledge transmission.

## Evidence summary

One conceptual paper (Riva 2025) proposing the Cognitive Infrastructure Studies framework. Evidence is theoretical, drawing on infrastructure studies, the System 0 concept (Chiriatti et al. 2024), and narrative scenarios. Status set to "proposed" pending empirical validation via infrastructure breakdown experiments or comparable methods.

## Conditions and scope

- Applies most clearly to contexts of high AI preprocessing (recommendation algorithms, LLM-mediated search, personalized content feeds)
- Adaptive invisibility means the effect may be stronger for more sophisticated, integrated systems
- Not yet empirically tested at the individual or population level

## Counter-evidence

Alternative view: humans retain meta-cognitive awareness of AI filtering and can override or supplement it; "tool use" framing is sufficient without the stronger "infrastructure" framing.

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- Can infrastructure breakdown experiments (withdrawing AI preprocessing after habituation) reveal measurable cognitive dependency?
- Is relevance judgment automation a qualitative shift or a continuum from prior media/information technologies?
- How does this interact with scientific reasoning specifically — do scientists using AI tools shift their epistemic agency in ways that affect discovery?
