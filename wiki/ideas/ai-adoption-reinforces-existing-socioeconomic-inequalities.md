## Statement

Early generative AI adoption patterns replicate existing socioeconomic privilege gradients: well-educated, younger, urban users gain first-mover advantages; disadvantaged groups face access barriers, GenAI literacy deficits, and algorithmic discrimination in high-stakes decisions (hiring, lending, welfare).

## Evidence summary

Primarily descriptive evidence from Germany (2024 policy brief). Consistent with broader digital divide literature. Causal evidence that AI adoption *causes* increased inequality (rather than correlates with pre-existing inequality) is still limited.

## Conditions and scope

Applies absent deliberate policy intervention. Effect size likely varies by domain (education, labor market, public services) and existing institutional equity mechanisms.

## Counter-evidence

AI tools could serve as equalizers if designed for accessibility and if literacy barriers are addressed — the "opportunity scenario" remains empirically open.

## Linked ideas

## Open questions

- What intervention intensity is needed to shift AI adoption from privilege-reinforcing to equalizing?
- Does the adoption gap narrow over technology generations (as with mobile phones) or persist?
