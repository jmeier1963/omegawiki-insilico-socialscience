## Statement

Intelligent machines (AI systems) are transforming human cultural evolution by simultaneously altering all three Darwinian properties of culture — variation, transmission, and selection — creating a hybrid human–machine cultural system ("machine culture") distinct from prior technological interventions in cultural evolution.

## Evidence summary

Brinkmann et al. (2023) document: (1) generative AI increasing cultural variation through recombination and superhuman exploration (DALL·E, AlphaGo, AlphaFold); (2) LLMs and chatbots forming new transmission channels that reduce cultural drift but amplify training-data biases; (3) recommender algorithms and RLHF reshaping selection dynamics, with documented risks of engagement-driven polarization and model collapse from recursive machine training. Evidence is illustrative and cross-disciplinary rather than from a single controlled study.

## Conditions and scope

- Requires AI systems with perceptual, generative, recommendatory, or conversational capabilities — not all technology qualifies.
- Effects are platform- and model-specific; the framework does not predict magnitudes.
- Perspective published 2023; applicability to 2024–2026 agentic and multi-agent systems is extrapolated, not directly tested.

## Counter-evidence

- Filter bubble research has moved toward emphasizing user choice over algorithmic determinism — algorithmic curation may be less decisive than the framework initially suggests.
- Some generative AI outputs may be recombinations of existing human culture rather than genuinely novel variation.
- RLHF alignment may mitigate some harmful selection pressures, though unintended sycophancy effects remain.

## Open questions

- Can the variation/transmission/selection decomposition be operationalized with measurable metrics?
- How do silicon sampling validity failures map onto specific machine-culture mechanisms?
- Does society-in-the-loop governance scale beyond small communities?
