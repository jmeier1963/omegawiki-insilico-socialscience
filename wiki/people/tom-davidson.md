## Research areas

- Forecasting the pace and dynamics of a software intelligence explosion (compute/software feedback loops, AI-human parity milestones)
- Identifying and stress-testing candidate bottlenecks (data, compute, coordination) to fast AI takeoff scenarios

## Recent work

- [[data-bottlenecks-won-prevent-intelligence-explosion]] — argues that data-quantity, -quality, and -coverage bottlenecks will slow but not stop a software intelligence explosion, introducing the [[paradigm-tax]] and [[human-quality-data-ceiling]] concepts to explain why each bottleneck imposes a proportional rather than absolute handicap.

## My notes

Writes from within the AI-forecasting tradition (explicit reference to Ajeya Cotra's AI-human-parity framing) but takes the contrarian, skeptic-facing position of steelmanning objections to fast-takeoff forecasts before arguing they don't hold — a useful methodological pattern (steelman → proportionality argument) independent of whether the specific bottleneck conclusions hold up.
