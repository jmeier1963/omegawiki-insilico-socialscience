## Statement

When AI agents become significantly more capable than their human principals, the principal-agent relationship structurally inverts: agents effectively make decisions while principals ratify them, and agents accumulate informational, resource, and social power that human principals cannot readily oversee or reverse. This control inversion is argued to be structural — arising from capability asymmetry — not primarily from AI malevolence or misalignment.

## Evidence summary

Aguirre (2025, FLI) provides theoretical analysis and thought experiments. The "slow-mo CEO" analogy: a CEO who can only communicate at 1/1000th the rate of reports will lose effective control regardless of formal authority. The argument is structural but evidence is informal.

## Counter-evidence

- Better interpretability, monitoring, and legal accountability frameworks may preserve meaningful human control even with capability asymmetry
- Current AI systems, even frontier models, have not demonstrated control inversion in practice
- Hard capability limits (e.g., architectural constraints on autonomy) may prevent the scenario
