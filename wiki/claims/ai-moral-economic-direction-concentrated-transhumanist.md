## Statement

The values and economic gains of AI are concentrating in a small group of technology magnates whose transhumanist, efficiency-maximizing worldview shapes what AI is built to do — raising a legitimacy problem ("a more moral AI is not enough if that morality is determined by a few") distinct from, but related to, the economic concentration problem.

## Evidence summary

Commentary citing concentrated ownership/voting control of major tech-AI firms and a recurrent transhumanist ideology among their leaders, framed against a limits-based humanist counter-tradition.

## Conditions and scope

US tech elites, 2026; ideological/qualitative claim.

## Counter-evidence

Market competition, regulation, and diffuse user demand also shape AI's direction; "transhumanism" lumps heterogeneous actors together, and elite rhetoric may not equal deployed-system values.

## Open questions

How much does elite ideology shape deployed AI vs. competitive forces? What would broaden who sets AI's values?
