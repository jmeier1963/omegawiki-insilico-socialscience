## Statement

When people direct AI coding agents, what predicts success is how well they understand the problem (domain expertise), not whether they are trained coders. Agents handle execution ("how"); humans supply planning and judgment ("what"). Returns to expertise therefore persist or rise rather than being eroded by automation.

## Evidence summary

Anthropic's analysis of ~400,000 Claude Code sessions finds near-equal success rates across occupations on coding tasks, success increasing with domain expertise, a modest expert/intermediate gap, and rising estimated task value (~25% over seven months) as usage shifts toward end-to-end agentic work.

## Conditions and scope

Interactive agentic coding (CLI / claude.ai / desktop), 2025–2026 frontier models; success inferred from verifiable evidence.

## Counter-evidence

Substitution-focused accounts predict coding agents displacing developers wholesale; this claim instead predicts complementarity with domain understanding. Resolution depends on whether the planning/execution split holds as agents take on more planning.

## Open questions

Does the pattern generalize beyond coding to broader knowledge work? Does the modest expertise gap persist as models improve?
