## Statement

AI — particularly large language models and autonomous systems — will transform intelligence operations: enabling faster, more comprehensive collection and analysis (with fewer human analysts); automating counterintelligence monitoring; and operating effectively in environments where traditional human intelligence is limited. This transformation is two-edged: adversaries gain the same capabilities simultaneously.

## Evidence summary

Convergent evidence from national security practitioner (Neuberger), military AI analysis (Economist), and real-world combat (Ukraine). Confidence 0.65 (plausible, directionally clear, but extent and pace uncertain).

## Conditions and scope

- Applies to frontier AI-capable nations (US, China, NATO allies)
- Democracies face legal/ethical constraints that authoritarian adversaries do not
- Transformation is gradual, not instantaneous — procurement and cultural change are slow

## Counter-evidence

- AI reliability in intelligence contexts remains unproven at scale
- Historical examples show intelligence agencies are often slow to adopt new technology
- Adversarial AI introduces new deception vectors that may offset collection advantages

## Open questions

- Can democratic legal frameworks for intelligence adapt fast enough to AI-enabled capabilities?
- How to maintain meaningful human review of AI-generated intelligence assessments?
