## Statement

As generative AI automates increasingly large portions of cognitive work, education systems must shift emphasis from transmitting knowledge and teachable skills toward developing the capacities for meaning-making, moral reasoning, adaptive problem-solving in ill-defined contexts, and aesthetic/relational appreciation — capacities that are either not replicable by AI or that require distinctively human development.

## Evidence summary

OECD (2025) provides the most systematic articulation of this claim, with a 147-page conceptual framework grounded in philosophy, PISA research, and high-performing education system analysis. Bieger & Kolmar (2023) offer a complementary institutional-level argument from higher education.

## Conditions and scope

Normative claim; does not presuppose a specific timetable for AI capability development. Stronger if AI continues to improve at current rates; weaker if AI development plateaus.

## Counter-evidence

Human capital theory may still hold if AI augments rather than replaces human labor; the "uniquely human" domain may shrink faster than education systems can adapt.

## Open questions
