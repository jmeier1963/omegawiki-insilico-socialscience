## Statement

The scale of dominant internet platforms (not content per se) constitutes a structural threat to democracy, as monopoly-level control over public discourse enables disproportionate influence over political outcomes. Structural solutions (middleware content curation layers, interoperability mandates) are preferable to both government censorship and deregulation.

## Evidence summary

Fukuyama (2021) in Journal of Democracy argues from case analysis of 2021 US Capitol events and platform deplatforming decisions. Conceptual argument supported by examples; not empirically tested. Confidence moderate: influential policy argument, but lacks empirical evidence of middleware effectiveness.

## Conditions and scope

- Middleware requires platform cooperation or regulatory mandates
- May not apply in authoritarian contexts where platform power aligns with state interests

## Counter-evidence

- Platforms argue algorithmic curation already reflects user preferences
- Middleware interoperability may be technically infeasible at scale
