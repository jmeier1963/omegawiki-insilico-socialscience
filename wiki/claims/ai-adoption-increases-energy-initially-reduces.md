## Statement

AI adoption initially increases national energy consumption and CO2 emissions, but past a threshold of approximately $220–580 AI market per capita, AI use cases (energy optimization, smart grids, industrial efficiency) produce net environmental benefits — reducing emissions and accelerating renewable energy adoption. This follows a "Green AI Kuznets Curve" pattern.

## Evidence summary

Melguizo, Katz & Jung (2025) provide cross-country panel evidence from 23 middle and high-income countries. Results are consistent with the inverted-U hypothesis. Confidence moderate because: (1) working paper, not yet peer-reviewed; (2) causal identification is incomplete; (3) AI market per capita is a noisy proxy; (4) rebound effects (Jevons Paradox) may counteract the reduction.

## Conditions and scope

- Evidence limited to middle and high-income countries
- Threshold may shift as compute efficiency improves
- Does not address within-country variation or specific sectors

## Counter-evidence

- Jevons Paradox / rebound effects: efficiency gains from AI may increase total energy demand
- Compute scaling laws suggest continued AI energy growth regardless of application-level efficiency gains

## Open questions

- How long does it take countries to move from initial high-emission phase to beneficial phase?
