## Statement

The leading proposals to share AI's gains via public equity stakes or wealth funds (voluntary donations, forced transfers, market-value levies) would yield only a few hundred dollars per American per year at plausible scales — too little to make anyone rich or to insure against widespread job displacement. Their value is mainly political (visibility, a sense of shared stake), not material redistribution.

## Evidence summary

The Economist works through the mechanisms and arithmetic: a 3%-equity fund pays ~$20/person/year; even optimistic appreciation yields a few hundred dollars; a broad 0.2% levy raises ~$40bn/year with similar per-capita results. Each mechanism also has practical flaws (dilution, taxpayer risk, expropriation concerns).

## Conditions and scope

US, 2026 valuations; illustrative scenario arithmetic.

## Counter-evidence

If AI valuations rise far beyond current levels (as superintelligence advocates expect), the same percentage stakes could yield materially larger payouts; advocates argue the political/insurance value alone justifies the funds.

## Open questions
