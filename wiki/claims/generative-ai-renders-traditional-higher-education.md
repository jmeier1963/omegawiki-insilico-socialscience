## Statement

The quality of generative AI text generation is sufficient to pass or exceed passing thresholds in traditional written assignments across higher education, making these assessment formats unreliable as measures of student competence without fundamental redesign.

## Evidence summary

Primarily analytical (Bieger & Kolmar 2023): faculty observations that AI-generated essays pass with grades, corroborated by widespread practitioner reports and the ChatGPT-defeated-doctors-diagnosing case. Awaiting systematic empirical study of AI performance on assessment tasks across disciplines.

## Conditions and scope

Specifically targets written assessment; alternative formats (oral exams, real-time collaborative work, praxis-based portfolios) are not rendered dysfunctional by current AI.

## Counter-evidence

Some disciplines with specialized notation systems (advanced mathematics, formal logic, code review) may maintain partial resistance; AI performance varies by domain.

## Open questions

- What is the minimum assessment redesign needed to restore reliability?
- Are oral examinations scalable enough to replace written assessments at large institutions?
- Does AI assistance in assessments actually reduce learning outcomes, or just change the form of learning?
