## Statement

Narratives that frame AI development as humanity's path to self-transcendence — overcoming biological limits, weakness, and mortality — are incompatible with a governance framework grounded in human dignity and the common good. Such narratives risk legitimizing the subordination of actual human persons to speculative technological ideals.

## Evidence summary

The Magnifica Humanitas encyclical (Leo XIV, 2026) explicitly devotes a section to "underlying narratives: transhumanism and posthumanism," identifying them as "deceitful goals" and contrasting them with Christian humanism and the concept of grace. The document argues that building AI governance on transhumanist premises leads to "dehumanization" and a future that treats the person as a means rather than an end.

## Conditions and scope

- Normative claim from within Catholic Social Teaching; full acceptance requires engaging the theological anthropology.
- Shares philosophical territory with secular critiques of techno-solutionism and AI-as-upgrade framing.
- Not a claim about whether transhumanist outcomes are technically achievable.

## Counter-evidence

- Some secular AI ethicists argue that enhancement of human capabilities is compatible with dignity if governed equitably (e.g., Buchanan et al., transhumanist bioethics).
- The claim's force depends heavily on how "human dignity" is operationalized.

## Open questions

- How should AI governance frameworks engage with enhancement ethics without either endorsing or rejecting transhumanism wholesale?
- Are there forms of human enhancement through AI that are compatible with the dignity-and-common-good framework?
