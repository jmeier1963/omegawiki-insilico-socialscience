## Statement

Traditional technology policy is process-neutral. AI's qualitative distinctiveness — its ability to reason, pass human-equivalence tests, and act as autonomous agents — requires outcomes-focused policy that actively directs AI toward solving specific societal challenges (health, climate, productivity) rather than enabling all AI deployment indiscriminately.

## Evidence summary

IPPR (2025) argues from AI capability analysis and policy comparison. House of Lords (2024) separately recommends similar outcomes-based approach. Confidence low-moderate: argument is normative and politically contested; "challenge-led AI" approach has limited empirical track record.

## Counter-evidence

- Technology-neutral policy may be more robust to uncertainty about which AI applications prove valuable
- Outcomes-targeted policy may privilege certain use cases, crowding out unforeseen beneficial applications

## Open questions
