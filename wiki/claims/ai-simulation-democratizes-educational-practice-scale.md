## Statement

Multi-agent LLM systems can dramatically lower the cost and barrier of creating adaptive educational simulations, making high-quality experiential practice accessible to institutions and learners who currently lack it.

## Evidence summary

Currently supported only by a proof-of-concept prototype (PitchQuest). No randomized controlled trial comparing learning outcomes against traditional simulations or human instructors.

## Conditions and scope

Scope: non-safety-critical educational domains where hallucination can be mitigated by design. The claim does not extend to high-stakes domains (e.g., surgical training) without additional safeguards.

## Counter-evidence

LLM limitations (hallucination, narrative inconsistency, bias) may undermine simulation fidelity and learning outcomes in ways not yet empirically measured.

## Open questions

- What are the quantitative learning outcome gains vs. human-led simulations?
- What minimum fidelity threshold is needed for simulations to produce positive transfer to real-world performance?
