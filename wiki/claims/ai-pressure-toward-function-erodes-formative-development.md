## Statement

When technological disruption (currently AI, previously industrialization) drives education to prioritize functional utility — reskilling, credentialing, task-complementarity — it undermines the formative development (Bildung) through which humans develop genuinely robust intellectual, moral, and epistemic capabilities. The "race to stay useful" framing accepts human-as-function and thereby erodes human-as-formation.

## Evidence summary

One essay (McCord 2026) making a philosophical/historical argument. Credentialism as a prior historical case where function-first education eroded formative aims. No direct empirical measurement of Bildung erosion under AI pressure. Confidence is 0.5 (proposed/speculative).

## Conditions and scope

- Normative claim about educational values, not an empirical measurement
- Most directly applies to university-level education and professional formation
- Assumes Bildung is both valuable and distinguishable from functional skills acquisition

## Counter-evidence

- Karp, Andreessen: universities have already failed at Bildung; skipping them for vocational training is rational given actual institutional performance
- AI tools could in principle *expand* access to formative education by reducing barriers and personalizing engagement
