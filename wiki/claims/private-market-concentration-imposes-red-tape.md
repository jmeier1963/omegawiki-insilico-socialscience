## Statement

The regulatory costs imposed by dominant private technology gatekeepers — through app store commissions, API access restrictions, algorithmic ranking power, and terms-of-service compliance — function as a form of private "red tape" that imposes substantial barriers to entry and exit for dependent businesses, often exceeding government regulatory burdens.

## Evidence summary

Advocacy document from American Economic Liberties Project (2021); case studies rather than quantitative evidence. Confidence low: argument is plausible but evidence is selective and advocacy-motivated; antitrust enforcement post-2021 may have changed landscape.

## Counter-evidence

- Platform compliance costs are voluntary (firms choose to use platforms); government regulation is mandatory
- Network effects benefit all participants, not just gatekeepers

## Open questions

- Has antitrust enforcement (2022–2025) materially reduced these private regulatory burdens?
- Does the "other red tape" concept specifically apply to AI infrastructure gatekeepers (cloud, model APIs)?
