## Statement

Effectively and responsibly deploying powerful AI for scientific breakthroughs requires **radical redesign of research institutions and workflows** (AI-native organizational engineering), analogous to factory reconfiguration after electrification — not merely inserting AI tools into legacy high-modernist lab structures.

## Evidence summary

Inherent Labs (2026) manifesto claims legacy labs hamper breakthroughs and create ethical risks; uses historical factory dynamo analogy to argue marginal efficiency gains from retrofitting fail until processes are redesigned around decentralized power (AI-native modular workflows). No controlled studies compare redesigned vs. legacy institutions.

## Conditions and scope

- Targets institutions pursuing open-ended discovery with advanced AI, not incremental tool adoption.
- Authors are building the institution they advocate (Inherent Labs PBC) — vested interest.
- Redesign claim is qualitative; no metrics for "radical" vs. "sufficient" change.

## Counter-evidence

- Agent Laboratory and similar systems show substantial gains with human-in-the-loop gates inside existing institutions.
- Domain-specific AI tools (AlphaFold, GNoME) achieved breakthroughs without institutional reinvention.
- Organizational change is costly and slow; incremental adoption may be more tractable.
