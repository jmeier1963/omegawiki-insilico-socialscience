## Statement

When tech companies use AI-related cost savings as justification for large workforce reductions while simultaneously mandating AI adoption and surveillance for surviving employees, the resulting dual pressure — displacement anxiety plus compelled AI use — generates widespread organizational demoralization, resistance, and erosion of long-term career commitment.

## Evidence summary

Single qualitative case study (Meta, May 2026, NYT investigative report). Evidence is journalistic, not peer-reviewed; based on 11 anonymous sources and reviewed internal communications. Confidence is low because: (1) no baseline, (2) no control company, (3) potential selection bias in who spoke to reporters, (4) Meta's public statements contradict some employee characterizations.

The structural logic, however, is robust: employees face a legitimacy gap in which their own behavioral data is extracted to train systems that may replace them, while their performance reviews depend on demonstrating AI adoption.

## Conditions and scope

- Applies during periods of simultaneous AI investment + headcount reduction
- Most acute in knowledge-worker contexts (software engineers, product designers, researchers) where AI tools directly threaten core job tasks
- May be attenuated in companies with strong psychological safety culture, transparent AI strategy, or genuine opt-out mechanisms

## Counter-evidence

- Meta's official position: surveillance is for AI training only, not performance management; Zuckerberg denied it was "surveillance or performance tracking"
- Some employees may benefit from AI tools and see the transformation positively (selection: silent majority vs. vocal minority in internal forums)
- Demoralization may be primarily driven by layoff anxiety rather than AI adoption per se — the effects may be separable

## Open questions

- Does demoralization persist post-layoff or normalize once headcount uncertainty resolves?
- Is the "training your replacements" perception accurate — does employee behavioral data substantively improve AI capabilities?
- Does mandatory AI adoption actually improve AI tool quality/usage patterns vs. organic adoption?
