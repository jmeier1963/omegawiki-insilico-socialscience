## Statement

Assigning AI a specific pedagogical role (tutor, coach, mentor, teammate, tool, simulator, or student) in classroom tasks creates a structured human-AI division of cognitive labor that preserves student engagement and reduces the risk of cognitive off-loading relative to unstructured AI use.

## Evidence summary

Current evidence is conceptual (Mollick & Mollick 2023). The framework is widely adopted in practice but lacks randomized controlled trial validation of differential learning outcomes across role types.

## Conditions and scope

Scope: formal educational settings with explicit role assignment and instruction. Does not apply to informal or unmonitored AI use where role boundaries are not maintained.

## Counter-evidence

No direct empirical counter-evidence. The risk of complacency is acknowledged even within role-structured use.

## Open questions

- Which specific role(s) maximize learning gains vs. which merely replicate unstructured use?
- Can role adherence be measured and enforced at scale?
- Does effectiveness generalize beyond WEIRD student populations?
