## Statement

The largest cloud computing firms (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) have evolved from asset-light platform intermediaries into asset-heavy infrastructure providers, giving them structural "choke point" leverage over all digital economic activity. This concentration of compute access has produced techno-nationalist alliances between these firms and state actors, reshaping global AI governance.

## Evidence summary

Tan & Thelen (2025) document the shift from platform to cloud capitalism through financial analysis and corporate strategy review. The AI race has accelerated asset intensification and cemented choke-point power. Confidence moderate: analysis is qualitative and largely US-centric; counter-dynamics (EU regulation, DeepSeek-style efficiency gains) are ongoing.

## Conditions and scope

- Most applicable to US hyperscalers and the current AI race period (2023–2025)
- EU and Chinese cloud markets have different structural features
- Efficiency improvements (Jevons Paradox) may reinforce rather than break concentration

## Counter-evidence

- Open-source AI models (Llama, Mistral) reduce infrastructure lock-in
- EU regulatory pressure (AI Act, DMA) may constrain choke-point power
- Historical tech monopolies have been disrupted (IBM, Nokia)

## Open questions

- Does compute efficiency (DeepSeek) ultimately break or reinforce cloud concentration?
- Can regulatory intervention (EU, US antitrust) durably limit choke-point power?
