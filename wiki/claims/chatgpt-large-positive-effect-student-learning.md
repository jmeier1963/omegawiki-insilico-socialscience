## Statement

Meta-analytic evidence from 51 studies (2022–2025) indicates that ChatGPT integration in educational contexts has a large positive effect on student learning performance (Hedges' g≈0.87), with moderate positive effects on learning perception and higher-order thinking development. These effects are moderated by course type, learning model, and intervention duration, with problem-based learning and 4–8 week durations producing the strongest results.

## Evidence summary

Wang & Fan (2025) synthesize 51 controlled studies across a range of grade levels and course types. The aggregate effect on learning performance (g=0.867) is large by educational psychology standards — larger than most educational technology interventions. However, individual study effect sizes show extreme variability (g≈0.04 to g>2.8), suggesting high context-dependence. Effects are meaningfully moderated by learning model (problem-based learning performs best), course type, and duration.

## Conditions and scope

- Primarily college-level students
- Controlled comparisons (ChatGPT-assisted vs. control condition)
- Most evidence from STEM and language learning courses
- Stronger effects with problem-based learning and 4–8 week durations
- Likely publication bias toward positive findings

## Counter-evidence

- High inter-study heterogeneity casts doubt on the aggregate g estimate
- Most studies are short-term; long-term learning and retention not assessed
- Academic integrity and dependency effects not measured
- Publication bias plausibly inflates the effect size
- Conflicting individual studies find no significant effect (included in meta-analysis)

## Open questions

- Do ChatGPT learning effects persist over time? What is the retention effect?
- How do effects change as LLM capability improves across model versions?
- What specific pedagogical frameworks maximize benefits vs. risks (academic integrity, dependency)?
- Do effects hold in K-12 contexts and non-STEM subjects?
