## Statement

Fixed compute thresholds used to define "frontier" AI models in regulations (e.g., EU AI Act's 10^25 FLOP threshold) become obsolete rapidly as AI scaling continues. Effective AI governance requires dynamic threshold adjustment mechanisms — sunset clauses, rolling percentile cutoffs, or regulatory trigger reviews — rather than static definitions.

## Evidence summary

Scharre (CNAS, 2024) projects compute growth using scaling laws; House of Lords inquiry (2024) similarly recommends dynamic thresholds. Confidence moderate: argument is technically well-grounded but relies on continued scaling; efficiency breakthroughs (DeepSeek-style) could disrupt the compute-capability relationship.

## Counter-evidence

- Compute thresholds are politically tractable; dynamic adjustment may be harder to implement politically
- Capability evaluation (rather than compute) may be a better governance trigger
